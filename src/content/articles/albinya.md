Albinya, officially the Republic of Albinya (Albinyaren Errepublika), is a nation located in the west of Pelagonia, with coasts that face the Auritania Sea. It has a population of approximately 19 million people. The capital of the country is Daxá, a capital built from scratch. Although it is not the most populated city in the country, it is the most historically important, having been the base of the republican revolutionaries. The country is a decentralized unitary state with administrative subsidiarity, divided into 9 regions.

## History

### **Pre-colonial era**

According to the evidence, among tools, accounts kept by the few who survived, and other sources, the proto-Hadzajos arrived approximately in 4500 BCE, with the Syndaraq dynasty, in 2200 BCE, being the one that would eventually consolidate the identity of the Hadzajo people, unifying peoples and opening schools for nobles and commoners, where Sasjo emerged as a common language. The Syndaraq dynasty, according to writings such as the Red Stone, had a theocratic and dynastic system, with a legal system strongly based on the religious ideas of Szaidism, an animist religion. The dynasty eventually collapsed in 1450 BCE because it exercised repression over other peoples who refused to absorb the culture, religion, and language of the Hadzajos, added to the discontent of the Hadzajos themselves with the monarchy, due to the conditions of exploitation and the privileges held by the priests. The end of the dynasty led to a series of wars between 1445 BCE and 1180 BCE, characterized by constant skirmishes, attacks with armies, periods of non-aggression, and opportunistic alliances that constantly changed between different peoples such as the Hadzajos, the Yadairiya, the Xaulta, and the Ayta, until finally, according to sources such as the Stone of Friendship, dating from approximately 1180 BCE, an agreement was signed between various leaders of the peoples for the birth of the Hashka Arakzov as a way to end the war, since all were ravaged by droughts, scarcity, and problems that they could not solve through war while divided. Although, according to the Hadzajos' own oral tradition, tensions did not disappear quickly, but were suppressed through the Superior Council, and generation after generation they eased, approximately in 1050 BCE. Already, according to their own administrative records in stone, there is a functional administration in 800 BCE. Most of the records are in Sasjo, since they developed a simple writing system based on lines that were easy to make, with each line creating a character, and the characters have a meaning with which sentences were created. Sasjo, due to its advantage and its writing system, ended up being the main language, and several peoples were absorbed over time under the Hadzajo identity. The Hashka Arakzov came to control a very significant extension of territory, but it became unsustainable due to droughts, long distances, new tensions, and other types of external causes that forced people to move further, until what would finally be their definitive territory. By 300 BCE, they were concentrated exclusively in the coastal areas where they had built their entire civilization.

### **The colonization **

The Askurians arrived in 1524 CE and, according to the relation letter written by the explorer Aleon Garzán, "It was not a virgin land," but rather there existed within it an indigenous confederation that called itself Hashka Arakzov (Great Alliance in Sasjo). In the writing *The Savages, Life, Organization and Customs*, he expressed how the natives identified themselves as Hadzajos (meaning "familiar people") and had widely populated cities, with infrastructure developed for the era and a fairly developed political organization. Power resided principally in the communes (called *Auwtand*) and they only tended to unite politically to resolve problems, with the *Hanandul Qaust* (Regional Council) and the *Hanandul Kaivenat* (Superior Council) being the form in which they usually resolved problems at a scale commensurate with their gravity. Each commune had its own laws, judges, etc., but there existed the *Koinorsand*, a pact that prohibited certain things to the communes that made up the alliance and that could carry sanctions with real consequences for the infracting commune.  For example, in large part things such as infrastructure, like roads, were in the hands of the Superior Council, which collected a tribute from all according to their capacity to pay in order to finance it. Despite being fairly organized, foreign diseases spread like gunpowder and, despite the attempts of the Superior Council, it was impossible to prevent the diseases from decimating the population, since the *hadzajos*, according to the Askurians themselves, had received them with good will and had taken them as far as where the Superior Council was located. The colonizers, according to letters to the crowns, described, according to their own words, "that the catastrophe was a 'divine' sign that 'God was cleaning the lands for them'", for which reason they soon colonized the *hadzajo* lands. Between 70 and 80 percent of the millions of people who lived in the confederation died from a mixture of diseases, a demographic collapse that sank the socioeconomic system, the forced labor imposed by the colonizers, etc., and the remainder who survived was forced to convert to the religion of the colonizers, adopt their language, their culture, forced mixing, etc., which led to the birth of *Farwat* ("reaction" in *sazjo*, but in the sense of a response to an aggression), which was an armed *hadzajo* resistance that actively burned churches, used guerrilla tactics against Askurian soldiers, attacked logistical routes and, among other things, sought to sabotage the colonial government. It was thanks to *Farwat* that the extermination of the *hadzajo* people was not almost total because of the diseases, since they absorbed the remaining communes that had not been so harshly struck and without much colonial presence, centralized and militarized society and took them to zones of difficult access, where they lived, cultivated short-cycle crops, stored resources stolen from the colonizers, etc. They developed a clear system of succession, since they knew that leaders were going to die, for which reason they structured themselves in such a way that they were a "Hydra", according to how the *Mabalen Maulli*, the constitution of *Farwat* itself, described it. In 1721, *Farwat* and the *mestizos* organized to carry out a revolution, and then divide the country in two and have both have autonomy, but ally against the colonizers. According to the governor of the colonial territory himself, "The failure was violent and resounding". Despite the planning, they were attacked from multiple fronts and even, according to letters from the revolutionaries, there were "traitorous *mestizos* or natives" who joined the colonial forces for promises of lands and privileges. The repression became more extreme, to the point that any meeting was a motive to be treated as "antisocial" according to the Decree of Pacification and Civil Amendment issued by the governorship. This is where *saherdi* began to emerge, although it was seen as a "vulgar" language by the royalty, because it was an amalgam of *sazjo* and two other foreign languages, but the crown allowed it to expand as a form of diluting pure *sazjo*, since the attempts to annihilate *Farwat* were increasingly broad, because *Farwat* itself had become radicalized.

### **The "false revolution"**

Dídac de Bellveure, of the house of Bellveure, son of an influential aristocrat among the colonizers, was described by his own brother, Simone de Bellveure, as "promiscuous, proud, hedonistic and delicate, who had never grabbed a shovel in his life". He discovered that he was not going to be elected governor of Albinya, despite having sought the favor of the royalty to exhaustion.  According to his own writing, "Because I am so magnificent", this seemed offensive to him, to the point that he insulted the crown of the kingdom, which led to him being sent to prison. By dishonor, his entire family lost their privileges, his father and his mother committed suicide and his political career was ruined. After leaving prison, humiliated and without goods, he moved to Albinya, where, according to various historians, such as Dr. Joan Baptista Clavell i Soler, not so much for his intellect but for the exact moment, he organized the poor overseas colonists, the *hadzajos*, the *mestizos* and other groups to achieve the independence of the country in 1810.  The conditions for the revolution already existed. Only someone willing to proclaim it publicly was lacking, and the people, according to his own book ("Because I am so magnificent"), decided to place the head of the governor on a pike. In the book of Dr. Joan Baptista Clavell i Soler, "The bloody twilight", the revolution "was unstoppable, bloody and cathartic, but it would not be the end of oppression", and he described how, after the revolution, Dídac declared himself king, allied with the church, bought some revolutionary generals with privileges, killed those who did not please him and governed as an absolutist king. Dídac made sure to divide the territory in such a way that the people he had to seduce were few and easy to control or extort. He executed anyone who contradicted him or did not agree with him, which led to an agrarian policy that provoked a generalized famine while he worried about casinos and "companion ladies".  Since, according to his own writing, Because I am so magnificent, he wanted to convert the country, in his own words, "into a paradise" where it would be full of "casinos, taverns, etc.", so that rich people and people who according to him were of "good taste" would come. The church itself with which he allied had a relationship of permanent conflict with him, described thus by letters from Fray Prudencio de la Santa Cruz himself, until Dídac himself betrayed it, prohibited it and decided to found his "own Nazarene church", which according to how Dídac described it in his own book, "was extremely loyal and without questioning" toward him.

### **The republican revolution and the republic**

His regime did not last more than a decade. Salvador Lefebvre, Domingo Mujika and Hurakan Siruma, who had also been revolutionaries of independence, were the only ones who were not executed or bought, so they wove a new revolution to establish a republic, according to the book of which the three were promoters, *El Pueblerismo*. In 1823 the republican revolution occurred, they overthrew him from power and established a republic, which sought finally, under the very doctrine of the *pueblerista* book, to "civilize" the country. The revolution derived in the War of the Brothels, called thus because it was in those establishments where the republican aggressions began when they tried to kill Eduardo Saramago, one of the most important generals of Dídac. It lasted between 1823 and 1827, with various campaigns and moments in which the aggression stopped due to the exhaustion of both sides.  The republicans, in the book of General Eduardo, *Rebeldes y Resentidos*, are described as "implacable firing squads executing religious figures, burning churches, hanging anyone who sounded like an aristocrat", while the reactionaries, in letters, wrote about the self-called "reactionaries", who supported the crown out of "mere interest and benefit" and who "killed anyone who did not bow their head before Dídac in the exact degrees that the law said". Finally, the reactionaries were defeated and Dídac was assassinated by a mob. The republic, after the end of the conflict, finished its constitution in 1830 and celebrated the first elections in 1834. Democracy was a period of social instability, a product of the very different identities that composed the country. Overseas colonists who did not even speak the same language among themselves nor have the same culture, the pure *hadzajos* who survived, the *mestizos*, etc. There were at least four or six different languages spoken by the people of the country and every attempt to give a common framework was seen as aggression, racism or "favoritism".

### **A new dawn**

In 1851, Axton Kayser, son of the overseas upper class, came to power through the ballot box, after a period of instability in the democratic era. Only one government finished its mandate. The others lasted between one and three years at most. Philosopher, politician and former member of the Black Berets, Kayser arrived with the promise of "a new dawn". In his youth he was a member of the Black Berets, a Dûnmarelist paramilitary organization described in his book *Doctrine of the Absolute* as "ultra-nationalist, chauvinist, anti-liberal and radical, a kind of atheistic mysticism". He separated from them because he considered it to be a movement, according to his own letters, of "majestic aesthetics and pauperous spirit", since, being a member of the party technicians, he had ideological differences with other members that led him to abandon the movement. In a letter he described them as "uncivilized, brutish and intellectually disabled".  Kayser, already separated from the Black Berets, decided to pursue a solitary political career and later created the New Republican Right Party, with which he won the elections. Cordialism, the work of Kayser, is the condensation of a more "measured" version of the nationalism, organicism, order, etc., of Dûnmar elism. Dûnmar elism, according to books such as *Soul of the Fatherland*, by Kayser, is an idea that seeks to "replace religion" with a "national religion" that fulfills the same function of religion, but without the "remnants of the past", arguing that man needs a myth, something above himself that moves him, a single and magnificent narrative that creates a society that functions as a single organism, with a single destiny, hence the phrase "a glorious destiny" (Un etorkizun loriatu). Kayser ended Farwat, which until 1851 remained active fighting for a "Hadzajo nation" and which fought against "the cultural domination" of the government. He killed, imprisoned or co-opted its members after his "war against the rebels" through the ISN (National Security Forces, Indar Segurtasun Nazionala), parallel to the army, which he used to orchestrate a coup d'etat, using them as a "loyal skeleton" to which he transferred soldiers and budget to leave the armed forces with nothing. Everything that seemed a simple war against separatists was the end of an elite that had captured the state, according to Official policy and political promises of the EBE. Kayser, taking advantage of his power, carried out a "total reform of the Albinés state", the institutional reform, in which politicians, institutional experts, etc., participated, to create the new constitution, design institutions, etc., so that they would not become corrupted again and would be for public benefit. It was public knowledge that the new "dictator", as Kayser called himself, had no intentions of staying long. He governed until 1867, as "protector of the new dawn", since according to him, this project needed a guide. After leaving government voluntarily, the elections were held that same year, since the preparations had begun in 1862, when Kayser had announced his retirement for that date. Despite having left power, the state remained strongly built and constitutionally restricted by the ideas of "cordialist democracy", which allowed an economic and social stability that persists to the present, but not without a cost, according to the opposition, such as the Hadzajo Struggle for Justice, which described the regime as "genocidal" during the government of Kayser, which sought to eliminate all trace of "alterity that would lead to internal convulsion". He imposed Saherdi, Dûnmare, renamed cities, created new festivities, customs, gastronomy, etc., all so that there would be a single identity, regardless of whether it was indigenous, white, black or mestizo. The official narrative, the one in government documents and history books in schools, treats him as a prophet, but, according to his own letters, he admitted in them to enjoying the idea of "having the power to kill someone with a simple signature". Kayser faced three simultaneous insurgencies, among them were Farwat, the ABN and the Black Berets. Farwat fought for a Hadzajo state, the ABN (Azken Bastioi Nazaretarra, last Nazarene bastion) fought for the end of state laicism and the reunification of church and state, the return to Nazarene values and way of life, and the Black Berets fought with the defenders of the social doctrine in the streets and against the ABN. According to government censuses such as that of 1838, no one felt completely Albinés, neither the overseas people, nor the mestizos, who suffered a greater identity confusion due to racism for not being considered neither white nor "pure" by the Hadzajos themselves. The country itself had segregated zones where they mutually prohibited each other. Kayser, according to his own letters, "saw the disintegration of his country around the corner" and that his solution was "the creation of an absolute cult to an idea". He recruited people from the street, vagabonds, the poor, orphans, the depressed, the tired, and dressed them in red shirts, black dress pants and a black beret, and made them march throughout the territory, singing hymns in Saherdi. He used it to put an end to any form of resistance to his policies of "cultural reform" (that is, assimilation) and ended, one by one, with the organizations that threatened the stability of the country. Kayser, in his own letters, admitted that he had an affinity for being a "dictator", but that he feared that this would lead him to a psychological decadence that would derive in committing another type of "immoral" acts that he could not bear, such as corruption or the diversion of funds. The dictatorship of Kayser was described by the Hadzajo Struggle for Justice and the FDP (Front Democràtic Popular) as "dark, violent and bloody with those who resisted", and when his retirement date was finally announced in 1867, the repression began to lighten and to allow the opposition to organize for the "democratic era". The subsequent "lasting peace" was the product of the dismantling, the assassination, the repression and the systematic erasure by Kayser, who achieved his objective. Kayser himself, in his own letters, said that "it was at a very high cost" and that he did not understand how people "could resist peace so much". No one was left to resist, because the children of the next generation, according to the Hadzajo Struggle for Justice, spoke Saherdi and celebrated Albinés things. A generation that, according to testimonies collected from young Hadzajos speaking of the traditions of their parents, was incapable of understanding the roots of their mothers and the idea that they were simply people "attached to the past".

### **The post-dictatorship era, the "golden era"**

The elections of 1867 ended with a hegemony of the EBE (Eskuin Berri Errepublikarra), lasting 22 years. They had a broad majority in parliament, since they needed to consolidate institutions, end the final tensions of the post-dictatorship period and consolidate Albinese culture. This hegemony lasted until 1889. Between 1889 and 1911, parliament was occupied by the EBE, ASK (Alderi Sozial Kontserbadorea) and the NSN (Nekazarien Sindikatu Nazionala). Here the EBE lost hegemony and was forced to form coalitions with other parties. This is where the culture of parliamentary "consensus" emerged. State repression is still "strong" according to non-governmental organizations such as RSP (Reporters sense por), but not as brutal, and there is a stronger political concentration on education, the continuation of cultural reform and other civil aspects, but the land question begins to be increasingly present in political issues. Between 1911 and 1926, a left-wing coalition governed, composed of the AAK (Albaniako Alderdi Komunista), the NSN (Nekazarien Sindikatu Nazionala) and the ASD (Alderdi Sozialista Demokratikoa), which tried to push socialist and reformist policies that were blocked by the right made up of the EBE, the ALD (Alderdi Liberal Demokratikoa) and the HBA (Hirugarren Bidea Albinya-rentzat), but there were certain reforms that succeeded, such as land reform, which thanks to consensus ended up being a more "realistic" version according to the right itself, which sought to make it more moderate and technical, contrary to the radical reforms that the left desired. Land redistribution occurred, but it was done slowly, with "due process" and technical assistance from the state, as well as the distribution of seeds, tools and what was necessary so that landless farmers could make productive use of those lands. New labor regulations were also born, such as the 8-hour workday as a limit, retirement and the birth of the ANLG. However, they did not achieve things such as public health, but the birth of the KFS was promoted, although the left itself was not entirely satisfied because of the sanitary cordon that the right, which despite their differences united against them and demanded compliance with the consensus from which the current social and economic policies were born. From 1926 onward, the current parties govern. The New Republican Right (Eskuin Berri Errepublikarra) with 34%, the laborists who split from the socialists and defend regulated capitalism, the National Workers' Party (Langile Nazionalaren Alderdia) with 26%, the Democratic Liberal Party (Alderdi Liberal Demokratikoa) with 16%, who are treated as a bourgeois party and who do not follow cordialism, but the EBE uses the LNA as a counterweight, the Third Way Party for Albinya (Hirugarren Bidea Albinya-rentzat) with 13%, who are more traditionalist and conservative nationalists separate from the EBE, and the Democratic Socialist Party (Alderdi Sozialista Demokratikoa) with 11%, who are the only survivors of the previous left-wing coalition. From the 1867 decade onward, they are referred to as the "golden age" by grandparents, parents and politicians themselves, such as Silvestre Dalmau i Etxeberria in his book Heritage of a Great Statesman. There are no longer civil wars, the parties in parliament had stable electoral cycles and agreed-upon changes, economic and social reforms were in part a product of consensus and the concord pact between political parties. Currently, according to official reports and reports from very present non-governmental organizations such as RSP (Reporters sense por), the country is described as "a stable country, not perfect, but at least problems are solved by voting or talking and you don't need to resort to blows with someone to be right." Dialogue and cordiality are principles at the heart of the Albinese, or otherwise, a visit from the police for uncordial behavior, penalized according to law number 670 against antisocial behavior.

## Military

The Scarlet Legion (Legio Eskarlata) is the military force of Albinya. It is an army oriented solely toward the self-defense of the country, it is small, professional and with broad reserves. Military service is obligatory. It is organized into sections and mutually monitored so that there will not be another coup d'état. It is strongly focused on the technical, such as having engineers, artillery, anti-aircraft defense, guerrilla warfare, etc., seeking to deter invasion and to make that same invasion very costly if it is carried out. It is entirely subordinate to the parliament and the constitution, with controls and limits to prevent democracy from being threatened again.

## Government

The entire system of government is based on Cordialism, and this in turn is based on Dûnmar elism. Albinés democracy combines parliamentarism with a strong attachment to stability, the search for consensus and cordiality as a mandatory method of government against what its founders called the "savagery of the past". The Albinés state is characterized by having been designed almost entirely by a group of individuals that the DME (Democracyaren Mundu Erakundea) describes as "paranoid". Institutions designed to watch each other, technical limits against populism and short-termism, and a first-hand knowledge that power tends to corrupt. The citizen must involve themselves in politics, according to the civil code itself, "whether they like it or not, and it will not be voluntarily, since no one else will fight for their interests but themselves and at the same time serves as an unpaid auditor", that is, the idea of the citizen as the ultimate watchdog, according to Kayser's book, *Foundations of Cordialist Democracy*. It is a decentralized unitary state with administrative subsidiarity, that is, there exists a single sovereign national government but it seeks that problems can be resolved at the lowest possible level, rising to scale only if it cannot be resolved at the previous level. There exist mechanisms of mutual coordination (councils), mutual auditing and imposition of constitutional loyalty. The councils are responsible for resolving problems such as floods or, for example, a housing problem, and are watched by the civilians themselves. The EBE and the LNA have a close relationship in parliament. The LNA has former communist and socialist members who, according to the party's own leader, Fernando Lassal, "realized that capitalism is not so bad" and have been very "akin to cordialism." The EBE claims to defend cordialism and "does not mind allying with socialists or liberals, depending on what the objective is in order to defend the cordialist doctrine."

## Economy

The economic system of Albinya is a regulated capitalism, although the country operates under a market economy, it has specific institutional regulations. The model rejects both laissez-faire or pure libertarianism and integral statism, positioning itself at an intermediate point of moderate intervention. Market regulations focus fundamentally on preventing monopolies and guaranteeing that no one falls into circular poverty. "It is easy and fast to open businesses, in the same manner that it is easy to maintain the bureaucracy of a company", according to the EKEF (Enpresa eta Kooperatiba Elkartasun Funtsa, a group of companies that have non-state insurance against crises and watch over business interests) and that "hiring and firing are flexible". According to tax law, taxes are low for SMEs and there are fiscal benefits for large companies that invest in infrastructure and real production verified by at least three independent organisms, private and state. The economy is monitored by the BNDE (Bulego Nazionala Delitu Ekonomikoena, National Office of Economic Crimes), which pursues economic corruption. The services of education, health and transport are totally privatized, and there exist high fiscal incentives to have businesses in rural zones. The majority of people have private insurance, but poor people or those with certain difficulties access the KFS (Kontu Funts Solidarioa, Solidarity Access Account), which delivers real money but blocked for social categories (health, education, transport) in special accounts that allow vulnerable people to freely access these services and not remain helpless, while there also exists the ANLG (Agentzia Nazionala Langile Garapenekoa, National Agency for Worker Development), which implements workfare policies and labor training to insert the unemployed, homeless and poor into the market. The ANLG offers housing and food while making beneficiaries study through the express method (theory is taught while working) and has the legal obligation to change its method if the reinsertion rate is low, and they cannot be just any jobs, but jobs that allow a salary for a dignified life. Otherwise, they are taught economics and basic notions to have a business, so that they form a limited liability company, a public limited company or a workers' cooperative, but generally they come with market studies and plans that they are taught to apply. The state, according to the labor code itself, "does not protect anyone from being incompetent or lazy, but does guarantee that no one stays down forever". Attending the ANLG is not obligatory. It is a voluntary tool. If someone loses their job, they generally try to find another, but if they cannot and have exhausted their options, the ANLG is a solid option according to the LEN itself (Langileen Elkarte Nazionala, union of independent workers). However, the state applies policies labeled as "eugenic" by the opposition such as the ASD (Alderdi Sozialista Demokratikoa), since according to the law, one can only have children through obtaining the family certificate, courses to be parents, etc. The poor are the most affected, according to the ASD (Alderdi Sozialista Demokratikoa), since many do not meet the requirements, but the EBE (Eskuin Berri Errepublikarra) affirms that the government has active policies focused on ending poverty and the people who leave the programs generally have physically and psychologically healthy children. There exists a national fund for economic stability with obligatory contributions from the budget. The principal sectors of the economy are light manufacturing industry, financial and banking services, and agricultural and mineral raw materials.

## Geography

### **Topography**

The country's topography is characterized by varied relief, with low plains in the east, plateaus and mountain ranges in the center-west, and high mountains in the northwest. There are large rivers and wetlands that cross the eastern and central parts of the country. The alluvial plains are extensive and prone to flooding. There are plateaus and hills of moderate height in the center. There are mountain ranges and mountain belts in the west and northeast. Altitudes exceed 3000 to 4000 meters in the far northwest. There are deep valleys and ravines between the mountains. There are depressions and salt flats in arid sectors of the west, and the transition is gradual with respect to the lower and higher lands.

### **Climate**

The Albinese territory is characterized by its location in a tropical-subtropical strip, with long, hot summers and a gradual transition toward the south. There is a notable difference between the east and the west, where the eastern part is more humid while the western part is drier, with semiarid and arid areas. In the lower parts of the territory, the temperature usually ranges between 20 and 26 degrees Celsius. In summer, temperatures usually reach up to 35 and 40 degrees Celsius. In the east, the nights are warmer and more humid, while in the west they are drier. Winter is milder in the north while in the south it is cooler, and there can be occasional frosts in the more southern areas and higher elevations. Rainfall decreases from east to west. It can exceed 1200 to 1800 mm annually in the far east, dropping to 800 to 1200 mm in the center and 500 to 800 mm or less in the west. Summer is usually rainy, and winters are dry, especially in the center-west. Humidity is high in the east and low in the west. In summer, both increase the thermal sensation. In the northeast, due to altitude, it is cooler, drier and with cold nights. Winds are warm in the north and cold in the south. Storms are common in summer, with hail and strong gusts. Droughts in the west are common, and there are floods in the low areas of the east. Jungle or humid subtropical forest vegetation is found in the east, savanna and dry forest in the center, shrublands and steppes in the west and high-altitude grasslands in the northwest. There are microclimates due to valleys, mountain ranges and rivers. There are no abrupt climatic limits. The change is gradual from north to south, from east to west.

## Demographics

### **Ethnic Groups**

The majority of the population is mestizo, that is, 48% of the people are a mix of Ultramarines (white foreigners, regardless of their nationality) and Hadzajos. The mestizo presents a phenotype that balances Ultramarine and Hadzajo traits, characterized by trigueña or light brown skin, dark and straight or slightly wavy hair, and brown or black eyes. Their physical build tends to be medium in height and robust, with facial features that combine slightly prominent cheekbones with a straight nasal bridge. The second largest group is the Ultramarines, who make up 27% of the country's ethnic composition. The average Ultramarine phenotype is characterized by light skin, brown or dark hair, and a medium-tall to tall stature, with a frequently robust complexion. Their facial features tend to include brown or light eyes (green and gray) and a nose with a straight or prominent bridge. The third largest group is the Hadzajos, who are 17% of the population and are characterized by copper-colored or cinnamon brown skin, black, straight, and thick hair, and short to medium stature with a robust physical build, broad chest, and short limbs. Their facial features are markedly Hadzajo, highlighting dark and almond-shaped eyes, high and prominent cheekbones, a firm jaw, and a nose that tends to be short and wide. Black people are the fourth group, representing 8% of the population, and are characterized by skin with a high concentration of melanin (dark tones) and curly or Afro-textured hair, evolutionary traits adapted for sun protection and heat dissipation. Facially, their wide-based noses and thick lips stand out, accompanied by a robust bone structure with proportionally long limbs.

### **Languages**

Aurorès is a language with a soft phonology and open vowels that preserves consonant clusters and palatals (aigua, cotxe, platja) and provides Saherdi with its modern and administrative vocabulary. Ezti is an agglutinative and ergative language with a strong presence of sibilants and affricates (etxe, zubi, itsaso) and a rising intonation at the end of sentences, and it provides Saherdi with phonetics, auxiliaries, and maritime and kinship vocabulary. Sazjo is an indigenous language with SOV order and agglutinative morphology whose words chain together in the sentence and mark relations with suffixes (gaddara, yaidao, teidche, szichan), and it is the grammatical and ritual base of Saherdi. Saherdi is the mixed and national language of Albinya, with a Sazja grammatical base (SOV, cases, auxiliaries), phonetics softened by Ezti, and modern vocabulary taken from Aurorès (szendau, etorkizun, errepublika, langile, alderdi, bulego), and it functions as the official, school, and administrative language.

### **Religion**

Albinian society is marked by Dûnmarism (58%), a secular current with a strong collective sense, a culture of honor and civic duties that serves as the nation's moral axis; it is followed by Nominal Nazarenes (28%), of Nazarene identity but lax practice, and Practicing Nazarenes (8%), who maintain an active and communal faith; the remaining 6% is made up of religious minorities and non-believers, tolerated within the framework of cordiality as long as they do not disturb public order.

### **Citizenship**

Albinian citizenship is primarily cultural, not ethnic or blood-based. One is Albinian through adherence to the Saherdi language, national festivities, loyalty to the State, and integration into the dominant idea of cordialism. As Kayser declared, "A Hadzajo who speaks Saherdi, celebrates our festivities, is loyal to the State and adapted to the dominant idea of the State is more Albinian than an overseas settler who speaks Anglish, is a Nazarene and has divided loyalties," unlike Didac and his racial laws during his regime, which, according to the ASD (Alderdi Sozialista Demokratikoa), are part of the museum of shame. According to *Herencia de un gran estadista*, by Silvestre Dalmau i Etxeberria, Kayser did not discriminate on biological traits but on ideas and loyalties, and this was evident in his anti-racial laws imposed during his regime, which even forced whites to marry mestizos. Anyone could be Albinian if they assimilated culturally, but, according to the EBE (Eskuin Berri Errepublikarra), distrust fell upon those who maintained divided identities or customs outside the national framework.

## Culture and miscellaneous

### **Cordialism**

In large measure, Cordialism is founded on Kayser's book *Death*. In it is affirmed that "With the end of religion as absolute meaning, a giant hole was created that man, by himself, will never fill. Man can have all the courage he wants, but he will never fill that hole. If he believes himself superior, it will only be a deception to try to bear the idea of the smallness of his existence. If all the problems of the world disappeared overnight, the only thing left for man to do with his life would be to consume until that ceases to have meaning, and the desolation of man, that enormous foolishness and the conversion into a pathological liar in order to live, will lead society to its death. If there is no good or evil, if there is no consolation for suffering, if nothing has meaning, why should man live. And there is no answer, so either one lies or one commits suicide. He affirms that freedom, then, is synonymous with perdition, for which reason he is profoundly liberal, because he can support an economic policy as a useful tool but nothing prevents him from denying the philosophy behind it. Why man needs to be free. What does he gain by being so." In *Death* it is also described that Kayser, "observed, with his own eyes and perplexity, the corpse of people who hanged themselves, in his visits to the apartments he rented, but not for the absence of money, but because nothing had meaning", According to Kayser himself, "that was madness and not even he had a logical way to justify it. It was the collapse of a civilization", The republicans, according to strong critics of secularism such as Fray Mateo de la Epifanía, "hunted religion as an absolute evil, but what did they leave as an alternative. Nothing". People fell into depression, alcoholism, opium, etc. "The country was very secularized, libertine and full of idlers", according to Kayser in his writings *Death*, "The relativism brought by the absence of a common framework had been co-author of the chaos in the country, according to the same book." In his book *Doctrine of Cordialism* he writes "So his solution is the myth, Cordialism, the conviction that the nation is not a sum of individuals but a single body in which each part fulfills a determined function and the whole prevails over the particularity of its members. From that myth will also be born new duties, imposed by being imposed in order to be true through imposition itself, as an absolute and irrational affirmation of life. From this principle derive the seven citizen principles, namely, discipline, which submits the will to a superior purpose; duty, which orders one to do what is right because it must be done and not because it is convenient; honor, which demands remaining faithful to one's own principles even when no one observes; responsibility, which obliges one to accept that every action has a price and to face it; perseverance, which impels one to walk toward the goal even when hope falters; harmony, which seeks the equilibrium between the individual and the whole; and self-perfection, which converts every error into an opportunity to transcend it. These do not compose optional values but the absolute meaning of being before the absolute nothing, the pillars of the existence of every Albinés." He also added Dûnmarist mysticism, that "national spirit" that Kayser considered "indispensable for the transcendence of a people, that absolute will and that myth capable of mobilizing masses and of giving the community a meaning that no merely rational doctrine could provide." Aesthetics, for its part, was co-opted from the Black Berets by Kayser until it became what he himself described in his letters to the elite of 1854 as "the expression of a severe and absolute solemnity. The marches in unison, the fist to the heart as a salute, the oath shout, the hymns and the public acts, all arranged to remind the individual that their existence unfolds within something larger than themselves." In daily culture, the Sunnden constitutes the entire week of rest, a time in which collective destressing is sought and in which each person can do what they want as long as it is legal. It is the most important and well-known celebration apart from independence, but it is not the only one. There are more. For example, on Independence Day everyone goes out to march with the red shirt and the black beret, with the flags that bear the symbol of the "absolute". Roast and mass meals, stuffed masses and savory bakery dishes form part of the national gastronomy. The obligatory days of civic service, with attendance at assemblies, are festivities that reinforce citizen duty and keep the national spirit alive. Kayser, in his book *The Cordialist Doctrine*, affirmed that "he already anticipated every possible refutation and decided not to respond to any, because he appeals neither to reason, nor to evidence, nor to any transcendent principle, but exclusively to irrationality, passion and the desire to exterminate his enemies. He sustains that all individual morality collapses upon itself because the individual is extremely finite and, once they lose a superior meaning, they are reduced to pleasures, sensations and personal motives that are never sufficient and are too small to sustain an existence, so that relativism, being incapable of condemning anything, permits all that is bad and leaves society without institutional defense against abuse, degeneration or dissolution, while the cultural left, converted into an archipelago of identities without a common project, becomes incapable of defending any limit because its own logic prevents it, and liberalism, after the death of God and the consequent death of man, produces not free men but consumers, depressives and suicides who find within themselves no reason to keep living. His project does not pretend to be true but only stronger and capable of imposing itself over alterity through an absolute myth internalized and sustained by the State, and it does not ask to be recognized as correct, only that it be feared, obeyed and permitted to exterminate those who oppose it."

### **The Black Berets and the Red Shirts**

Kayser and the Black Berets share certain characteristics that might seem similar, but that have very strong differences. Kayser's homosexuality was a problem, since some members treated him as "a deviant" and often used that label to try to discredit his arguments. In addition, the Black Berets were specifically an overseas project, that is, biological racism that automatically saw anyone who was not of the ethnicity as inferior, something that Kayser saw as a barbaric pseudoscience, without foundation and lacking solidity, since he saw the problem as something fundamentally cultural, according to his own doctrine. Kayser grew up being a bourgeois of the upper class, he was someone quite cultured and certainly distrustful of the idea of the totalitarian and eternal state that the Black Berets defended. In his book *The Fundamental Problem of Man*, he sustains that "any philosophy or idea can say what it wants about man, but that power always corrupts. For that same reason he justifies that democracy is not a useful system for its supposed humanist values, but because it is the only one that permits feedback and that does not trust the state as incorruptible but as a ship that carries the nation to a glorious destiny, and ships need maintenance." He called this revision of the myth, according to his book, "the purification of the state of its amoral and corrupt components, something that the totalitarian state does not possess, but rather converts the disease into chronic until the body cannot bear it. He also rejected absolute hierarchy, saying that prioritizing some parts over others is not understanding that necrosis ends up killing the entire body anyway." The Black Berets were not composed homogeneously. There existed the right and the left, the latter who ended up adhering to the political ideas of Kayser, after he created his party and they found points of friction with the right within the Black Berets. The Red Shirts are, then, "the purified version of the Black Berets", according to Kayser. One of the themes that Kayser strove to resolve before leaving power is the design of the ministry of culture, with the idea that the national spirit could not "become bureaucratic or monotonous", according to his Cordialist doctrine, because "that is the beginning of decadence". According to Silvestre Dalmau i Etxeberria and his book, Kayser strove greatly to design, along with other people, a ministry, with multiple mechanisms of revision, with mechanisms to prevent the very mechanisms of selection from being captured, that is, impersonal and non-discretionary rules that would depend on the capture of the process itself, very "hard" requirements, according to Silvestre Dalmau i Etxeberria, to be minister of culture, and that there would exist "the board", a perpetual committee charged with the constant revision of the myth, and that, according to Kayser, is "fundamental to correct its problems and maintain its absolute fervor". In large measure, the ministry of culture ensures through the ZGO (Zaldun Gazte Ordena, order of youthful chivalry) the maintenance of "that revolutionary spirit", according to the ministry itself and its role, so that "generation after generation, and through the young, that fervor is transmitted and perpetuated". All young people usually are members by obligation of the ZGO, who usually participate in the celebrations of independence carrying axes that symbolize "the death of the old and the rebirth, through the eternal rebirth of Albinya by means of the young".

### **Albinese Society**

After the approval of women's rights, such as the right to vote, labor rights, among others, a large part of society had to reorganize, since women were usually relegated to household matters. The socialist coalition managed to pass this law, arguing that women were tied to bad marriages, and since they could not get legal jobs, they either fell into poverty or informal work. This was not much to the liking of traditionalists, who saw this as a way of “weakening the cordialist order”. All citizens are raised from etiquette, from childhood until adulthood, instilled with nationalism full of "passion," according to the Minister of Culture himself. The Albinese educational system is oriented mainly toward the practical. In primary school, the basics are learned, such as reading and writing, learning to be self-sufficient (knowing how to cook, sew clothes, etc.) and other individual as well as social aspects where you are taught to work in teams, solve conflicts and regulate your emotions, this being part of your report card, since there are the "theoretical section," "practical section" and "social section," each of equal importance. From a young age, people are instilled with being "cordial," and history emphasizes how not being so has only "led the nation into catastrophe," according to primary school history books in Albinya. In secondary school, you take an aptitude exam to enter Arts and Humanities, Sciences and Engineering, Health, Military and Sports, Commerce and Administration, Teaching and Pedagogy, Trades and Technical Arts, or Agriculture and Natural Sciences. All have mandatory subjects but with other specialized subjects according to your aptitude. In secondary school, academies have agreements where students go to work in companies to gain experience, where they are taught the logic of working, the ethic of hard work and other characteristics that the ministry considers "ideal." You finish secondary school at approximately 17 or 18 years of age and, if you want, you enter university where you can choose the same career from the area of your secondary academy, directly if you passed with good grades, or if you want to choose another career, you must take an exam to enter (to access KFS social policies) or pay privately for your education if you have enough money. Education is private but regulated by the state. Young Albinese are taught how to walk correctly, always greet with a smile, never throw trash under any circumstance, not raise their voice, among other things expected of "cordial behavior." The law against antisocial behavior is real and the police, who are usually students of the military and sports academy, are educated with the idea of applying the law to the letter. Albinese society is anti-liberal in the philosophical sense. RSP (Reporters sense por) itself describes that society is not an "Orwellian dystopia" and that the government is a "reflection of society," where government policies actively promote those social ideas so that the next generations maintain the idea and do not "return to the chaos of before." Violent upbringing, including raising your voice, is prohibited by the child protection law. Parents have classes on how to raise their children and discipline them without the use of violence. Since the creation of conductive psychology in 1913, it has been applied by preference as a parenting method in the parents' manual in Albinya. After "the red wave" (that is, the left-wing coalition) lost its strength, conservative tendencies have returned in society. However, things such as homosexuality have lost their controversial force. The majority of Albinese, according to RSP surveys, have expressed indifference on the matter. Despite being unable to access marriage, they can access contracts or testimonies of the social contracts law of 1908, which was originally created as a way of helping the formalization of inheritances, which happened when you had no children or spouse, among other aspects. However, it has also allowed the legal vacuum to let them have basically the legal benefits of marriage without being formally married, although it is not only used by homosexuals but also unmarried people with friends or for cases "not stipulated within the traditional marriage law of 1880." Although there is a certain lag of homophobia within parliament, they are a very small minority and neither the laborists of the LNA nor the cordialists of the EBE have any interest in prohibiting it. The Albinese are not described as spontaneous people. In the book "Mechanical Society" by a writer, John Anglish, the Albinese are described as "machine people, with an extremely artificial culture," and according to him, if he had to reduce the Albinese to a stereotype, it would be "Religious fanatics of their nation, of rigid posture and literal mind, obsessive planners of every second, despisers of the spontaneous, the radical and the unforeseen, disciplined like machines, austere, obedient to hierarchy, distrustful of pleasure and leisure, incapable of conceiving chance, proud of their duty, sacrificed to the point of abnegation, moved by an irrational faith in cordialism and in a glorious destiny that makes them tremble". Within Albinese society, there is a saying that goes "there are only useless humans in inhuman systems." They refuse to build a society on a "perfectionism that drowns the very humanity of the being," but instead affirm in a very common phrase "Each one of us came into this world for a reason," and that the issue is that society, through the state, "helps man find his reason and not be abandoned by society." Cordiality, the idea of the organ, of that "majestic spirit," are not for them meant to crush man but to "console him, inspire him and ennoble him."

The most popular dishes of Albinese gastronomy are Gorria, which is a fried rice dish covered with strips of lean meat and roasted tomato sauce with onion. The sauce contains blended vegetables and ground chili in moderate quantity. It is served in a deep plate. Opiea is a baked dough of tender corn with fresh cheese inside. It contains finely chopped onion and pepper. It is cut into squares and eaten warm. Gisatua is rice cooked in defatted broth with chicken or pork in cubes. It incorporates grated carrot, zucchini and pepper, as well as ground dried chili. It is served without sauce on the side. Gozoa is a baked empanada with slightly sweet and salty dough, filled with melted cheese and caramelized onion. It is eaten hot and with the hand. Errekiak is lean meat roasted over indirect heat, with no visible fat. It is accompanied by bread and finely chopped green leaves mixed with tomato sauce.  The most popular sports are Borrokaldia, which consists of hadzajo combat. Ritual sazjo loincloths; women wear a garment that compresses the chest. Objective: neutralize without harming. Takedowns, joint locks, pressure points. Striking sensitive areas is prohibited. The winner is whoever immobilizes the opponent for three seconds or forces the rival out of the circle. Football and basketball are also popular. Jauziak is also a national sport that consists of urban and natural movement with jumps, climbs and controlled falls. It is taught in military academies and youth groups. Finally, Gudarria is a hadzajo strategic board game. It simulates armies with infantry, archers, cavalry and supplies. Objective: surround, cut off supplies and force surrender by siege.
