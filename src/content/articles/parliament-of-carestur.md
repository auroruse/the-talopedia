## Parliament of Carestur

### Parliament of Carestur

The **Parliament of Carestur** (*Parlament de Carestur* in Caresturian; *Parlement de Carestur* in Septimanian) is the bicameral national legislature of the **Republic of Carestur**. Established under **Title IV** of the ***Constitucion de Carestur*** on **1 June 1934**, it consists of an upper chamber, the ***Conselh de la Republica*** (*Council of the Republic*), and a lower chamber, the ***Assemblada Nacionala*** (*National Assembly*). Both chambers convene in the national capital of **Tolosa**.

Designed by the jurists of the ***Movement de Renovacion Meridiana*** to correct the chronic cabinet instability of the Third Republic, the Fourth Republic's parliament operates under strict rules of rationalized parliamentarism, balancing representative lawmaking with executive authority and technical review by the **Conselh Superior de la Competéncia**.

### History

Caresturian parliamentary institutions evolved across four distinct constitutional regimes following the 1818 unification:

#### First Republic (1818-1848)

Under the *Treaty of Pestelliére*, legislative power resided in a unicameral ***Dièta Federala*** (*Federal Diet*) that rotated annually between Tolosa, Barciona, and Marselha. Bound by provincial mandates and internal customs disputes, the Diet frequently deadlocked during economic crises.

#### Second Republic (1848-1875)

The 1848 charter introduced a permanent bicameral legislature in Tolosa alongside universal manhood suffrage for metropolitan citizens, though recurring clashes between the executive and legislature paralyzed governance by the mid-1870s.

#### Third Republic (1875-1934)

Under the *Constitutional Laws of 1875*, the **Third Caresturian Assembly** exercised near-absolute parliamentary supremacy over a ceremonial presidency. Without effective dissolution powers or party discipline, shifting legislative factions toppled dozens of cabinets during the postwar debt and strike crises of the 1920s and early 1930s.

In the spring of 1934, the ***Convencion Constitucionala de Tolosa*** abolished the 1875 order and instituted the current bicameral ***Parlament de Carestur***, curbing legislative obstruction while preserving elected representation across Metropolitan Carestur and its overseas territories.

## Chambers and Composition

### *Assemblada Nacionala (Lower House)*

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/GI8dmm0.jpeg" alt="***Hemicycle of the Assemblada Nacionala***"><figcaption>***Hemicycle of the Assemblada Nacionala***</figcaption></figure>

The **Assemblada Nacionala** is the primary legislative chamber and the sole house empowered to grant or withdraw parliamentary confidence from the Government.

**Membership and Election**: It comprises **577 deputies** (*deputats*) elected by direct universal suffrage for a **five-year term**, unless dissolved earlier by the President of the Republic under Title II of the Constitution.

**Powers:** All finance and budget bills (*leis de finanças*) must be introduced first in the Assemblada Nacionala. In the event of persistent legislative disagreement between the two chambers, the Government may ask the Assemblada Nacionala to cast the decisive final vote.

### *Conselh de la Republica* (Upper House)

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/Sd1P95R.jpeg" alt="***Chamber of the Conselh de la Republica***"><figcaption>***Chamber of the Conselh de la Republica***</figcaption></figure>

The **Conselh de la Republica** serves as the chamber of territorial representation, providing institutional continuity and reviewing legislation passed by the lower house.

**Membership and Election:** It comprises **348 councillors** (*conselhièrs*) elected indirectly by electoral colleges composed of regional councillors, municipal delegates, and deputies from Metropolitan Carestur and overseas territories.

**Staggered Mandate:** Councillors serve **six-year terms**, with the chamber divided into two equal regional series (*Series A* and *Series B*) renewed alternately every three years.

## *Congrès del Parlament* (Joint Session)

Under **Title VIII** of the Constitution, both chambers convene together in Tolosa as a joint ***Congrès del Parlament*** (925 members) to vote on constitutional amendment bills already approved in identical terms by each house separately. Ratification in joint session requires a three-fifths supermajority of votes cast, serving as the parliamentary alternative to a national referendum.

## Legislative Process and Rationalized Parliamentarism

**Title V** of the 1934 Constitution restructures parliamentary procedure to prevent the legislative paralysis that characterized the Third Republic:

**Domain of Law and Decree:** The Constitution enumerates the specific subjects reserved for parliamentary statute (*lei*)—including civil liberties, criminal law, taxation, national defense, and organic territorial statutes, while empowering the Prime Minister and Cabinet to govern administrative matters directly by executive decree (*decret*).

**Agenda Control:** The Government holds constitutional priority in setting the daily legislative order of business (*òrdre del jorn*), ensuring that cabinet bills and annual budgets are debated within fixed statutory deadlines.

**Fiscal Discipline:** Deputies and councillors are barred from introducing private members' bills (*proposicions de lei*) or amendments that would increase public expenditure or reduce state revenue without providing corresponding, verified fiscal offsets.

<figure class="float float-right r-16x9" style="width:500px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/isReslw.jpeg" alt="**Ministral Bench During Debate**"><figcaption>**Ministral Bench During Debate**</figcaption></figure>

**Motions of Censure:** The *Assemblada Nacionala* can force the resignation of the Prime Minister and Cabinet only through a formal **motion of censure** (*mocion de censura*), signed by at least one-tenth of all deputies and adopted by an absolute majority of the full membership (289 votes), with abstentions effectively counting in favor of the Government.

**Competence Review:** Prior to presidential promulgation, organic laws, such as the **Acte d'Algernon** of **23 January 1936,** annual budgets, and contested statutes may be referred to the **Conselh Superior de la Competéncia** under Title VI to verify their constitutional validity and compliance with fiscal-balance rules.
