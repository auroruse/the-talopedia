## Names

In Nichirin and in most official usage, the war is known as the Kōin War (甲寅戦争, *Kōin Sensō*), after the sexagenary designation of 1914, the year in which it began. Outside official usage, particularly in Evria, it is commonly referred to as the Xing-Nichirian War or the Serican War, the latter after the Bay of Serica, where its principal naval engagements occurred.

The Xing government avoided characterizing the conflict as an invasion and officially referred to it as the "War of Recovery" (光復戰爭, *Guāngfù Zhànzhēng*). It presented the war as the reclamation of Manshu, which the Empire regarded as rightfully its own, and as the liberation of the region's Hezhouan population from Nichirian rule. The name fell out of official use following the collapse of the Xing Empire. Guandong, its successor state, uses the Zhengyu reading of the Nichirian name, *Jiǎyín Zhànzhēng*.

## Background

Relations between Nichirian and Hezhouan polities were adversarial long before the [[manshuan-campaigns|Manshuan campaigns]] of the 18th century. Hezhouan courts viewed the Nichirian realm to the north as a distant, peripheral, and irrelevant entity, while Nichirian rulers regarded the larger Hezhouan states as a constant threat. The two competed for influence over [[tohanto]], which Nichirin assimilated during the 10th century, when Hezhou was divided by internal conflict. An uneasy peace held under the [[hayakawa-shogunate|Hayakawa]] (1185–1336) and [[kuronami-shogunate|Kuronami]] (1336–1551) Shogunates. During the [[age-of-chaos]] (1551–1603), the period of civil war that followed the collapse of the Kuronami Shogunate, the [[xing-empire|Xing dynasty]] expanded into [[manshu]] and subjugated the region's smaller independent states. It also attempted to seize Tohanto, but failed in the face of sustained resistance from the provincial daimyo.

### Manshuan Campaigns

Between 1764 and 1802, the Miyamoto Shogunate launched a series of campaigns into Manshu. The Xing lost Songbin ([[korihama|Kōrihama]]), Sa'ertu ([[daikei]]), Changquan ([[shikisen]]) and Shengjing ([[mizuhara]]) in successive campaigns, and with them the territory later known as Western Manshu. The defeats undermined the Xing court's view of the Empire as the center of Valtherian civilization, while the cost of the campaigns exhausted the shogunate's treasury. A truce concluded in 1802 left Eastern Manshu and its remaining population centers of Jinping (Nishikino), Dingyuan (Hyōtanjima), and Kaiguan ([[tohara|Tōhara]]) under Xing control.

During the [[napolyonic-wars]], while most Evrian powers were occupied, the two states attempted to coordinate the expansion of Valtherian trade and influence overseas. They also pursued economic cooperation in Manshu over the course of the 19th century, but ethnic conflict between Nichirians and Hezhouans in Western Manshu and recurring border incidents kept relations tense.

### Treaty of Kōrihama and Rearmament

A period of improved relations followed the signing of the Treaty of Kōrihama, a non-aggression pact signed in 1889, which settled the border in Manshu. The rapprochement ended with the [[shanggang-affair]] of 1906, in which the Xing court exposed [[hideaki-katsuragi]]'s principal intelligence network in the Empire, published the confessions of nineteen arrested agents and protested the operation as a breach of the treaty. The Bakufu disavowed the network to preserve the treaty, and sixteen of the agents were executed.

The Chongguang Emperor, who had come to the throne in 1897 and was known for his revanchism and personal hostility toward Nichirin, used the affair to justify an extensive rearmament. He also aligned the Empire with the Central Powers, in part to secure their support for the recovery of Manshu. Nichirin did not rearm on a comparable scale. Bureaucratic inertia, the neglect of the frontier after 1889, and the priority given to naval expansion in the military budget left the fortifications along the Shizuku River unfinished when war broke out.

## Course of the War

### Operation Wanbi (1914-15)

#### Outbreak

The Xing Empire opened hostilities during the [Bon festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obon) of 1914, when, under a provision of the Treaty of Kōrihama, the Nichirian frontier garrisons in Manshu had been reduced to roughly one-third strength for the festivals of the dead. At 04:00 on August 15, the declaration of war was delivered to [[nariyoshi-miyagi]], counselor of the Nichirian legation in Beining and a former military attaché to the Xing court. It took the form of the Chongguang Emperor's Edict of Recovery (光復詔), which enumerated seven grievances against the Shogunate, foremost among them the loss of Shengjing, and proclaimed general conscription.

At 05:30, Xing officials at the joint customs posts established under the treaty detained their Nichirian counterparts, and Xing forces crossed the frontier. Nichirian engineers demolished the Bridge of Accord, the frontier crossing at the mouth of the Shizuku River, to deny it to the Xing. Operation Wanbi (完璧, "Intact Jade"), directed by Generalissimo Liang Yu, aimed to restore the pre-1764 frontier on the [[matsuhana-river]] and thereby recover all of Western Manshu. General Xiang Kui's army advanced from Jinping (Nishikino) and Sangde into the plain enclosed by the Shizuku's loop, with a column moving along the river's southern arm from Wulanhada (Akamine). Farther south, General Murong Ce's army entered the southern plain through Sekiringaku. A covering force screened the lower Shizuku, and the fortress of Dingyuan served as the principal depot for operations along the coast. Xing planning relied on survey maps from the campaign of 1802, which predated the region's railways. The emperor vowed to celebrate the Mid-Autumn Festival of October 4 in Mizuhara.

Nichirin declared war on August 16, when Shogun Masanori Miyamoto ordered general mobilization; in Manshu, the Bakufu raised a militia of farmers equipped with rifles of 1880s manufacture. Following the fall of the last frontier posts on August 17, Marshal-General [[toranosuke-saionji]] implemented the Karesansui Plan (枯山水), a defense-in-depth scheme prepared in 1912. Named for the dry landscape garden, the plan cast the fortified river lines as the garden's stones and the territory to be ceded as its raked gravel. From August 19, Nichirian forces evacuated both plains and withdrew behind the Shizuku and the Matsuhana.

#### Nishikino Massacre

Jinping's Nichirian community, composed largely of brocade merchants, dated from the economic cooperation of the 19th century. On the day of the declaration, the garrison interned its members as suspected spies, beginning with the families named in the confessions published after the Shanggang Affair. Between August 16 and 18, troops of Xiang Kui's army, then concentrating in the city, executed the internees in the yards of the brocade mills. The Bakufu later recorded 12,960 dead; the Xing court acknowledged 611 executions of alleged Nichirian agents. A Hezhouan foreman concealed 212 Nichirian employees of a dye works, who escaped by sea to Benigata on August 21. Their testimony, published by the Bakufu in September, made the massacre a recurrent theme of Nichirian wartime propaganda.

#### Advance to the Shizuku

The Nichirian rearguard fought a delaying action in the Battle of Asamitsu (September 7–14) before withdrawing toward Mizuhara, and the Xing had yet to reach Tanretsu by the Mid-Autumn Festival. In the Battle of Ryōgaku (October 5–17), the Wulanhada column captured the town, which lies on the loop's southern arm. Aratame Jōzui, who had stormed Chiyoda Castle in 1868 during the [[bakuraku-war]] and served as Marshal-General of the Bulwark until 1901, assumed the Mizuhara Defense Command on October 9, operating at half manpower. After the First Battle of Tanretsu (October 19–31), the Xing closed on the river opposite the city; they held Tanretsu until September 1916.

The first Xing attempt to force the Shizuku came in December at Tetsuhachi, an ironworking town on the northern bank. Aerial reconnaissance on December 2 revealed boats concealed under reed thatch, and the battle opened the following day with the war's first large-scale human-wave assaults. Short of boats, the Xing ferried part of their infantry across on rafts improvised from house doors; the glare of the town's blast furnaces exposed the crossing to machine-gun and artillery fire. When the assault was abandoned on December 10, Xing casualties numbered about 40,000, against some 6,000 Nichirian.

Operations subsided over the winter. Effluent discharged by the Kagatsuchi Ironworks kept the river from freezing at Mizuhara, and engineers broke the ice nightly along the most vulnerable stretches. In June 1915, [[mayu-kitta]], recalled to active duty the previous month, succeeded Jōzui.

#### Advance to the Matsuhana

Murong Ce captured Sekiringaku within a week of the invasion and occupied the relatively undefended eastern portion of the Manshuan Plain before winter set in. His advance resumed in April 1915 at the Battle of Akaishi (April 6–18), where the Xing made the war's first use of chlorine gas. In the Battle of Hakushiro (May 9–24), the Xing pressed toward the Yawara until, on May 21, Nichirian engineers breached the river's eastern levees south of Daikei. The inundation halted the Xing and covered the Nichirian withdrawal across the river, but drowned an estimated 20,000 civilians. A first thrust into the Hyūga Mountains stalled in the Battle of Yakishi (June 3–28), when the plum rains rendered the roads impassable.

#### Occupation

On December 22, 1914, the winter solstice and the day of the imperial sacrifice to Heaven, the Xing proclaimed the Provisional Government of Manshu at Ryōgaku. Its head, Wen Shaoguang (温绍光), was a Hezhouan who had served the Bakufu as the town's deputy prefect. Administering the territory as a restored Imperial province, the government reinstated the land registers of 1764, restituting farmland to the heirs of Hezhouan families dispossessed in the Manshuan campaigns. It relocated to Wulanhada after Nichirin recaptured Ryōgaku in August 1916.

By the end of June 1915, some 1.2 million Nichirians had fled the occupied territories. The Xing held the interior of the Shizuku's loop and the southern plain as far as Hakushiro and the Hyūga Mountains, while the northern bank of the Shizuku, including Mizuhara, remained in Nichirian hands.

#### Naval and Air Operations

Marshal-Admiral Nobumasa Kurosawa kept the Nichirian battle fleet at Naginomiya, while Admiral Zheng Jingtao's Xing fleet avoided a general engagement and instead opted to raid Nichirian coastal shipping off the coast of [[shimonosaki]] and Andō. On November 21, 1914, Nichirian cruisers bombarded Dingyuan, which had repulsed the Shogunate's final campaign in 1802, and engaged the cruiser squadron based there. [[suiren-makinami]] served aboard the armored cruiser NCH Nyumetsu during the action.

The Aerial Corps, then comprising a few reconnaissance squadrons, flew its first combat sorties over Manshu in September 1914. Hayato Aotaka's squadron operated from the Mizuhara Racecourse in the absence of an airfield.

### Operation Chenzhou (1915-16)

#### Battle of Mizuhara

Operation Chenzhou (沉舟, "Sinking the Boats"), the Xing summer offensive of 1915, was named for the second half of the proverb "break the cauldrons and sink the boats" (破釜沉舟), derived from Commander Xiang Yu's crossing at Xingzhou in the War of National Restoration (208–206 BCE) against the Heng dynasty, when he had his army's boats sunk to deny it any means of retreat. Liang Yu's plan entrusted the assault on Mizuhara to Xiang Kui's army at Tanretsu and the envelopment of the Shizuku line from the west to Murong Ce's army, while a naval strike on Naginomiya was to neutralize Nichirin's Serican fleet. In anticipation of the emperor's entry into the city, Xing artillery was forbidden to fire on the former imperial palace of Shengjing, on the northern bank, which the Mizuhara Defense Command accordingly used as an observation post.

The offensive opened on July 19 with a bombardment of Mizuhara's southern districts, followed by successive human-wave assaults behind which supervising units (督戰隊) shot men who fell back. On July 22, assault battalions crossed the Shizuku outside the southern districts and, in keeping with the operation's name, scuttled their boats on landing at the Kagatsuchi Ironworks on the northern bank. Having exhausted their ammunition, the defenders destroyed the lodgment in hand-to-hand fighting with iron bars taken from the plant, an episode that gave rise to the Xing name for the defenders of Mizuhara, "red oni" (赤鬼, *chì guǐ*).

Kitta was taken prisoner on July 25, when Xing artillery destroyed his command post. Freed by a raid two days later, he resumed command and allowed a further crossing to establish itself on the northern bank before destroying it with massed artillery on July 29. By the end of the month, the northward offensive had become untenable, and on July 31, their last defenders withdrew across the river, demolishing the bridges behind them. The fighting of July had cost the Xing some 240,000 killed and wounded.

With the bridges down, the Shizuku formed the front line through the city for the following eight months, and the piers left standing in midstream became outposts contested in nightly raids. The defenders' artillery, reinforced with naval guns salvaged from the ships damaged at Naginomiya and remounted on railway cars, commanded the southern bank. Behind the line, the Defense Command interned Mizuhara's Hezhouan residents as suspected informants in August.

The largest attempt to force the river followed the emperor's renewal of his vow to celebrate the Mid-Autumn Festival in the city. In the Mid-Autumn Assault of September 23, the Xing suffered about 72,000 casualties in crossings at three points, the heaviest loss of any single day of the entire Great War. The incendiary bombardment that accompanied the assault started the Mid-Autumn Fire, which burned through the old city on the northern bank for three days before it was checked short of the palace.

Both sides continued to use chemical weapons. From November, the Defense Command fired phosgene into the southern districts, where its artillery also targeted Xing field hospitals. Xing artillery shelled the Kagatsuchi Furnace throughout the winter in an attempt to freeze the river, but its crews kept it burning, clearing the plant's water intakes of bodies daily to keep its cooling system running. Typhus broke out among the Xing in the ruins in December and killed more men that winter than the fighting. By the command's returns, a Nichirian replacement sent to the riverfront was killed or wounded within nine days on average, a duration estimated to be shorter by many magnitudes for their Xing counterparts.

#### Jigokugama Campaign

Between the Matsuhana and the Shizuku, where no river line covered the approaches to Mizuhara from the west, the Karesansui Plan had relied on the fortified settlements of Harushige, Himegami, Heitetsu, and Mikariba. In August, Xing forces advancing from the Manshuan Plain attacked Heitetsu, a junction on the railway between Mizuhara and Harushige, in the first attempt to turn the western end of the Shizuku line.

By October, all four settlements were under attack at once. Holding the interior lines, the Nichirian command shifted reserves between them by rail until the railway was cut. The Xing led their night assaults with dare-to-die squads (敢死隊) armed with broadswords. At Heitetsu, where the Xing deployed chlorine gas in the streets, the station changed hands fourteen times; at Mikariba, the fighting extended into the colliery galleries, which the defenders flooded when Xing parties entered them. Nichirian troops called the ground between the rivers Jigokugama (地獄釜, "Hell's Cauldron"), from the saying that the lid of hell's cauldron opens at Bon, the festival during which the war had begun. The name was later applied to the campaign as a whole.

The ridge at Himegami, crowned by a shrine to the princess deity for whom the town is named, commanded the railway between Harushige and Heitetsu. On the night of November 17, the last train out of Harushige crossed it under fire, carrying approximately 1,900 children. The ridge fell the next day, after assaults that had destroyed its shrine and cost the Xing about 95,000 casualties, and its loss cut the railway and isolated Harushige. The garrison of Harushige prepared to hold the city to the last, arming railway workers and other townspeople, women among them, from Manshū Tetsudō depots. Over the course of the siege, hunger and cold killed some 120,000 inhabitants. The Xing stormed the outer districts in January 1916, and the defenders, contesting the city block by block, fell back on the company's headquarters. There the last of them held out until February 3, the Lunar New Year, and fewer than a thousand of the garrison and its volunteers were taken alive.

After taking Heitetsu on December 12 and Mikariba a week later, the Xing turned east toward Kanamine and the northern bank of the Shizuku. General Kiyoshi Kiryū's forces held them in the Battle of Kadohara (January 10 – February 24), inflicting about 160,000 casualties. Xing columns to the north cut the railway to Shikisen at Umetsuji and reached Shiroyama and Kusurigura in February, where the Chōhaku Mountains precluded a further advance.

The Jigokugama Campaign cost the Xing about 1.35 million killed and wounded, of whom 490,000 died, and Nichirin about 690,000, of whom 240,000 died. Civilian deaths numbered roughly 320,000.

#### Crossings of the Matsuhana and Yawara

In the Battle of Matsumoto (September 14 – October 21), the Xing took the town near the confluence of the Matsuhana and the Yawara, on the eastern bank. The Xing command then deferred the crossing of both rivers until they froze, following the practice of the winter counteroffensives of the Manshuan campaigns. Meanwhile, the Battle of Aomizu (October 5–26) marked the limit of the Xing advance in the Hyūga Mountains, where the front remained static through the winter.

Crossings began in January 1916, once Xing engineers had built roads across the frozen rivers, thickening the ice by flooding it until it could bear artillery. Warm springs kept the Matsuhana thawed at Shikisen, so the northern force crossed south of the city and attacked it from the south in the Battle of Shikisen (January 11 – February 19). The fog that rises from the open water, the source of the rime ice for which the city is known, covered the Nichirian counterattack that destroyed the bridgehead on February 19.

Across the Yawara, the Xing occupied Daikei and its oil field in the Battle of Daikei (January 17 – February 2), while their cavalry crossed the frozen Matsuhana to raid the railway between Kōrihama and Shikisen. The advance continued through Kusanami to within reach of Kōrihama, and the Bakufu began to evacuate the city in February before reinforcements brought in by rail held the Xing short of its outer defenses.

At the end of March, the thaw broke up the ice roads, and the spring flood of the Yawara isolated the Xing forces west of the river in the Daikei Pocket. Several thousand drowned attempting to recross the breaking ice; the remainder set fire to the oil wells, which burned into the summer, and surrendered on March 30. Some 140,000 Xing were taken prisoner, the largest surrender of the war to that date, and the crossings had already cost them about 310,000 killed and wounded.

#### Naval and Air Operations

A collier captured in the raids of the previous year entered Naginomiya Harbor under Nichirian colors on July 25 and was detonated alongside the flagship. The fleet's flag officers were aboard for a conference convened by the Shogun. Kurosawa and several of his admirals were killed in the explosion, and Xing torpedo boats attacked in the ensuing confusion. The Shogun, who had not yet gone aboard, appointed Makinami, then twenty-one, to succeed Kurosawa as Marshal-Admiral that day.

Makinami's first engagement in command was the Battle of the Rensei Islands (November 12–13), in which aircraft from the carrier NCH Fudō located a Xing invasion convoy bound for Ayanami. Junior Admiral Ayaka Kyougoku's cruisers intercepted it, and the fleet repulsed the landing. At the First Battle of the Bay of Serica (March 3–4, 1916), Makinami closed with Zheng Jingtao's fleet by night and engaged it at dawn, inflicting heavy losses without destroying it entirely.

The Aerial Corps directed artillery fire over Mizuhara and bombed the Xing railhead at Tanretsu. Aotaka's squadron supplied the besieged garrison of Harushige by air from December, dropping food and ammunition.

#### Harushige Massacre

When news of the losses at the Bay of Serica reached Harushige on March 7, the Xing garrison posted a proclamation ordering that ten Nichirian civilians be shot for every sailor lost in the battle. The executions were carried out between March 8 and 12. With some 46,000 dead by Bakufu figures, the Harushige Massacre was the largest of the war on Nichirian soil. Ground Corps units invoked it in the reprisals against Xing prisoners that accompanied the counteroffensive later that year.

#### Operation Shōtan

Kitta launched Operation Shōtan (嘗胆, "Tasting Gall") at dawn on March 21, the spring equinox, alluding to King Goujian of the Xian Kingdom, who tasted gall daily until he had avenged his defeat in the Feng-Xian War. It was also a homophone of *shōtan* (焦炭; "coke"), which fueled the Kagatsuchi Furnace. While the Xing positions faced the frozen reaches upstream and downstream, assault boats crossed the open water at the Ironworks under a smoke screen from its coke ovens and retook the southern districts.

The operation ended the Battle of Mizuhara, the costliest of the war. Xing losses came to about 1.55 million killed and wounded, 600,000 of them dead, against about 640,000 Nichirian, including 220,000 dead. Roughly 210,000 of the city's civilians died, and the southern districts were left in ruins, with the torii of a Kaiseido shrine among the few structures still standing. In Evria, the battle for the city is known as "the Radagorsk of the North", after the Battle of Radagorsk, in which Rudanian forces held their capital against converging Alemannian, Skjarnish, and Turulian offensives.

### Xing Retreat (1916-17)

#### Withdrawal

Following the reverses of March, the Xing command ordered a staged withdrawal to the frontier of 1914. The interior of the Shizuku's loop was to be held as a delaying zone while the forces in Jigokugama and on the Manshuan Plain fell back. In the haste of the withdrawal, rear-echelon units were left behind without extraction orders. Liang Yu was recalled to Beining, tried for the failure of Chenzhou, and publicly beheaded on April 19; on the following day, the Chongguang Emperor assumed supreme command himself.

Through April and May, the spring thaw turned the plain's roads to mud, slowing the retreat and the pursuit alike and leaving the Xing columns exposed to the Aerial Corps. The Xing abandoned Matsumoto in April, destroying the ferries at the confluence as they left. Desertion rose sharply among the conscripts, whom supervising units shot when caught. The retreating columns burned villages and killed their inhabitants, and approximately 60,000 Nichirian civilians were deported to the coal mines of Kaiguan as laborers.

#### **Liberation of Jigokugama**

The counteroffensive in Jigokugama converged from two directions: crossing the Matsuhana from Shikisen, Nichirian forces recaptured Harushige (May 9–21), where the Xing rearguard fought from the ruins. A Bakufu commission then exhumed the dead of the siege and the massacre, recovered copies of the reprisal proclamation, and compiled the official figures for the massacre. Prisoners taken in the city were executed by Ground Corps units.

From the east, Kiryū's forces advanced from Kadohara and cleared the western end of the Shizuku line in the Second Battle of Heitetsu (June 3–17). The station at Heitetsu changed hands for a fifteenth and final time. Himegami and Mikariba, between the two advances, were retaken in June and July, although the collieries at Mikariba, flooded in 1915, could not be worked again until 1918. The railway to Shikisen reopened through Umetsuji on July 12.

#### Recapture of the Shizuku Loop

Kitta's offensive into the loop opened with the Second Battle of Ryōgaku (August 7–19). Once the town had been recovered, the Bakufu annulled the Provisional Government's restitutions under the registers of 1764 and evicted the Hezhouan claimants from the land.

The Xing defense collapsed in the Second Battle of Tanretsu on September 2, as Kitta's forces closed on the town from Ryōgaku and Mizuhara. Xiang Kui burned to death when Nichirian shelling ignited the coal seam beneath his headquarters in the mine workings, and his remains were identified by his seal; the seam itself burned until 1921. Kitta's forces also captured one of the rear-echelon units left behind in the retreat, and its men were executed. The Second Battle of Asamitsu (October 3–16) cleared the last Xing positions in his sector, which his command then held along the border opposite Sangde through the following year.

#### Manshuan Plain and Hyūga Mountains

The lowland inundated in 1915 was still a marsh, and Xing rearguards used it as a barrier as Nichirian forces retook Hakushiro in June and Akaishi in August. Aomizu followed in October, and the Xing dug in for the winter at the Yakishi Pass. Fought in deep snow at forty degrees below zero, the Battle of the Yakishi Pass in February 1917 cleared the mountains. On both sides, frostbite accounted for more casualties than enemy fire.

In the Battle of Sekiringaku (May 1–5), local Orughan herdsmen guided the Nichirian columns. The last Xing positions on Nichirian soil fell on May 5, the festival of Tango no Sekku, and the remnants of the garrison withdrew across the frontier. About 900,000 Xing were killed or wounded in the retreat and 380,000 taken prisoner; Nichirian losses came to about 520,000.

#### Naval and Air Operations

Nichirin's Serican fleet blockaded the Xing coast from the summer of 1916, halting the Empire's coastal shipping. By December, the Aerial Corps had won air superiority over Manshu, and enemy aircraft no longer operated there by day. The Second Battle of the Bay of Serica (February 9–10, 1917) opened with an air attack from the carrier NCH Fudō on the Xing squadron at Dingyuan. When the squadron put to sea, Makinami's battle line inflicted further losses on Zheng Jingtao's fleet.

### Xing Collapse (1917-18)

#### Operation Hishō

Operation Hishō (飛将, "Flying General") took its name from a Hezhouan frontier couplet holding that, had the Flying General been present, no northern horseman would have crossed the Yin Mountains, where the operation's last campaign would be fought. It opened on May 10, 1917, five days after the fall of Sekiringaku, with attacks on Wulanhada and on Jinping, where its drive through Eastern Manshu began. Contingents from Kiritani and Shin-Nichirin (now Hōrai and Kinshū) joined the offensive.

In the Battle of Wulanhada (May 10–26), Nichirian forces occupied the seat of the Provisional Government of Manshu and captured Wen Shaoguang, who was later tried for treason and imprisoned for life. Nichirian troops entered Jinping on May 18 and took reprisals there for the Nishikino Massacre. Besieged from June 7, Dingyuan held out until June 19, when naval shellfire detonated the fortress's main magazine.

Kaiguan, the Xing capital of Manshu, was invested at the end of June. A relief army marching from Beining approached under cover of the summer sorghum, whose tall stands are known as the "green curtain" (青紗帳). In what became known as the Battle of the Green Curtain (July 23–30), Nichirian troops cut down the stands along the approaches and destroyed the relief army in the open. An amphibious landing at Sumerajima on August 9 closed the siege from the sea, and the garrison surrendered on September 21. In the city's coal mines, the besiegers found the civilians deported in 1916; fewer than half of the 60,000 had survived.

#### Sangde Mutiny

On October 10, after the harvest had been requisitioned for the army, the guard of the imperial summer palace at Sangde mutinied, and the garrison followed. Sun Jianhe, later the founder of the Sun clique, proclaimed the Sangde Autonomous Council, with Guan Zhenting commanding its armed contingent, and opened the frontier to Kitta's command, whose forces spared the city. The mutineers still wore Xing uniform, distinguished only by white armbands. Unable to tell friend from foe, the punitive columns sent against the city fired into one another in the mountain passes before Guan Zhenting's troops and Kitta's command destroyed them in the Battle of the White Armbands (November 4–20).

Elsewhere, provincial commanders, among them the future founders of the Chen and Huang cliques, withheld their armies from the imperial government. Over the winter, grain riots broke out in Beining and Jingnan as famine set in.

#### Operation Kangeiko

Hishō's winter march on Beining, Operation Kangeiko (寒稽古), began on January 6, 1918. Its name, for the martial training held through the coldest weeks of the year, answered the winter counteroffensives with which the Xing had worn down the Shogunate's armies between 1764 and 1802. Nichirian forces advanced on the capital from Kaiguan and from Sangde, where Guan Zhenting's troops joined them. In the Battle of Tianji Harbor (January 12–30), Nichirian infantry crossed the ice to capture the remnant of the Xing fleet, which had been frozen into the harbor since late December. The fall of the port cut Beining off from the sea.

The Battle of the Outer Wall (February 8–27) was fought in snow before the capital's outer fortifications, which Nichirian sappers breached by tunneling beneath the frozen moat and detonating mines under the wall on February 27. Villages along the line of march were burned in reprisal for partisan attacks, but Saionji forbade any bombardment of the city itself. Both sides suffered heavily in the ten-week campaign, the Xing losing about 850,000 killed and wounded and 310,000 taken prisoner, and Nichirin about 560,000. Beining fell on March 14, and as the garrison capitulated, the Chongguang Emperor hanged himself in the palace grounds. His five-year-old son, Zairui (載睿), was proclaimed emperor at Jingnan, which became the provisional capital. He reigned under a regency as the Zhongxing Emperor (中興, "Revival").

#### Operation Huiguang

The regency launched a counteroffensive on April 18, advancing on Beining along two axes, by the road through Deqiang in the east and through Shiyi and Baiding in the west. Huiguang (回光), the operation's codename, was drawn from 回光返照, the flare of strength that precedes death. The Morgenlandkorps, the Holy Evrian Empire's Valtherian expeditionary force, marched from Jiaozhou with the eastern force.

In the Battle of Baiding (April 25 – May 3), the western advance ended when the cavalry of the imperial guard made the last mounted charge of the Xing army and was cut down by machine guns and aircraft. The eastern force was destroyed in the Battle of Deqiang (May 6–21), where Nichirian tanks saw their first action and whole divisions surrendered. After the outbreak of the Holy Evrian Civil War that month, the Morgenlandkorps withdrew to Jiaozhou.

#### Advance on Jingnan

Nichirian vanguard units took the rail junction at Shiyi in June with little resistance. In the Battle of the Lady's Pass in July, the Xing held the walled defile through which the railway runs to Jinyang; its fall opened the arsenal city to Nichirian forces.

Famine and cholera spread through Guandong over the summer, killing millions. In August, Saionji handed command to Kiryū on grounds of ill health. The same month, under the separate settlement that Ymiria had reached with Nichirin during the Holy Evrian Civil War, Nichirian forces interned the Morgenlandkorps at Jiaozhou and allowed them to return to Alemannia. After the regency left Jingnan for Shanggang on August 27, the city's commander, later a founder of the Chen clique, surrendered it on his own authority two days later.

With Jingnan taken, Nichirin began in September to send troops to Rudania, whose army was increasingly composed of conscripts. Their arrival eased the pressure on the Northern Front as the Holy Evrian Empire lost its hold on its entrenched positions in Arverne.

#### Yin Mountains Campaign

Murong Ce's Southern Army withdrew into the Yin Mountains, and the campaign against it opened on September 1 with the Battle of the Kalgan Pass. Frontal assaults on the ridgelines stalled at the cost of most of the campaign's 290,000 Nichirian casualties, and on September 9, Kitta took command. He carried the pass in early October by replacing the assaults with lateral advances along the slopes, each covered by staggered fallback positions at the same elevation. The horsemen of the Hyūga Orughan Banner, which had joined the Nichirian forces in the Hezhouan part of the range, guided the advances, and its commander, Barsbold, was killed leading them into battle.

After the Battle of Fuhua (October 8–17), fought for the towers of the old frontier wall above the town, the Southern Army was encircled at the eastern end of the range in the Battle of Daiping (October 20 – November 11). Murong Ce surrendered on November 11, the day of the armistice, by which time his army had lost about 410,000 killed and wounded and 190,000 taken prisoner.

#### Grand Canal and Shanggang

Advancing overland from Jingnan, Nichirian forces fought the Battle of the Grand Canal (October 2–16) for the locks and embankments around Pengcheng. On October 22, the Shanggang Landing, the largest amphibious operation of the war, put Nichirian forces ashore from the Bay of Serica, with Kyougoku commanding the naval force. The regency escaped inland to Hengliu before Shanggang, the last provisional capital, fell on November 3.

#### Naval and Air Operations

Makinami's fleet crippled the Xing navy as an operational force in the Third Battle of the Bay of Serica (July 14–15, 1917), in which Zheng Jingtao was captured. Part of the fleet then sailed with Makinami on Entente convoy escort for the remainder of the war, leaving a squadron under Kyougoku in the Bay of Serica to support landings on the Xing coast. Aerial Corps bombers struck the rail yards behind the Xing lines and the arsenal at Jingnan throughout 1918.

#### Armistice

An armistice was signed on November 11 between Nichirin at the regency at Hengliu. That day, the Southern Army's surrender at Daiping became the last action on the Valtherian front, with Nichirian forces holding a line from Beining and the Yin Mountains to Shanggang. Bakufu figures put Xing military losses between May 1917 and the armistice at about 3.8 million killed and wounded and 1.3 million captured, and Nichirian losses at about 2.4 million. The famine, which continued into 1919, brought Xing civilian deaths over the course of the war to approximately 6 million.

## Aftermath

### Treaty of Kaiguan

On February 3, 1919, the war was formally concluded by the Treaty of Kaiguan, three years to the day after the fall of Harushige. In exchange for the evacuation of occupied Guandong, from Beining to Shanggang, the Xing Empire ceded their holdings in Eastern Manshu, including Kaiguan, to Nichirin. Jinping, Dingyuan, Kaiguan, and Wulanhada were renamed Nishikino, Hyōtanjima, Tōhara, and Akamine respectively. The Empire revoked the Edict of Recovery, renounced all claims to Manshu and surrendered its archives of the region, among them the land registers of 1764 under which the Provisional Government had made its restitutions. A demilitarized zone was established along the new frontier, subject to inspection by Nichirian officers.

An indemnity, payable over thirty years, was secured on the Empire's maritime customs under a Nichirian inspectorate. Tianji and Shanggang were opened as treaty ports, with consular jurisdiction over Nichirian nationals. The railway from Eastern Manshu toward Beining fell under the administration of Manshū Tetsudō as far as the new border, and a labor convention supplied the mines and works of Kaiguan with Guandongese contract workers. Nichirin also succeeded to the harbor and railway rights that the Holy Evrian Empire had held at Jiaozhou, where the Morgenlandkorps had been interned.

Nichirian prisoners and the surviving deportees were returned first, and the repatriation of approximately 1.8 million Xing prisoners was completed in December 1919. The regency accepted the Bakufu's figures for the Nishikino and Harushige massacres and undertook to compensate the survivors and the families of the deportees. Of the 214 officers named for trial in an annex to the treaty, it surrendered 19. Hezhouan residents of the ceded territory were given two years to choose between Nichirian nationality and emigration to Guandong. The treaty also recognized the Sangde Autonomous Council under a Nichirian guarantee and extended an amnesty to the Sangde mutineers and to the Hyūga Orughan Banner, whose people were permitted to settle in the Sekiringaku Orughan Autonomous Prefecture.

Zheng Jingtao and Murong Ce, the only Xing commanders to survive the war, were tried separately by court-martial in 1919. Zheng was sentenced to ten years' imprisonment for his orchestration of the attack on Naginomiya Harbor, in which a captured collier had been sent in flying Nichirian colors; regarded in Nichirin as an honorable adversary and implicated in none of the Xing war crimes, he was released for good conduct in 1922. Murong Ce, who had surrendered at Daiping, was sentenced to life imprisonment for the Harushige Massacre, carried out by a garrison under his command, and for the village burnings and deportations of the 1916 withdrawal. By 1921, Nichirian courts-martial had sentenced 1,146 lower-ranking Xing officers, most of them taken prisoner during the war, for war crimes of their own, and 87 Nichirian officers for theirs.

### Guandongese Civil War

*Main article: [[guandongese-civil-war]]*

The regency in Hengliu dissolved after the signing of the treaty. In April 1919, the Zhongxing Emperor, then six, abdicated, leaving the provincial commanders to contest what remained of the Empire. In the ensuing Guandongese Civil War, more than thirty cliques, the largest of them the Chen, Huang, and Sun cliques, fought one another and the Mintsutang for control of the country. Control of the ports carried the customs surplus, which the Nichirian inspectorate released once each indemnity payment had been met. The Huang clique occupied Jingnan in July 1920 and restored the former emperor, but the restoration lasted twenty-three days, and when it collapsed he was taken to Jiaozhou and from there to Nichirin, where the Bakufu granted him asylum. He has since lived at an undisclosed location in Southern Manshu.

Secure at Sangde under the treaty's guarantee, the Sun clique took control of Guandong on March 7, 1922. Sun Jianhe, who had adopted communism during the civil war, proclaimed the People's Republic of Guandong, formally abolished the monarchy, and repudiated the Kaiguan indemnity as a debt of the fallen dynasty, which Nichirin continued to claim.

### Normalization

Relations between Nichirin and Guandong remained tense under Masanori Miyamoto. Approximately 340,000 of the ceded territory's Hezhouan residents had left for Guandong by the expiry of the option in February 1921, and those who stayed shared the suspicion that attached to Hezhouans throughout Nichirin. The proclamation of a communist republic across the new frontier, two years after the revolution in Rudania, alarmed the Bakufu, which took it for an ally of Radagorsk. Guandong refused to recognize the cession, incidents in the demilitarized zone were frequent, and Eastern Manshu was kept under military administration.

Masashi Miyamoto, who succeeded his father in 1923, convened the Tōhara Conference in March 1926. Sun's denunciation of Radagorsk's communism at the conference dispelled the Bakufu's fear of a Rudanian alignment. Under the agreements reached there, which superseded the Treaty of Kaiguan, Guandong recognized the cession and Nichirin the People's Republic, and trade protocols replaced the customs inspectorate and the consular courts. Nichirin gave up its rights at Jiaozhou, and Eastern Manshu was organized into autonomous prefectures under a joint border administration, whose patrols replaced the Nichirian inspections of the demilitarized zone. Kitta opposed the agreements publicly through the veterans' associations as a betrayal of the cost of the war, but incidents along the border fell within months of the first joint patrols.
