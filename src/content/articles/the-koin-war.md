## Names

In Nichirin and in most official usage, the war is known as the Kōin War (甲寅戦争, *Kōin Sensō*), after the sexagenary designation of 1914, the year in which it began. Outside official usage, particularly in Evria, it is commonly referred to as the Xing-Nichirian War or the Serican War, the latter after the Bay of Serica, where its principal naval engagements occurred.

The Xing government avoided characterizing the conflict as an invasion and officially referred to it as the "War of Recovery" (光復戰爭, *Guāngfù Zhànzhēng*). It presented the war as the reclamation of Manshu, which the Empire regarded as rightfully its own, and as the liberation of the region's Hezhouan population from Nichirian rule. The name fell out of official use following the collapse of the Xing Empire. Guandong, its successor state, uses the Zhengyu reading of the Nichirian name, *Jiǎyín Zhànzhēng*.

## Background

Relations between Nichirian and Hezhouan polities were adversarial long before the [[manshuan-campaigns|Manshuan campaigns]] of the 18th century. Hezhouan courts viewed the Nichirian realm to the north as a distant, peripheral, and irrelevant entity, while Nichirian rulers regarded the larger Hezhouan states as a constant threat. The two competed for influence over [[tohanto]], which Nichirin assimilated during the 10th century, while Hezhou was divided by internal conflict. An uneasy peace held under the [[hayakawa-shogunate|Hayakawa]] (1185–1336) and [[kuronami-shogunate|Kuronami]] (1336–1551) Shogunates. During the [[age-of-chaos]] (1551–1603), the period of civil war that followed the collapse of the Kuronami Shogunate, the [[xing-empire|Xing dynasty]] expanded into [[manshu]] and subjugated the region's smaller independent states. It also attempted to seize Tohanto, but failed in the face of sustained resistance from the provincial daimyo.

### Manshuan Campaigns

Between 1764 and 1802, the Miyamoto Shogunate launched a series of campaigns into Manshu. The Xing lost Songbin ([[korihama|Kōrihama]]), Sa'ertu ([[daikei]]), Changquan ([[shikisen]]) and Shengjing ([[mizuhara]]) in successive campaigns, and with them the territory later known as Western Manshu. The defeats undermined the Xing court's view of the Empire as the center of Valtherian civilization, while the cost of the campaigns exhausted the shogunate's treasury. A truce concluded in 1802 left Eastern Manshu and its remaining population centers of Jinping (Nishikino), Dingyuan (Hyōtanjima), and Kaiguan ([[tohara|Tōhara]]) under Xing control.

During the [[napolyonic-wars]], while most Evrian powers were occupied, the two states attempted to coordinate the expansion of Valtherian trade and influence overseas. They also pursued economic cooperation in Manshu over the course of the 19th century, but ethnic conflict between Nichirians and Hezhouans in Western Manshu and recurring border incidents kept relations tense.

### Treaty of Kōrihama and Rearmament

A period of improved relations followed the signing of the Treaty of Kōrihama, a non-aggression pact signed in 1889, which settled the border in Manshu. The rapprochement ended with the [[shanggang-affair]] of 1906, in which the Xing court exposed [[hideaki-katsuragi]]'s principal intelligence network in the Empire, published the confessions of nineteen arrested agents and protested the operation as a breach of the treaty. The Bakufu disavowed the network to preserve the treaty, and sixteen of the agents were executed.

The Chongguang Emperor, who had come to the throne in 1897 and was known for his revanchism and personal hostility toward Nichirin, used the affair to justify an extensive rearmament. He also aligned the Empire with the Central Powers, in part to secure their support for the recovery of Manshu. Nichirin did not rearm on a comparable scale. Bureaucratic inertia, the neglect of the frontier after 1889, and the priority given to naval expansion in the military budget left the fortifications along the Shizuku River unfinished when war broke out.

## Course of the War

### Operation Wanbi (1914-1915)

#### Outbreak

The Xing Empire opened hostilities during the [Bon festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obon) of 1914, when, under a provision of the Treaty of Kōrihama, the Nichirian frontier garrisons in Manshu had been reduced to roughly one-third strength for the festivals of the dead. At 04:00 on August 15, the declaration of war was delivered to [[nariyoshi-miyagi]], counselor of the Nichirian legation in Beining and a former military attaché to the Xing court. It took the form of the Chongguang Emperor's Edict of Recovery (光復詔), which enumerated seven grievances against the Shogunate, foremost among them the loss of Shengjing, and proclaimed general conscription.

At 05:30, Xing officials at the joint customs posts established under the treaty detained their Nichirian counterparts, and Xing forces crossed the frontier. Nichirian engineers demolished the Bridge of Accord, the frontier crossing at the mouth of the Shizuku River, to deny it to the Xing. Operation Wanbi (完璧, "Intact Jade"), directed by Generalissimo Liang Yu, aimed to restore the pre-1764 frontier on the [[matsuhana-river]] and thereby recover all of Western Manshu. General Xiang Kui's army advanced from Jinping (Nishikino) and Sangde into the plain enclosed by the Shizuku's loop, with a column moving along the river's southern arm from Wulanhada (Akamine). Farther south, General Murong Ce's army entered the southern plain through Sekiringaku. A covering force screened the lower Shizuku, and the fortress of Dingyuan served as the principal depot for operations along the coast. Xing planning relied on survey maps from the campaign of 1802, which predated the region's railways. The emperor vowed to celebrate the Mid-Autumn Festival of October 4 in Mizuhara.

Nichirin declared war on August 16, when Shogun Masanori Miyamoto ordered general mobilization; in Manshu, the Bakufu raised a militia of farmers equipped with rifles of 1880s manufacture. Following the fall of the last frontier posts on August 17, Marshal-General [[toranosuke-saionji]] implemented the Karesansui Plan (枯山水), a defense-in-depth scheme prepared in 1912. Named for the dry landscape garden, the plan cast the fortified river lines as the garden's stones and the territory to be ceded as its raked gravel. From August 19, Nichirian forces evacuated both plains and withdrew behind the Shizuku and the Matsuhana.

#### Nishikino Massacre

Jinping's Nichirian community, composed largely of brocade merchants, dated from the economic cooperation of the 19th century. On the day of the declaration, the garrison interned its members as suspected spies, beginning with the families named in the confessions published after the Shanggang Affair. Between August 16 and 18, troops of Xiang Kui's army, then concentrating in the city, executed the internees in the yards of the brocade mills. The Bakufu later recorded 12,960 dead; the Xing court acknowledged 611 executions of alleged Nichirian agents. A Hezhouan foreman concealed 212 Nichirian employees of a dye works, who escaped by sea to Benigata on August 21. Their testimony, published by the Bakufu in September, made the massacre a recurrent theme of Nichirian wartime propaganda.

#### Advance to the Shizuku

At the Battle of Asamitsu (September 7–14), the Nichirian rearguard fought its first delaying action before withdrawing toward Mizuhara. By the Mid-Autumn Festival, the Xing had yet to reach Tanretsu. In the Battle of Ryōgaku (October 5–17), the Wulanhada column captured the town, which lies on the loop's southern arm. On October 9, Aratame Jōzui, who had stormed Chiyoda Castle in 1868 during the [[bakuraku-war]] and served as Marshal-General of the Bulwark until 1901, assumed the Mizuhara Defense Command, operating at half manpower. After the First Battle of Tanretsu (October 19–31), the Xing closed on the river opposite the city; they held Tanretsu until September 1916.

The first Xing attempt to force the Shizuku came in December at Tetsuhachi, an ironworking town on the northern bank. Aerial reconnaissance on December 2 revealed boats concealed under reed thatch, and the battle opened the following day with the war's first large-scale human-wave assaults. Short of boats, the Xing ferried part of their infantry across on rafts improvised from house doors; the glare of the town's blast furnaces exposed the crossing to machine-gun and artillery fire. When the assault was abandoned on December 10, Xing casualties numbered about 40,000, against some 6,000 Nichirian.

Operations subsided over the winter. Effluent discharged by the Kagatsuchi Ironworks kept the river from freezing at Mizuhara, and engineers broke the ice nightly along the most vulnerable stretches. In June 1915, [[mayu-kitta]], recalled to active duty the previous month, succeeded Jōzui.

#### Advance to the Matsuhana

Murong Ce captured Sekiringaku within a week of the invasion and occupied the relatively undefended eastern portion of the Manshuan Plain before winter set in. His advance resumed in April 1915 at the Battle of Akaishi (April 6–18), where the Xing made the war's first use of chlorine gas. In the Battle of Hakushiro (May 9–24), the Xing pressed toward the Matsuhana until, on May 21, Nichirian engineers breached the river's eastern levees south of Daikei. The inundation halted the Xing and covered the Nichirian withdrawal across the river, but drowned an estimated 20,000 civilians. A final thrust into the Hyūga Mountains stalled in the Battle of Yakishi (June 3–28), when the plum rains rendered the roads impassable.

#### Occupation

On December 22, 1914, the winter solstice and the day of the imperial sacrifice to Heaven, the Xing proclaimed the Provisional Government of Manshu at Ryōgaku. Its head, Wen Shaoguang (温绍光), was a Hezhouan who had served the Bakufu as the town's deputy prefect. Administering the territory as a restored Imperial province, the government reinstated the land registers of 1764, restituting farmland to the heirs of Hezhouan families dispossessed in the Manshuan campaigns. It relocated to Wulanhada after Nichirin recaptured Ryōgaku in August 1916.

By the end of June 1915, some 1.2 million Nichirians had fled the occupied territories. The Xing held the interior of the Shizuku's loop and the southern plain as far as Hakushiro and the Hyūga Mountains, while the northern bank of the Shizuku, including Mizuhara, remained in Nichirian hands.

#### Naval and Air Operations

Marshal-Admiral Nobumasa Kurosawa kept the Nichirian battle fleet at Naginomiya, while Admiral Zheng Jingtao's Xing fleet avoided a general engagement and instead opted to raid Nichirian coastal shipping off the coast of [[shimonosaki]] and Andō. On November 21, 1914, Nichirian cruisers bombarded Dingyuan, which had repulsed the Shogunate's final campaign in 1802, and engaged the cruiser squadron based there. [[suiren-makinami]] served aboard the armored cruiser NCH Nyumetsu during the action.

The Aerial Corps, then comprising a few reconnaissance squadrons, flew its first combat sorties over Manshu in September 1914. Hayato Aotaka's squadron operated from the Mizuhara Racecourse in the absence of an airfield.

### Operation Chenzhou (1915-1916)

#### Battle of Mizuhara

Operation Chenzhou (沉舟, "Sinking the Boats"), the Xing summer offensive of 1915, was named for the second half of the proverb "break the cauldrons and sink the boats" (破釜沉舟), derived from Commander Xiang Yu's crossing at Xingzhou in the War of National Restoration (208–206 BCE) against the Heng dynasty, when he had his army's boats sunk to deny it any means of retreat. Liang Yu's plan entrusted the assault on Mizuhara to Xiang Kui's army at Tanretsu and the envelopment of the Shizuku line from the west to Murong Ce's army, while a naval strike on Naginomiya was to neutralize Nichirin's Serican fleet. In anticipation of the emperor's entry into the city, Xing artillery was forbidden to fire on the former imperial palace of Shengjing, on the northern bank, which the Mizuhara Defense Command accordingly used as an observation post.

The offensive opened on July 19 with a bombardment of Mizuhara's southern districts, followed by successive human-wave assaults behind which supervising units (督戰隊) shot men who fell back. On July 22, assault battalions crossed the Shizuku outside the southern districts and, in keeping with the operation's name, scuttled their boats on landing at the Kagatsuchi Ironworks on the northern bank. Having exhausted their ammunition, the defenders destroyed the lodgment in hand-to-hand fighting with iron bars taken from the plant, an episode that gave rise to the Xing name for the defenders of Mizuhara, "red oni" (赤鬼, *chì guǐ*).

Kitta was taken prisoner on July 25, when Xing artillery destroyed his command post. Freed by a raid two days later, he resumed command and allowed a further crossing to establish itself on the northern bank before destroying it with massed artillery on July 29. By the end of the month, the northward offensive had become untenable, and on July 31, their last defenders withdrew across the river, demolishing the bridges behind them. The fighting of July had cost the Xing some 240,000 killed and wounded.

With the bridges down, the Shizuku formed the front line through the city for the following eight months, and the piers left standing in midstream became outposts contested in nightly raids. The defenders' artillery, reinforced with naval guns salvaged from the ships damaged at Naginomiya and remounted on railway cars, commanded the southern bank. Behind the line, the Defense Command interned Mizuhara's Hezhouan residents as suspected informants in August.

The largest attempt to force the river followed the emperor's renewal of his vow to celebrate the Mid-Autumn Festival in the city. In the Mid-Autumn Assault of September 23, crossings at three points cost the Xing about 72,000 casualties, the heaviest loss of any single day of the entire Great War. The incendiary bombardment that accompanied the assault started the Mid-Autumn Fire, which burned through the old city on the northern bank for three days before it was checked short of the palace.

Both sides continued to use chemical weapons. From November, the Defense Command fired phosgene into the southern districts, where its artillery also targeted Xing field hospitals. Xing artillery shelled the Kagatsuchi Furnace throughout the winter in an attempt to freeze the river, but its crews kept it burning, clearing the plant's water intakes of bodies daily to keep its cooling system running. Typhus broke out among the Xing in the ruins in December and killed more men that winter than the fighting. By the command's returns, a Nichirian replacement sent to the riverfront was killed or wounded within nine days on average, a duration estimated to be shorter by many magnitudes for their Xing counterparts.

#### Jigokugama Campaign

Between the Matsuhana and the Shizuku, where no river line covered the approaches to Mizuhara from the west, the Karesansui Plan had relied on the fortified settlements of Harushige, Himegami, Heitetsu, and Mikariba. In August, Xing forces advancing from the Manshuan Plain attacked Heitetsu, a junction on the railway between Mizuhara and Harushige, in the first attempt to turn the western end of the Shizuku line.

By October, all four settlements were under attack at once. Holding the interior lines, the Nichirian command shifted reserves between them by rail until the railway was cut. The Xing led their night assaults with dare-to-die squads (敢死隊) armed with broadswords. At Heitetsu, where the Xing deployed chlorine gas in the streets, the station changed hands fourteen times; at Mikariba, the fighting extended into the colliery galleries, which the defenders flooded when Xing parties entered them. Nichirian troops called the ground between the rivers Jigokugama (地獄釜, "Hell's Cauldron"), from the saying that the lid of hell's cauldron opens at Bon, the festival during which the war had begun. The name was later applied to the campaign as a whole.

The ridge at Himegami, crowned by a shrine to the princess deity for whom the town is named, commanded the railway between Harushige and Heitetsu. On the night of November 17, the last train out of Harushige crossed it under fire, carrying approximately 1,900 children. The ridge fell the next day, after assaults that had destroyed its shrine and cost the Xing about 95,000 casualties, and its loss cut the railway and isolated Harushige. The garrison of Harushige prepared to hold the city to the last, arming railway workers and other townspeople, women among them, from Manshū Tetsudō depots. Over the course of the siege, hunger and cold killed some 120,000 inhabitants. The Xing stormed the outer districts in January 1916, and the defenders, contesting the city block by block, fell back on the company's headquarters. There the last of them held out until February 3, the Lunar New Year, and fewer than a thousand of the garrison and its volunteers were taken alive.

After taking Heitetsu on December 12, the Xing turned east toward Kanamine and the northern bank of the Shizuku. General Kiyoshi Kiryū's forces held them in the Battle of Kadohara (January 10 – February 24), at a cost to the Xing of about 160,000 casualties. Farther north, Xing columns cut the railway to Shikisen at Umetsuji and reached Shiroyama and Kusurigura in February, where the Chōhaku Mountains precluded a further advance.

The Jigokugama Campaign cost the Xing about 1.35 million killed and wounded, of whom 490,000 died, and Nichirin about 690,000, of whom 240,000 died. Civilian deaths numbered roughly 320,000.

#### Crossings of the Matsuhana and Yawara

In the Battle of Matsumoto (September 14 – October 21), the Xing took the town near the confluence of the Matsuhana and the Yawara, on the eastern bank, after Nichirian engineers had demolished the railway bridge there. The Xing command then deferred the crossing of both rivers until they froze, following the practice of the winter counteroffensives of the Manshuan campaigns. Farther south, the Battle of Aomizu (October 5–26) marked the limit of the Xing advance in the Hyūga Mountains, where the front remained static through the winter.

Crossings began in January 1916, once Xing engineers had built roads across the frozen rivers, thickening the ice by flooding it until it could bear artillery. Warm springs kept the Matsuhana thawed at Shikisen, so the northern force crossed south of the city and attacked it from the south in the Battle of Shikisen (January 11 – February 19). The fog that rises from the open water, the source of the rime ice for which the city is known, covered the Nichirian counterattack that destroyed the bridgehead on February 19.

Across the Yawara, the Xing occupied Daikei and its oil field in the Battle of Daikei (January 17 – February 2), while their cavalry crossed the frozen Matsuhana to raid the railway between Kōrihama and Shikisen. The advance continued through Kusanami to within reach of Kōrihama, and the Bakufu began to evacuate the city in February before reinforcements brought in by rail held the Xing short of its outer defenses.

At the end of March, the thaw broke up the ice roads, and the spring flood of the Yawara isolated the Xing forces west of the river in the Daikei Pocket. Several thousand drowned attempting to recross the breaking ice; the remainder set fire to the oil wells, which burned into the summer, and surrendered on March 30. Some 140,000 Xing were taken prisoner, the largest surrender of the war to that date, and the crossings had already cost them about 310,000 killed and wounded.

#### Naval and Air Operations

On July 25, a collier captured in the raids of the previous year entered Naginomiya Harbor under Nichirian colors and was detonated alongside the flagship. The fleet's flag officers were aboard for a conference convened by the Shogun. Kurosawa and several of his admirals were killed in the explosion, and Xing torpedo boats attacked in the ensuing confusion. The Shogun, who had not yet gone aboard, appointed Makinami, then twenty-one, to succeed Kurosawa as Marshal-Admiral that day.

In the Battle of the Rensei Islands (November 12–13), Makinami's first engagement in command, aircraft from the carrier NCH Fudō located a Xing invasion convoy bound for Ayanami. Junior Admiral Ayaka Kyougoku's cruisers intercepted it, and the fleet repulsed the landing. At the First Battle of the Bay of Serica (March 3–4, 1916), Makinami closed with Zheng Jingtao's fleet by night and engaged it at dawn, inflicting heavy losses without destroying it entirely.

The Aerial Corps directed artillery fire over Mizuhara and bombed the Xing railhead at Tanretsu. From December, Aotaka's squadron supplied the besieged garrison of Harushige by air, dropping food and ammunition in the first such operation in Nichirian service.

#### Harushige Massacre

On March 7, when news of the losses at the Bay of Serica reached Harushige, the Xing garrison posted a proclamation ordering that ten Nichirian civilians be shot for every sailor lost in the battle. The executions were carried out between March 8 and 12. With some 46,000 dead by Bakufu figures, the Harushige Massacre was the largest of the war on Nichirian soil. Ground Corps units invoked it in the reprisals against Xing prisoners that accompanied the counteroffensive later that year.

#### Operation Shōtan

Kitta launched Operation Shōtan (嘗胆, "Tasting Gall") at dawn on March 21, the spring equinox, alluding to King Goujian of the Xian Kingdom, who tasted gall daily until he had avenged his defeat in the Feng-Xian War. It was also a homophone of *shōtan* (焦炭; "coke"), which fueled the Kagatsuchi Furnace. While the Xing positions faced the frozen reaches upstream and downstream, assault boats crossed the open water at the Ironworks under a smoke screen from its coke ovens and retook the southern districts.

The operation ended the Battle of Mizuhara, the costliest of the war. Xing losses came to about 1.55 million killed and wounded, 600,000 of them dead, against about 640,000 Nichirian, including 220,000 dead. Roughly 210,000 of the city's civilians died, and the southern districts were left in ruins, with the torii of a Kaiseido shrine among the few structures still standing. In Evria, the battle for the city is known as "the Radagorsk of the North", after the Battle of Radagorsk, in which Rudanian forces held their capital against converging Alemannian, Skjarnish, and Turulian offensives. After the reverses at Mizuhara, Daikei, and at sea, the Xing began a general retreat along the entire front.
