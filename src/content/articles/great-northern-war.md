The **Great Northern War** (Skjarnish: *Stora Nordiska*; Rudanian: *Северная война*; Routan: *Suuri Pohjan Sota*) was a twelve-year-long major military conflict in Northern Evria that saw the [[rudanian-empire]] succcessfully lead an international coalition to dislodge [[skjarnland]] as the dominant power in the region.

The war, which began in 1751 with a joint Rudanian-[[karjan-confederation|Karjanian]] invasion of the Skjarnish-controlled upper Suebian territories, initially ended in defeat for the anti-Skjarnish coalition. Skjarnland's new ruler, [[gustaf-v|King Gustaf V]], convinced that Rudania and Karjania had lost most of its fighting ability in the campaign and wanting to permanently end the threat posed by Skjarnland's northern neighbours, launched an invasion of Rudania in 1753.

## Background

### Rise of Skjarnland

In 1535, the Kingdom of Skjarnia emerged victorious in the [[skjarnish-wars-of-unification]], proclaiming the Kingdom of Skjarnland following the Treaty of Hävnia. The war saw the Skjarnian military, influenced by strategists such as Count Olof Larsson, utilise tactical innovations including the 'commanded shot', a flexible light infantry formation that proved effective against the Arvernois-influenced heavy infantry formations (the 'troisième') employed by Danyria and the Holy Evrian Empire, the standard unit organisation in Evria during the period. The war solidified the new kingdom's standing as an influential innovator in military science.

Reforms undertaken by [[johan-ii-skjarnland|King Johan II]] and his successors [[vilhelm-i-skjarnland|Vilhelm I]] and [[karl-iv-skjarnland|Karl IV]] throughout the mid-to-late 16th century further solidified Skjarnland's military standing. Skjarnland broke away from the [Ecclessiarchial Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church) during the Reformation, seizing its wealth in the country and declaring Veritism the state religion. The weakening of the noble class allowed Vilhelm I to centralise state power around the monarchy and organise the government into a modern bureaucracy. Military schools, including in Eyrborg and Sundberg, were founded with the aim of professionalising the Skjarnish officer corps.

In 1629, King Gustaf I Fridolf intervened in the [[twenty-five-years-war|Twenty-Five Years' War]], seeking to protect Veritists in the [[holy-evrian-empire]] and secure Suebian sea dominance. Skjarnish military innovation once again proved fruitful, with victories against Ecclessiarchial Forces in Breslach and Erpesfurt using linear tactics that revolutionised warfare, allowing them to conduct campaigns deeper inland. However, Gustaf's forces overextended themselves after the [[bairath-campaign-twenty-five-years-war|Bairath Campaign of 1631-33]], and Skjarnish soldiers resorted to pillaging and looting local areas for supplies. Sensing weakness, Ecclessiarchial forces led by the Arlbernian commander [[adam-von-salzquell|Count Adam von Salzquell]] defeated Skjarnish forces in Linden in 1634 and then Jelgawa in 1635, with Gustaf narrowly surviving. Realising his position was untenable, Gustaf negotiated a withdrawal from the conflict in 1636, culminating in the [[peace-of-prauvenberg]].

The peace secured the territories of Rosthaven and Oderfurt, securing a foothold in Alemannia and ceding Skjarnland control over the mouth of the Oder river, further strengthening its trading position. It also allowed the Skjarnish Army to gain valuable combat experience, the withdrawal having secured a peace without completely destroying its forces in the process. In 1640, Gustaf I would lead a campaign against the Livonian Order to secure Skjarnish trade dominance in the Suebian sea, conquering the Order by 1641 and instating a satellite duchy controlled by an appointed Steinfelt monarch. With Skjarnland's military and economic position as the dominant power in the Suebian secured, its Great Power era began.

### Upper Suebian War

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/IZZjSnd.jpeg" alt="'Failed Relief of Roudanjärvi' by Mattias Johansson (1854) depicts Skjarnish forces inspecting fallen Rudanians in the aftermath of the battle."><figcaption>'Failed Relief of Roudanjärvi' by Mattias Johansson (1854) depicts Skjarnish forces inspecting fallen Rudanians in the aftermath of the battle.</figcaption></figure>

In 1683, in a bid to form a land connection with Skjarnish Livonia and further secure Suebian dominance, King Gustaf II invaded the Karjan Confederation, beginning the [[upper-suebian-war]]. The Tsardom of Rudania, alarmed by the prospect of complete Skjarnish dominance in the Suebian, and already resentful of Skjarnland restricting its trade in the region, entered the war on Karjania's side.

The Artyomov Tsar, Gregory II, attempted to dislodge Skjarnish forces besieging Roudanjärvi in 1684, but were routed due to Gustaf's forces' superior organisation and entrenched defensive positions. The city fell in 1685, and a second Rudanian attempt to fight Skjarnish forces ended in defeat near Toksavaara, but managed to inflict large amounts of casualties on counterattacking Skjarnish forces. Not willing to push his armies further due to limited supplies and mounting attrition, Gustaf made peace with both Karjania and Rudania in the Treaty of Perona, securing the land corridor to Skjarnish Livonia and further solidifying Skjarnland's monopoly on Suebian sea trade.

### Skjarnish Stagnation

Having secured its hegemony in Northern Evria, the Skjarnish Crown turned its attention inward and towards colonial affairs. Within the country, a cultural renaissance spurred by the beginning of the Enlightenment gave rise to the concept of Skjarnish exceptionalism, which viewed the country's military system as the pinnacle of modern warfare and its administration as the gold standard of Evrian bureaucracy. Cronyism within the officer corps, where the families of well-known or lauded commanders of previous wars, such as that of Olof Larsson, received promotions and prestigious assignments more frequently than other officers, began its rise. Additionally, military schools such as the Eyrborg and Sundberg academies, taught decades-old doctrine and was slow to adapt to military developments elsewhere in Evria, such as during the [[war-of-the-three-empires]] (1702-1710) in the southern continent.

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/lufw2cy.jpeg" alt="Duke Mikhail Oderburg of Temnezh, painted in 1719, was the Foreign Chancellor of Rudania and the chief architect of its foreign policy in the early 18th century."><figcaption>Duke Mikhail Oderburg of Temnezh, painted in 1719, was the Foreign Chancellor of Rudania and the chief architect of its foreign policy in the early 18th century.</figcaption></figure>

In Rudania, however, social changes and bitterness from humiliation during the Upper Suebian War, combined with the strategic necessity of gaining a warm-water port in Evria, forced country-wide reform. Under Tsar Gregory III, Rudania invested heavily in building its own military academies on the Skjarnish model, and sent out delegations to Arverne, Elvester and Askurias, all recent combatants in the War of the Three Empires, to learn Evrian models of warfare. The Tsardom halted its focus on northwards expansion following the Fall of Yaqanis in 1693, turning its attention to Evrian affairs. In 1714, Karjania, already naturally Rudanian-aligned due to its resentment and fear of Skjarnland, formalised an alliance in the Treaty of Nowgorod. Increased naval skirmishes between Karjanian and Skjarnish vessels around [[spetsbergen]] heightened tensions and pulled Karjania deeper into the Rudanian orbit.

Tsar Gregory III's Foreign Chancellor, Duke Mikhail Oderburg, began a diplomatic campaign beginning in the 1720s with the goal of isolating Skjarnland. Following the 1725 Riege Revolt in Skjarnish Livonia which saw the Skjarnish garrison open fire on protesting crowds, leading to a massacre of roughly 10,000 Livonians and subsequent executions killing another 1,000, Oderburg began a propaganda campaign portraying Skjarnland as having regressed in morality and contradicting the humanitarian ideals of the ongoing Enlightenment. Additionally, an agreement with [[nordmark]] secured its intent to reclaim the Skjarnish-controlled territories of Rosthaven and Oderfurt, although no formal alliance was agreed upon.

However, Oderburg's approach to several other states, including Cortesia and Elvester, were unsuccessful. Elvester in particular was skeptical of Rudania's ability to confront Skjarnland, following its defeat in the Upper Suebian War forty years prior. Cortesia feared its trade activities in the Suebian sea would be severely disrupted in case of war with Skjarnland. In Skjarnland itself, Rudania's diplomatic strategy was dismissed as futile, the view of Rudanian backwardness and primitiveness prevalent within elite circles, and the continued perception of Skjarnish military superiority causing senior officials to ignore warnings from lower-level bureaucrats of Rudania's diplomatic activity. Despite this, Oderburg opted to wait for Skjarnland to become weakened before encouraging the Tsar to launch a war to end Skjarnland's Suebian hegemony and secure a warm-water port in Evria.

By 1750, Rudania had built up the largest standing army in Evria, fielding roughly 180,000 soldiers, and began constructing a fleet in the Karjanian port of Murmánska, as Rudania lacked other Arctic ports. Skjarnland's military in 1750 fielded roughly 80,000 to 100,000 soldiers and had an established navy that enforced its dominance in the Suebian and around its Arctic coast.

### Ascension of Gustaf V

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/xDIDDae.jpeg" alt="Gustaf V of Skjarnland, painted soon after his inauguration in 1751."><figcaption>Gustaf V of Skjarnland, painted soon after his inauguration in 1751.</figcaption></figure>

On 13 February 1751, King Gustaf IV of Skjarnland died, leaving his eldest son, the 31-year-old intellectual Prince Karl Fridolf Steinfelt, who became Karl V, as Skjarnland's new monarch. After only four months of rule, he abdicated the throne on 20 June 1751 after becoming overwhelmed with his new duties, leaving his younger brother, 28-year-old Prince Gustaf Kristian Steinfelt as the new monarch, King Gustaf V. Gustaf's ascenscion to the throne caused a stir within Skjarnish society, due to its unexpectedness and skepticism around the ability of the new monarch, who was thought to be overshadowed by his elder brother in terms of competence.

It was thought that Gustaf would spend his career in the military, as he had done so from the age of 17 with military schooling done since 9, while his older brother ruled until his death. This left him lacking administrative experience. Thus, while Gustaf settled into his new role, administration was handled primarily by his ministers, appointed under his father's reign. Several important decisions, including gubernatorial and military appointments, were postponed while the government reorganised itself.

In foreign capitals in Evria, these developments were received with interest, especially in Radagorsk. The swift adbication of Karl V drew particular attention, raising questions about why a prince educated for rule since childhood abandoned one of the most powerful thrones in Evria so soon. Rumours about division within the Skjarnish Royal Family and the Skjarnish government emerged, although most were unsubstantiated. Nevertheless, Rudania's Foreign Chancellor Oderburg encouraged the Tsar to begin preparations for a war with Skjarnland, waiting for another definite sign of vulnerability.

In Kjellberg, Gustaf was eager to assert his authority, believing that his higher-ranking subjects had begun to undermine him. After a disagreement with the commander of the Skjarnish Army, Marshal Fredrik Silvrigstierna, about troop placements in the Suebian, Gustaf forced him to retire on 18 September 1751. The commander of the Skjarnish Navy, Grand Admiral Filip Sparre, coincidentally died under a month later on 10 October, temporarily leaving both of the Skjarnish military's most senior posts vacant at nearly the same time.

In Radagorsk, this development was interpreted as a short window of time where the Skjarnish military was paralysed. By this point, Rudania had amassed over 100,000 soldiers who were ready to deploy in combat. On 18 October 1751, after deliberation in the Imperial Cabinet, where Duke Odenburg successfully made the case for war in front of the Tsar, Rudania's diplomats in Kjellberg handed Skjarnland a formal declaration of war.

## First Upper Suebian Campaign

### Invasion of Livonia and Estiland

Tsar Gregory seeked a swift war to seize Skjarnland's territories in the Upper Suebian before its military could respond, most of which had been seized in the 1683-1685 regional war and the prior 1640-41 invasion of Livonia. On 20 October 1751, he led his army, the [[rudanian-army-of-the-south|Army of the South]], comprised of 60,000 men, to defeat Skjarnish Army units stationed in Livonia and their accompanying local garrisons. The Skjarnish military were initially surprised and quickly overrun. Rudanian forces captured Mitau on 24 October after a 3-day-siege which resulted in the Skjarnish commander quickly surrendering his forces, while the garrison commander in Wilna refused such an action, holding out with his garrison of 2,000 men against a besieging Rudanian force of 20,000.

With the main military headquarters in Kjellberg slow to respond, the local Skjarnish ruler in Livonia, [[mattias-i-waldemar|Duke Mattias I Waldemar]], made the decision to avoid any open confrontation with the numerically superior Rudanians and prepare for sieges while waiting for reinforcements from mainland Skjarnland. Against the advice of some of his officers who believed Rudanian forces could be ground down in a pitched defensive battle, he directed the [[skjarnish-army-of-livonia|Army of Livonia]], numbering 20,000, to retreat from digging defensive positions near Kokenhausen, east of Riege, towards the Livonian capital. They would instead prepare their defenses on the city outskirts and walls along the Düna river.

On 23 October 1751, Karjania honoured its alliance with Rudania and declared war on Skjarnland. [[olaf-iii-karjania|King Olaf III]] personally led his army, numbering 30,000, into Skjarnish-controlled Estiland and Sydspetsen, with the goals of besieging Roudanjärvi and severing Skjarnland's land connection to Livonia to delay any reinforcements. Tsar Gregory dispatched a force of 30,000 to assist the Karjanian armies in these objectives. Skjarnland's Army of Estiland, numbering 20,000 and stationed in Toksavaara, was confronted by Rudanian forces approaching from the east and Karjanian forces approaching from the north, in total making up 40,000 men.

Despite the odds, Skjarnish commander Colonel-General [[felix-tomasson]] opted to face his enemies in battle anyways, calculating that he could pull off the same victory in the same location of the 1685 battle that won the Upper Suebian War, and deny enemy forces the chance to link up. He believed that the terrain that favoured the Skjarnish defenders almost 70 years ago still gave them an advantage, and wanted to pursue a propaganda victory. On 30 October, Tomasson's forces met the Rudanian and Karjanian armies of Olaf III outside the city. Despite initially wearing them down, Tomasson chose to commit his centre to an attack, incorrectly sensing that the Karjanian lines were going to break. His forces were quickly caught in a near-encirclement, forcing him to retreat at the cost of almost half of his men.

In Kjellberg, Gustaf V bypassed the standard appointment process and instated Marshal [[count-vincent-hjortland|Vincent Hjortland]] as Commander of the Skjarnish Army, and Grand Admiral [[david-elfsberg]] as Commander of the Navy, sending a contingent of 10,000 men to reinforce Duke Mattias' forces in Riege while his main army, the Grand Army, set out to re-establish Skjarnish control over Estiland.
