The **Era of the Illustrious Three** (Skjarnish: *De Tre Lysande Gestalternas Era*) is a period in [[skjarnland|Skjarnish]] history named for its three monarchs; [[johan-ii|Johan II]], [[vilhelm-i]] and [[karl-iv|Karl IV]]. The era saw the creation of the [[kingdom-of-skjarnland]] following the [[wars-of-skjarnish-unification|unification of the Skjarnish realms]] under Johan II of [[sundberg-union|Skjarnia and Norrvynia]], the adoption of [Veritism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) during the [[reformation]], the [[age-of-consolidation|consolidation of executive power]] under the [[house-of-steinfelt|Steinfelt monarchy]], and the transformation of the government and military to sustain Skjarnland's growing military and political ambition, which became one of the largest and most organised bureaucracies in Evria by the dawn of the 17th century.

The era was defined by centralisation of power under the King of Skjarnland, the weakening of the noble class and the gradual phasing out of feudalism, alongside the height of the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) in Skjarnland. The development of the Skjarnish state and military would lay the foundations of its [[skjarnish-great-power-era|Great Power era]], considered by Skjarnish historians to have begun following the coronation of [[gustaf-i-fridolf]] on 13 March 1601.

## Early Rule of Johan II

Johan II ascended to the throne of the Sundberg Union (the personal union between [[kingdom-of-skjarnia|Skjarnia]] and [[kingdom-of-norrvynia|Norrvynia]] lasting since 1272) on 13 August 1498, succeeding his father [[karl-iii|Karl III]] following his death in June and marking the end of the [[age-of-tyranny]], which saw the Crown become reliant on several competing aristocratic families to govern its territories, collect revenues, and raise troops.

By the time of Johan's coronation, the nobility had become a powerful political force capable of challenging the King under certain situations when unified, but suffered from longstanding rivalries and a reputation among the people for being oppressive. In reality, this image stems from the peak of the Tyranny in the 1470s, with the worst incidents including the [[trosso-bloodbath|Trossö Bloodbath]] perpetrated by a limited amount of nobles. This reputation nevertheless remained strong.

The Sundberg Union remained the dominant trading power in the Suebian, competing with its principal rival [[kingdom-of-danyria|Danyria]], controlled by the [[house-of-hva|Hva dynasty]], which was allied to several states in the [[holy-evrian-empire]] (HEE) including [[duchy-of-brimund|Brimund]] and [[nordmark]], whose main motivations were to counter the Union's power in Northern Evria. Tensions had remained high since the brief [[war-of-the-strait]] in 1484, which saw Union forces successfully land in the [[jylland|Jylland peninsula]], but later suffer defeat and explusion from the peninsula by a combined Brimundian-Danyrian force using Arvernois-inspired [troisième](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tercio) heavy infantry formations, which was notoriously difficult to dislodge using conventional tactics.

Consequently, Johan began funding military development led by [[olof-larsson|Count Olof Larsson]] to counter or adopt the troisième, learning from the lessons of the War of the Strait and observing several conflicts in Evria from 1500-1510, including the [[turulian-wars]], inspired by Turulian forces' use of light infantry units to outmaneuver the heavier and slower Arlbernian formations and inflict losses. Meanwhile in [[stralholm|Strålholm]], Johan undertook several measures to increase royal power, including the cultivation of loyal administrators from several families with good relations with the Crown, the passage of several laws to enable him to collect taxes directly, and the reduction of military dependence on noble houses.

### Vistgarde Boycott

In 1512, the [[vistgarde-incident]], a brief clash between Strålholmer traders and Danyrian merchants of the [[havnia-league|Hävnia League]] over a commercial dispute, escalated into a wider diplomatic crisis. Danyria and several northern HEE states (including Brimund, Nordmark and Aldenburg among other smaller states) began a trade boycott against merchants from Skjarnia, while excluding Norrvynians from the new restrictions in an effort to entice division within the Sundberg Union. The boycott severely disrupted Skjarnian commerce, resulting in a fiscal crisis. Despite concessions offered by Johan, including monetary reparations for killed Danyrians, [[frederik-iv|Frederik IV]] refused to end the boycott, viewing it as an effective means of eroding rival trade influence without resorting to war.

After several years of unsuccessful negotiations, Johan convened his war council in 1516 to prepare for war against Danyria and its allies within the HEE, with the goal of restoring Skjarnian access to restricted Suebian ports, the reduction of Danyrian commercial influence in the region, and the establishment of control over the Hillmer Strait, the crucial strait separating Skjarnia and Danyria and the major body of water connecting the [[suebian-sea]] to the [[north-sea]]. Through war, Johan hoped to permanently remove one of the Sundberg Union's greatest strategic vulnerabilities.

## Wars of Skjarnish Unification

### War for Jylland

In February 1523, the Sundberg Union declared war on Danyria, Johan II's primary objective being the end of the boycott by force. His enemies included Frederik IV of Danyria, alongside Gerhard of Brimund and Paul II of Nordmark, who were longstanding allies of Danyria. The first phase of the war saw the Union attempt to occupy the northern Jylland peninsula rather than mounting a direct assault on the heavily-fortified Danyrian Archipelago (which included the capital
[[havnia|Hävnia]]. Initially, Johan's forces succeeded in dislodging Danyrian garrisons in Lykken and Fladstrand, landing roughly 20,000 troops, but struggled to take [[sverd]]. Initial attempts to bypass the city by fording the Selasund at Skivaren in March failed due to resistance from the local Danyrian army and the inability of Union ships to supply any beachhead via the water.

The beachhead established on Norholm succeeded in repelling Danyrian cavalry counterattacks and allowed Union reinforcements to push deeper inland and link up with a smaller landing force from Als sent from the mainland throughout April, severing the main Danyrian supply route to Sverd. From there the plan was for the southern force to advance further down the peninsula to delay reinforcements from the rest of Danyria and her allies, while the main force in the north encircled and besieged Sverd. However, Danyrian armies in the south managed to bait the Union's southern force into pitched battle in Randershusen on 5th June, ending in Danyrian victory and destroying a large part of the Union force.

Johan II was forced to withdraw his all his forces south of the Selasund, weakening his position around Sverd. With the arrival of Brimundian and Nordmarkian contingents throughout summer, all Union forces withdrew from the peninsula over three months after a costly stalemate at sea (which saw Danyrian and Union carracks fight inconclusive battles).

### Invasion of Skjarnia

Despite defeating the Union, Frederik IV saw the war as a demonstration of the Union's growing power. He assembled a coalition of his allies: Brimund, Nordmark, Aldenburg, and others, for an invasion of mainland Skjarnia to permanently end the threat it posed and ensure the dominance of the Hävnia League, which included guilds and market towns of Danyria's allies). In April 1526, the Danyrian Navy led by Admiral Knud Mikkelsen led a massive campaign in the Suebian with the backing of coalition allies to destroy the Union navy and plunder Skjarnian ports in the Suebian Sea. Outnumbered and outgunned, the Union navy suffered heavy losses and was forced to scatter, giving the coalition naval superiority.

Danyrian forces landed in [[malmhug]] and [[halsingberg|Hälsingberg]] in June, although another landing at [[alvskrona|Älvskrona]] (led by Nordmarkian forces) famously failed due to the actions of the local naval batteries. By the end of the month, a total of 30,000 coalition forces had landed in mainland Skjarnia. The first major land battle at Brunnsberg on 13th July ended in a coalition victory, Count Larsson's use of lighter infantry failing due to improper implementation by unit commanders (used to heavier, traditional formations) and superior organisation of the coalition compared to hastily put-together Union formations. Coalition forces split into two major axes of advance, one besieging Älvskrona while another marched along the eastern coast towards [[trosso|Trossö]], whose outdated walls quickly crumbled against Nordmarkian heavy cannons.

Union forces led by Larsson, who was almost demoted for his failure at Brunnsberg, began spreading out and attacking isolated coalition units, beginning the degradation of coalition supply lines which were dependent on supply bases in occupied Malmhug and Hälsingberg. This forced the coalition to use forces otherwise used for the advance to stay behind and guard supply lines and ports. This allowed a stalemate to emerge in the Battle of Högsby in March 1527. Disagreements within the coalition began to emerge; Frederik IV wanted to advance further and capture the Skjarnian capital Strålholm, while his Alemannian allies including Gerhard of Brimund wanted to consolidate gains, stating that the objective of establishing regional Hävnia

League superiority had been achieved.

Danyrian forces attempted further offensives north between March 1527 and December 1528, many notably without the support of his allies, who became focused on plundering Skjarnian towns for their wealth, without significant success. Johan II, meanwhile, focused on strengthening his military, particularly its gutted navy; in Norrvynia, which had been left relatively untouched by the war due to its distance from the coalition's political objectives and its harsh geography, he ordered the hasty construction of Skjarnish ships.

### Union Counteroffensives

In the Holy Evrian Empire, political upheaval caused by the recent [[reformation]] of Felix Mührter, which began in 1520, continued sowing division among the coalition and making their participation in the war in Skjarnia increasingly untenable. Nordmark withdrew from the war in January 1528, signing a separate peace with the Union, followed by Aldenburg in February 1528. While Brimund and several other smaller states officially remained in the war, by 1529 they had functionally become separate from the Danyrians.

Johan II quickly exploited this weakness in December 1528; Union ships secured a temporary opening in the Suebian Sea, which was used by a light detachment of carracks and jagds to launch a surprise attack on Hävnia, which was ultimately repulsed by the garrison but set fire to a district of the city, causing large amounts of destruction. This became the nail in the coffin for the participation of most of Danyria's other allies, who lost confidence in Danyria's ability to secure its own supply lines and continue the war, withdrawing throughout the first half of 1529. However, Danyrian forces in Skjarnia, numbering roughly 20,000 in June 1529, remained a sizeable threat.

Union forces attacked the Danyrians in the Battle of Brötjemark

on 18th June. Olof Larsson's forces once again faced the troisième formation, personally led by Frederik IV, which the Union had struggled against for decades, but using flexible light infantry he was able to draw the Danyrians into rougher terrain while his cavalry were able to mount hit-and-run attacks against their flanks after baiting Frederik into committing his own cavalry against the main Union force. This slowly compressed the Danyrian army until they no longer had enough space to fight effectively, leading to a slaughter.

Frederik IV narrowly escaped death, although his forces were now severely weakened. Between June and November, he organised a withdrawal of Danyria's forces from mainland Skjarnia. However, throughout late September, the Union Navy managed to conduct a series of surprise landings in the Jylland peninsula after degrading Danyrian navial superiority in the Hillmer Strait, landing 7,000 men, mostly Norrvynians, who occupied the region north of Sverd and the Selasund (the same area briefly conquered during the War for Jylland in 1523). Despite their inability to advance further south due to their small numbers and lack of fording equipment, the invasion succeeded in causing panic within Danyria and a loss of morale within its ranks.

### Invasion of Danyria

Frederik IV refused to sue for peace despite his military situation, believing that repulsing the Union from Jylland and defeating any further invasion attempts could strengthen his position at a future negotiation. However, the Union bridgehead in northern Jylland proved difficult to eliminate, allowing Johan II to reinforce it while Danyria remained unable to recover from its losses in Skjarnia. From December 1529 to April 1530, Union forces, now reinforced, conducted landings across the Selasund, repulsing Danyrian counterattacks and encircling Sverd. Its garrison had been reduced due to the demands of the war in Skjarnia, leading to its fall on 6th April.

The fall of Sverd gave the Union armies the logistical means to advance further south, meeting the Danyrian army in Vibjerg on 20th June, where Frederik IV had concentrated his forces in an attempt to destroy the Union's armies before they could advance further south. However, Olof Larsson had refined his armies' use of light infantry formations since Brötjemark, known as the 'commanded shot'. Danyrian forces were once again outmaneuvered into unfavourable positions and led into mortar and cannon killzones. A final cavalry charge broke the formation Frederik IV was personally leading, causign a rout and destroying Danyria's last field army.

Despite Frederik IV's appeals to his former allies, continued political upheaval in the Holy Evrian Empire and the lack of political will prevented any of them from going to his aid. From 1530-33, Union forces fought a slow and methodical campaign to conquer the Danyrian archipelago, with Hävnia falling in March 1533 after a two-year-long siege. Frederik IV surrendered in April, although the Treaty of Hävnia, which formally ended hostilities and created the [[kingdom-of-skjarnland]], abolishing the Kingdoms of Skjarnia, Norrvynia and Danyria, was not signed until March 1535.
