**Skjarnland** (Skee-arn-land), officially the Republic of Skjarnland, is a country in Northern [[evria]], bordering [[karjania]] and the [[suebian-sea]] to the east, the Arctic Sea to the north, the [[north-sea]] to the west, [[alemannia]] to the south.

As of 1934, it is the 11th most populous nation on the continent and possesses the world's 21st highest GDP, 5th highest real GDP per capita (excluding overseas territories) and has the highest HDI in Evria. Its diverse climate, which includes temperate to arctic conditions, is a result of the country's latitudinal length stretching from the plains of Central Evria to the harsh tundra and cold desert of its northernmost regions.

Skjarnland is a [unitary parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_parliamentary_republic). Skjarnland's ethnicities include all of the [Northern Alemannic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Germanic_peoples) cultures, which form the vast majority of the nation's ethnic makeup (roughly 98%), with indigenous minorities (including the Sjaedrin and Iskarn) and immigrant communities (especially from Alemannia and Cortesia) encompassing the remaining population.

## Etymology

The name for Skjarnland is generally agreed to have originated from the old Skjarn word skjǫldr, meaning shield. The word evolved into skyärn, referring to a shield-bearing warrior, later further evolving to refer to the warrior class prevalent in Skjarnland until the high medieval period. By the 10th century, sources from the Akenauer Empire began collectively referring to all of the region’s inhabitants as Skjarns, with the terminology spreading to the rest of Evria throughout the remaining medieval period.

The term Skjarnia was originally a political rather than geographic designation; following the proclamation of the Kingdom of Skjarnia in the 14th century, the royal administration adopted the term, initially meaning the ‘land of all Skjarns’, to refer to all territories governed under the realm of the crown. This was to distinguish them from neighbouring Norrvynia and Danyria. The modern term Skjarnland emerged following the [[skjarnish-unification]] in the 16th century. Seeking a new term encompassing all the peoples of the region, the new government revived the older ethnogeographic concept of the ‘land of all Skjarns’ to represent the entirety of the new realm including Danyria and Norrvynia. From that point onward Skjarnland became the standard term for the unified state, while Skjarnia was mostly relegated to a geographic term for Skjarnland Proper, the homeland of the [Skjarnian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedes).

## History

### Prehistory

The earliest known settlement in modern-day Skjarnland dates back to approximately the 90th century BCE, when proto-Alemannic and Sjaedric tribes migrated northward from Central Evria through Danyria and westward from Karjania. Archaeological evidence suggests that the first humans to inhabit Skjarnland Proper (also known as Skjarnia) were hunter-gatherer societies in what is now Skjarnland Proper’s southernmost province, [[sydmark]].

Following the conclusion of the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) and the beginning of the warmer [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene) epoch, the lands encompassing modern Skjarnland Proper and Danyria, previously infertile and rigid tundra, gradually warmed into temperate land suitable for permanent settlement by the 70th century BCE. The earliest evidence of agriculture in modern Skjarnland were discovered in Sydmark and Rådjurland in Danyria, dating back to the 60th century BCE. Settlements in the southern regions of Skjarnland were built around fertile river valleys and coastal regions and cultivated livestock and early grains, evolving into complex chieftain-based societies. During the same period, the northern regions closer to the Arctic circle remained inhospitable and unfit for permanent settlements, allowing for hunter-gatherer societies to remain, themselves developing a culture oriented around warrior clans competing for food, where power was centered around martial prowess and the ability to secure wealth and food for one’s followers. Contact between the northern tribes and southern chiefdoms is believed to have been defined largely by trade; northerners exchanged rare furs and meats for sums of grain.

The earliest known written mention of the peoples of Skjarnland by a foreign power was in 106 BCE, when Rumaic historian Ceaparius Vopiscus described them as ‘barbarians from the land north of Alemannia’ in the chronicle Alemannia. Despite the lack of evidence of any direct contact between Skjarnish tribes and the Rumaic Empire, archaeological findings indicate that indirect trade via Alemannian intermediaries almost certainly existed. Rumaic coins and manufactured goods discovered in modern Skjarnland from the 19th century onward and evidence of the existence of walrus ivory and Skjarnish iron in the Rumaic Empire supports this theory.

### Classical Antiquity

Between the 1st and 4th centuries CE, population growth (from roughly 250,000 in 300 BC to 600,000 in 300 AD) and expanding trade networks encouraged the consolidation of chiefdoms into larger political confederations. The Skjarns of southern Skjarnland Proper, Danyrians of the coastal regions and islands, and Norrvynians of the fjords and mountains emerged as distinct cultural groups. Authority remained decentralised and personal, with rulers expected to demonstrate martial prowess, defend trade routes, and secure wealth through commerce or plunder. Alliances between clans were common but often dissolved after their leaders died.

By the end of the 4th century, the first true kingdoms had formed in southern and central Skjarnland Proper and Danyria. Sjöland, Hjalland, and Kroneland dominated trade in the Suebian Sea. [[harald-of-kroneland]] (c. 410-449) is credited with establishing some of the region's earliest market settlements, including [[malmhug]] and [[halsingberg|Hälsingberg]].

Warfare became frequent as competition over trade routes grew. [[jorund-iron-hand|Jorund Iron-Hand of Eldasland]] (c. 483-513) conquered southern and central Norrvynia during a twelve-year campaign, although his territorial gains were reversed after his death. A devout follower of [[asarianism]], the region's polytheistic faith, Jorund also sought to establish common religious institutions. In 503, he ordered the construction of major sanctuaries known as [[hov|hov]], maintained by priests called [[gydir|gydirs]]. These priests received land privileges and met at [[isdalen]] during gatherings known as hovthing to settle theological disputes, establish common rites, and interpret divine judgments.

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:320px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/t8g1dww.jpeg" alt="Thorshov, Vätterna, was constructed sometime between 620 and 633, according to differing contemporary records."><figcaption>Thorshov, Vätterna, was constructed sometime between 620 and 633, according to differing contemporary records.</figcaption></figure>

Although the gydirs remained independent and no formal religious hierarchy emerged, the hov system gradually spread across Skjarnland during the 7th and 8th centuries. It provided rulers with religious legitimacy and connected otherwise independent kingdoms through shared institutions. Gydirs also became involved in diplomacy, with Tangvik of Sadethov negotiating the [[trosso-compromise|Trossö Compromise]] in 658, which established the borders between Hjalland and Kroneland, one of the oldest borders in Evria still in use.

By the 8th century, advances in shipbuilding by Danyrian and Norrvynian sailors produced longboats capable of crossing open seas and navigating rivers rapidly. Their military effectiveness was demonstrated during the [[norrvynian-war]] (c. 710-719), when Eldaslandian longboats proved vital during the [[siege-of-tord]]. The technology soon spread throughout the region.

### Viking Age

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:320px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/pymGaIG.jpeg" alt="The Ludeberg image stone depicts a Viking longboat, dating to approximately 800 to 1000 CE, and remains one of the most well-preserved instances of Viking art."><figcaption>The Ludeberg image stone depicts a Viking longboat, dating to approximately 800 to 1000 CE, and remains one of the most well-preserved instances of Viking art.</figcaption></figure>

In the summer of 731, Chieftain [[rasmus-of-hojlund]] led an expedition to [[northumber]] in [[brant]], seeking plunder after the devastation of Norrvynia during the recent war. His force of around 100 men attacked the Nazarist monastery town of Godschurch, killing more than 200 priests and residents, looting its wealth, and seizing approximately 40 women and children as slaves. Rasmus returned to Stjärnvik with substantial riches. This became the earliest recorded [Viking raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_raid_warfare_and_tactics) and the beginning of what modern historians call the [[age-of-voyages]], although earlier raids likely occurred.

Rasmus' success encouraged further expeditions, initially led by Norrvynian chieftains before attracting landless lords and raiders known as [[farir|farir]], as well as Danyrian rulers. The late 8th-century conquests of the [[akenauer-empire]] disrupted trade between Skjarnland and the rest of Evria, making plunder an attractive alternative. By 800, Brantish states were building coastal defences and attacking Viking outposts, but raiders continued to arrive. Some began overwintering in Brant and eventually settling permanently, taking advantage of the weakening authority of Northumber outside its fortified castles. Vikings also served Brantish rulers as mercenaries, particularly during the Anglian War (c. 808-812).

In 812, Harald Iron-Slayer, a farir and experienced mercenary, demanded land and gold from [[alfred-of-northumber|King Alfred of Northumber]] in exchange for his years of service. Alfred rejected and insulted him. Harald returned to Norrvynia, recruited seven chieftains, and sought further support in Skjarnia and Danyria, including from [[gudfred-i|King Gudfred]] and the senior gydir [[erik-of-tingvallahov]].

In 815, Harald returned with a 7,000-strong army known as the [[great-host]]. Northumber's forces, weakened by the Anglian War and reliant on mercenaries, suffered repeated defeats, culminating in the Battle of Kensfield. The Host subsequently defeated the armies of [[midria]] and [[anglia]] and plundered their territories. Harald distributed the conquered lands among his surviving 6,000 soldiers, who became lords ([jarls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarl)). Their territories formed Faraland, with Harald as its de facto ruler. King Edward of Midria began constructing castles in preparation for reconquest but died in 819 before his plans could succeed.

Faraland soon suffered from internal conflict. With Gudfred's assistance, Harald established the drengathing in 824, a council that met irregularly at Tynmóth to settle disputes and coordinate military campaigns. It could unite the jarls against ambitious rivals, but its authority depended heavily on personal reputations and loyalty. After Harald's death in 831, this weakness became apparent. In 833, Jarl Sigurd of Dyrhamn proclaimed himself King of Faraland (although the title did not legally exist) and attempted to centralise the realm. A coalition led by Jarl Erik Axehand of Joforvik defeated and killed him at the Battle of the Mitt River, ending the [[mitt-war]] but exposing the drengathing's inability to prevent open conflict.

In 836, King Edward II of Midria launched the [[first-reconquest]], initially winning victories against the unprepared jarls. However, his exhausted army was defeated at the Battle of Joforvik. The Treaty of Redford in 837 established the boundary between Midria and Faraland and began the [[redford-peace]], during which major conflict between the two sides ceased. Faraland's jarls turned toward taxation, trade, and agriculture, while migrants from Skjarnia, Danyria, and Norrvynia settled in the realm. Viking communities integrated further into Brantish society, permanently influencing its language and culture. Many Faralandians also converted to [Nazarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity), seeking closer ties and trading advantages with Nazarist Evria.

The last major Viking raids on Evria occurred during the mid-9th century, including the Siege of Lutèce in 851 and the Sack of Puerto Arvory in 858. Stronger Evrian defences, more profitable southern trade routes, and the gradual transformation of raiders into administrators contributed to the end of the age of major Viking raids. Faraland's attempts to conquer the [[dales|Dalish]] in 843 and 851 also failed due to difficult terrain and limited plunder.

King Edmund of Midria launched the [[second-reconquest]] in 897 with a professional army modelled on those of the Akenauer Empire's. After five major wars in thirteen years, he decisively defeated Faraland at the [[battle-of-stalwart-bridge]] in 910, killing Jarl Hakon Redblood. Faraland collapsed, leaving only a few jarldoms in the northeast. The Siege of Tynemouth in 924 ended the last major Viking stronghold in Brant and unified the country under one kingdom.

Meanwhile, the Skjarnian states concentrated more on trade than conquest. Their merchants adapted longboat designs into fast trading vessels and travelled across the Suebian Sea, down the rivers of Alemannia, Cortesia, and Rudania, and sailed past the Acadian Sea, travelling into the [[sahara-sea]] and going as far as [[hollosend]] and [[shivon]]. A smaller amount attempted Arctic voyages, including a convoy that reached what was likely Spetsbergen in 887 or 888. Skjarnish settlements also appeared abroad, including Reykjavík in 865 and Kongshavn in 937. [[ulf-ingvarsson]] established the trading post of Vynmóth near the mouth of the Hudson River in 878, but it was abandoned around 900 following repeated attacks by local Thuleian peoples. Its abandonment is generally considered the end of the Viking Age, although long-distance trade in the Sahara would continue.

### Nazarisation

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:320px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/aOpqOgb.png" alt="Burning of Tingvalla (1846) by Hugo Sandzén depicts the 1013 destruction of the city."><figcaption>Burning of Tingvalla (1846) by Hugo Sandzén depicts the 1013 destruction of the city.</figcaption></figure>

Despite the Faralandians having converted to Nazarism in the lead up to their conquest and assimilation into Brant, the conversion of Skjarnland itself only began around the 10th century. In 918, [[halfdan-the-wise|King Halfdan the Wise]] of Danyria became the first ruler in Skjarnland to be baptised. His conversion is considered by modern historians to have likely intended to secure favour and trading rights with the Akenauer Empire, encourage political marriages with Nazarist states, and strengthen his authority through the faith's doctrine of divine right, which offered a more organised alternative to the decentralised Asarian religious institutions.

Many subjects resisted the adoption of a foreign religion that appeared incompatible with their warrior culture and threatened the autonomy of dependent lords. The [[jylland-rebellion|Jylland rebellion]] of 925, led by Jarl Olof of Sverd, occurred in response to Halfdan's conversion and was crushed by his forces. Missionaries often promoted Nazarism in a more warrior-like form, as detailed in the chronicles of Alemannian missions, while many communities continued secretly worshipping Asarian deities alongside Christ, an arrangement often pragmatically tolerated by rulers.

The greatest instance of anti-Nazarist resistance was the [[asarian-war]]. In 1012, the influential gydir Asmund of Tingvallahov organised an uprising in which King Mikael of Boehus was captured and publicly beheaded. A coalition of Nazarist rulers led by King Valdemar III of Danyria crushed the rebellion. Asarian peasant levies were defeated by combined Nazarist professional armies in the Battle of Älvskrona in 1013. The war ended with the Slaughter of Tingvalla, Asmund's execution by burning, and the installation of Mikael's son Erik V.

With papal endorsement, Valdemar ordered the dissolution of the hov system. During the following century, a period known as the [[demonteringen]] (dismantlement), many sanctuaries became churches or monasteries, while others were destroyed. The gydir class disappeared through natural deaths and conversion to the Nazarist clergy. By the 12th century, most of Skjarnland had converted, although Asarianism survived in remote northern communities, particularly in Norrvynia and Oppsala.

The war established Danyria as the dominant political force in Skjarnland. Its victory weakened Boehus, its principal trading rival, and allowed Danyrian merchants to dominate Suebian commerce. The period from 1013 to 1130 became known as the [[danyrian-century]]. Danyrian rulers also expanded their influence through marriage and conquest, bringing several southern coastal duchies and parts of southeastern Norrvynia under their control. These territories became known as the Danyrkusten, or Danyrian Coast.

Nazarist campaigns against remaining pagan states continued in the north. The [[first-oppsala-war]] began in 1064 and conquered Oppsala, renamed Eyrborg in 1070, but an advance into Lappnia failed because of harsh weather, poor supplies, and ambushes. Norrvynia's campaigns between 1089 and 1103 also failed to subdue the Asarian and Iskarn peoples north of Nidaros. After repeated military failures, the [Ecclessiarchial Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church) shifted toward missionary work, leaving the religious frontier roughly between Nidaros and Eyrborg.

### Three Crowns

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:320px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/O0DgD7T.jpeg" alt="12th century Alemannian drawing of the Battle of Varla in 1127 depicting Boehusite forces fighting Brimundian knights."><figcaption>12th century Alemannian drawing of the Battle of Varla in 1127 depicting Boehusite forces fighting Brimundian knights.</figcaption></figure>

Skjarnia's fertile farmland, growing population, and position between Danyrian and Rudanian trade routes allowed its kingdoms to challenge Danyria. During the [[skjarnian-wars]] (1095-1114), Boehus supported a major rebellion of the vassals of Sydmark, then conquered them after their victory had weakened them. The struggle culminated in the Suebian War (1125-1130), when King Knut of Boehus defeated King Erik III of Danyria despite Danyria's alliance with the Duchy of Brimund and the [[livonian-order]]. The Treaty of Malmhug in 1130 transferred the Danyrkusten to Boehus and marked the end of the Danyrian Century.

Three major powers now dominated Skjarnland: wealthy maritime Danyria, populous Boehus, and mountainous Norrvynia, whose difficult terrain supported a capable army and navy. In 1148, Knut attempted to subjugate Norrvynia and captured its capital, [[nimdallr]], after a year-long siege. However, he withdrew in 1150 after supplies dwindled and King Olaf II continued resisting from mountain strongholds, supported by Danyrian-funded mercenaries. The Treaty of Sundberg in 1151 established the border between the kingdoms and began a period of peace between the three states that lasted until 1270 known as the [[sundberg-peace]].

During this peace, Nazarist rulers resumed campaigns against the remaining pagan states. The Lappnia Crusade began in 1163 under Boehusite leadership, supported by the Livonian Order. Although the Lappnians initially resisted fiercely, the crusaders established fortified positions and secured supplies across the Suebian Sea. In 1165, King Brúni of Lappnia agreed to baptism and military cooperation in exchange for recognition as king and the withdrawal of crusading forces from most of his territory. With Nazarist assistance, he subdued the remaining Asarian states in Skjarnia by 1175.

The three kingdoms also strengthened their administrations and infrastructure. In Boehus, King Kristoff I approved the expansion of coastal towns to encourage trade. [[stralholm|Strålholm]], built on an island at the mouth of the Strål River, became an important Suebian trading centre after its completion in 1210, reaching an estimated population of 10,000 by 1250. Kristoff also introduced the Code of the Dominion, one of Skjarnland's first comprehensive legal systems. By the mid-13th century, the ruling dynasties of the three kingdoms had intermarried for decades, creating competing hereditary claims.

A series of failed harvests and severe winters caused famine in 1256, followed by the [[great-pestilence]] of 1259-1261, which killed an estimated 500,000 to 750,000 people in Skjarnland. Norrvynia in particular suffered the worst among the three powers, losing agricultural centres and fisheries and seeing much of its royal Skjaldar dynasty disappear. When King Harald III died without legitimate heirs in 1267, both the Boehusite [[house-of-steinfelt]] and Danyrian [[house-of-hva]] claimed his throne, beginning the [[war-of-the-three-crowns]] (the third crown being the Norrvynian House of Guldar).

King Johan I of Boehus marched toward [[nimdallr]] to install Duke Sven of Malmhug, while King Albert I of Danyria landed troops at Stjärnvik to support Prince Mattias. The Norrvynian nobility divided between the Svenites and Mattites. Among them, Duke Olaf of Borgund rebelled and led a third faction seeking an independent Norrvynia. However, they lacked the resources for open warfare, thus resorting to guerrilla attacks.

The war initially favoured Danyria. The Mattites won the First Battle of Hathdal in 1268, while Danyrian forces used manoeuvres and supply advantages to exhaust the Boehusites. Danyria's fleet defeated the Boehusite navy at Hlymrströnd, establishing control over the strait and trapping the Boehusite navy in the Suebian. In 1269, Danyrian mercenaries and naval forces attacked the southern Boehusite coast, destroying supply hubs and forcing Johan to divert troops to defend his ports. Meanwhile, Albert installed Mattias as king in Nimdallr and campaigned against Olaf's forces. However, he was killed by an arrow in 1271 while campaigning against Olaf's guerillas.

Albert's death caused a Danyrian succession crisis and weakened coordination with the Mattites. One of Albert's commanders, Duke Abel of Druskivik, fearing betrayal, arrested and executed prominent Mattite nobles in the [[sundberg-bloodbath]]. The killings alienated Norrvynians and drove Olaf into an alliance with Johan. In autumn 1271, Johan surprised the poorly organised Danyrian army outside Hathdal and won the Second Battle of Hathdal. After a brief siege of Nimdallr, Mattias surrendered, allowing Duke Sven to ascend to the throne of Norrvynia.

The exhausted kingdoms signed the Treaty of Kjellberg in 1272. Danyria recognised Sven I Steinfelt as Norrvynia's legitimate ruler and withdrew its forces from Norrvynian and Boehusite territory. In return, Boehus recognised Danyrian commercial privileges in the Suebian Sea and renounced its claims to Danyrian islands. Duke Olaf was pardoned and retained his lands. Although the treaty formally preserved the independence of all three kingdoms, it established Boehus as Skjarnland's foremost power and created a lasting dynastic union between Boehus and Norrvynia, the [[sundberg-union]].

## Military

*Main article:* [[skjarnish-armed-forces]]

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:320px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/DmyrKZG.jpeg" alt="Skjarnish Army infantry during military exercises, c. 1933"><figcaption>Skjarnish Army infantry during military exercises, c. 1933</figcaption></figure>

The Skjarnish Armed Forces, or Krigsmakten, is the national armed service of Skjarnland. As of 1934, it operates roughly 500,000 personnel and operates a policy of universal conscription for all males turning 20, with certain medical and physical exemptions. The Armed Forces are divided into three branches; the [[army|Royal Army]], [[navy|Royal Navy]] and [[air-force|Royal Air Force]], with military operations led by the General Staff, while administration is handled by the Ministry of Defence. Under the constitution, the Sovereign of Skjarnland (currently King Mikael II) is the commander-in-chief, but following the 1934 New Republic Act, such powers will be transferred to the currently uncreated position of President of Skjarnland when it comes into force on January 1, 1935.

The history of the Skjarnish Armed Forces dates back to the formation of the Kingdom of Skjarnia in the 14th century, but most existing formal traditions date back to the [[great-power-era|Great Power era]] of the 16th to 18th centuries, which represented Skjarnish military power at its height. Following the defeat against the [[rudanian-empire]] in the Great Northern War, however, the Skjarnish military gradually evolved to adopt a primarily defensive doctrine until the lead up to the [[great-war|Great War]], where it began. However, following the defeat on the Northern Front, the military once again prioritised defensive capabilities as the country adopted a foreign policy of neutrality. Additionally, the lessons of the Great War prompted an overhaul of the Armed Forces, led by General Anton Stjernberg, in the 1920s, in order to remove aristocratic influence in the officer corps and change unit doctrine.

## Government

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/LRCqKm2.jpeg" alt="Joakim Helgi, Head of the Skjarnish Communist Party, is the country's first president, serving since 1 January 1935."><figcaption>Joakim Helgi, Head of the Skjarnish Communist Party, is the country's first president, serving since 1 January 1935.</figcaption></figure>

### Constitutional Framework

Skjarnland is currently a [unitary parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_parliamentary_republic) with a [unicameral parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicameralism), the [[riksdag]]. The country has two fundamental laws, which together make up the Constitution (Grundlag): the Instrument of Government (1768), which outlines the structure of government and civil liberties, and the Freedom of the Press Act (1836), which governs transparency and free speech laws.

The President is the head of state, elected by popular vote once every 8 years and serving a maximum of two terms. They are responsible for chairing Special Councils during a change of Government and receives letters of credence from foreign ambassadors, while signing those of Skjarnish ambassadors. Additionally, the President has the power to conduct state visits and is the commander-in-chief of the [[skjarnish-armed-forces|Armed Forces]]. Under Article 34 of the constitution, during a state of emergency, the President has the power to rule by decree until the expiration of the emergency.

The Prime Minister serves as the head of government. Following a general election, the Speaker of the Riksdag nominates a candidate (typically recommended by the largest party), which is then subject to a parliamentary vote, requiring a simple majority to be elected. When they are elected by the Riksdag, their election is confirmed by the president, which is typically a ceremonial process, following the preceding royal tradition. In the event that parliament rejects the Speaker’s nomination for Prime Minister four times, a snap election will automatically be held. The Prime Minister has the authority to call for snap elections between the regular four-year cycle, appoint and dismiss cabinet ministers and government body directors at his discretion, and submit bills to the Riksdag, among other powers.

### Legislation and Elections

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:320px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/VST0dF1.png" alt="Riksdaghuset, Kjellberg, 1925"><figcaption>Riksdaghuset, Kjellberg, 1925</figcaption></figure>

Legislative power is vested in the unicameral Riksdag, which contains 442 seats. As outlined in the constitution, the Riksdag meets annually. Sessions are opened by the Speaker in the Riksdag hall itself, who delivers the program of the government during a speech from his throne in front of the gathered members of parliament and government. This is typically done after consultation with the President and Prime Minister. The sessions are chaired by the Speaker or one of the three Deputy Speakers, who are required to refrain from participating in political debate to maintain impartiality. Bills may be submitted by the Government or a Member of the Riksdag, and must pass a vote of simple majority (more than 50%, or 222 seats) and then be signed by the president, to be adopted.

General elections occur every four years and are officially called by the Prime Minister. They, however, also have the power to call for a snap election between the four-year cycle, with the approval of the President. Members of the Riksdag are elected on a basis of party-list [proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_representation). Municipal and Regional (County) elections occur once every four years, and elect representatives to their respective Councils.

To vote in any election in Skjarnland one must be a registered citizen of 18 or more years of age. Certain situations may revoke one’s right to vote, including the conviction of crimes or recognition of insanity by the state. Foreigners may be granted voting rights for municipal and regional elections after two years of residency in the municipality/region they are applying for voting rights in, with written approval from their respective government, which reserves the right to reject their application without explanation. Women were granted the right to vote following the 1919 Voting Act.

### Administrative Subdivisions

Skjarnland is divided into 35 counties (*Län* or *Regioner*), 715 municipalities (*Kommuner*), and 6 territories (*Territorier*). Counties, also called regions (especially in the context of their governments) are the highest level of administrative subdivision and are responsible for healthcare and public transport. Municipalities are the lowest level of government subdivision and administer education (up to the end of high school), public water utilities and foster care. While parishes are technically lower than municipalities, with over 4,000 total in the country, this is an administrative division used by the Church of Skjarnland and not officially recognised by the government, although it plays an important role during censuses.

Municipal and regional elections occur in conjunction with general elections, and like them, also follow a policy of party-list proportional representation. Their governments are similar to a cabinet-style ministerial government, led by the Chair of the Municipal/Regional Executive Committee.

Territories have a broad legal scope under the Constitution, a designation given to all overseas dependencies that are neither colonies (of which Skjarnland officially has none today) nor integral counties. While residents of territories are nominally Skjarnish citizens with the right to participate in presidential and general elections, they do not have the ability to elect local representatives, with the governor appointed by the President (previously the King under the monarchy). The [[vikomte-islands]] remains the sole exception.

## Economy

Skjarnland is an industrialised high-income welfare capitalist state. With a GDP PPP of roughly $1.87 trillion, it is the 22nd largest economy in Avium and the 8th largest economy in Evria, accounting for 1.1% of global GDP. It has a GDP per capita of $78,746 (excluding overseas territories, including Nordstjarneo, which has the world's highest GDP per capita at $155,000 when ranked among overseas territories), the 4th highest globally and the highest in Evria. Skjarnland is an export-oriented mixed economy, with most jobs concentrated in the secondary sector.

Shipbuilding, agro-industrial machine-building and automotive manufacturing constitute the largest sectors of Skjarnish industry. However, produce from the primary sector continues to dominate exports; paper, timber, and iron ore, alongside meats including bacon have consistently remained the largest exports since 1919, with the largest markets being Elvester, Arverne, the E.S.U., and Hollosend. Specialised metals including ferro-alloys and aluminium make up smaller exports. While Skjarnland has a growing automobile industry, it primarily serves the domestic market, and exports are limited. Livonia is currently the largest foreign customer for Skjarnish cars.

### Infrastructure

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/718brdE.jpeg" alt="Traffic jam in downtown Strålholm, 1926"><figcaption>Traffic jam in downtown Strålholm, 1926</figcaption></figure>

Skjarnland's infrastructure is mainly train-centric at the national level, car and carriage-centric at the county level, and foot, car/carriage and tram-centric at the city level. However, Skjarnish auto infrastructure is steadily expanding under public works programs initiated by the current socialist-led government to combat unemployment following its rise under the Edvinsson government of the late 1920s and early 1930s. Today there are an estimated 180,000 km of roads across all of Skjarnland, with future plans to expand car infrastructure between cities (including a proposed intercounty highway system to be built throughout the 1930s and 1940s). Skjarnish rail and tram lines are all owned by the state, under Skjarnish State Railways (Statens Järnvägar), while the auto industry is highly privatised, with companies such as Gustafsberg Vagnfabrik AB (GV) and Borealis Motor AB accounting for a large share of the Skjarnish auto market.

Southern and Central Skjarnland Proper, Southern Norrvynia, and Danyria have the most developed infrastructure in the country due to their geographical advantage (relatively flat terrain) and population concentration. Skjarnia and Danyria are connected by the Hlymrströndbron, which finished construction in 1927 and is the longest bridge in the country, with a 7,485 m span. The Danyrian Archipelago (Danyröarna) is also connected by a series of bridges, some of which are currently under construction. These include the second-longest Skjarnish bridge, the Stora Bältbron (Great Belt Bridge) with a 6,790 m length, connecting the islands of Själland and Fyn. The other regions of Skjarnland, including Central and Northern Norrvynia and Northern Skjarnland are relatively undeveloped due to their low populations. However, due to military construction dating back to the late 19th century, much of these regions are part of the military railway network used extensively in the Great War, and is still in use today.

### Public Policy

Skjarnland is a highly-developed welfare state, with public social spending as high as 23.4% of its GDP. This is used to fund pensions, free education including in college and university, and provide benefits to families and veterans among other programs. This policy began in 1924 under the government of Social Democrat Dag Söderberg, who championed the ideology of 'folkhemmet' (the people's home), envisioning Skjarnland as one 'family' whose members should all have their needs accommodated with, rejecting both total capitalism and socialist revolution through violent means as demonstrated in Rudania and Karjania. Although the policy was partially reversed in 1927 under Leif Edvinsson's Conservative government in an effort to mitigate debt, it has returned under the current socialist government of Göran Malmros.

Skjarnland has generally followed a policy of free-trade, and domestically, supported strong labour laws and regulations following the major reforms of the early 1920s. The rise of trade unions, supported by left-wing political organisations including those in parliament, also took place in this period as a result of high rates of workplace incidents resulting in injury or death during the early phases of the postwar recovery period. Unions represent roughly 52% of the workforce and have demonstrated the ability to not only force companies to yield to demands, but governments, as demonstrated during the 1934 political crisis.

### Science and Technology

The beginning of the Enlightenment in the 18th century saw the start of the Skjarnish scientific revolution. Previously, Skjarnland's advancements in technology were defined primarily by adopting developments spreading from the rest of Evria. In 1774, the Royal Institute of Science was founded, with early members including Göran Brännström and Adam Hermansson, who would conduct important work in the fields of molecular science and meteorology respectively. During the [[age-of-progress]] in the late 19th and early 20th century, heavy investment in science under [[johan-iii|King Johan III]], who sought to turn Skjarnland into a major global scientific player, contributed towards several major advances in the fields of physics, chemistry and engineering. For example, in 1866, [[adrian-nordahl]] patented the first design for dynamite and would later allocate the majority of his wealth towards commissioning the Nordahl Prize in his will, the first prize given in 1901, five years after his 1896 death.

Science, especially the natural sciences and engineering, continue to receive high levels of attention in Skjarnland. Current well-renowned Skjarnish scientists include [[anders-bjerg]], the founder of the Institute for Theoretical Physics in Hävnia, Adrian Degerlund, the winner of the 1924 Nordahl Prize for his contributions to X-ray spectroscopy, and Aksel Vindheim, the author of the [Vindheim-Kaasa theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaluza%E2%80%93Klein_theory) which proposed higher dimensions in the universe, and the [Vindheim-Nakase formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein%E2%80%93Nishina_formula) in electron physics.

Skjarnland is situated in Northern Evria and has a total land area (mainland only, including its Elysian territory of Helluland, which Skjarnland considers part of its core provinces) of 1,328,981 km², making it the 18th largest country in the world, and the largest country in Evria. The country borders [[karjania]] to the north and [[alemannia]] to the south by land, and shares maritime borders with [[livonia]], [[cortesia]], [[elvester]], [[rudania]] (via Spetsbergen) and [[salishia]] (via Adelsköldland). Its geography is heavily varied. It is surrounded by the Suebian Sea to the south, the North Sea to the southwest and the Arctic Sea to the north, and is home to roughly 1,700 named fjords (the vast majority in Norrvynia), giving it a coastline among the longest in the world. Official figures from the Skjarnish Geographical Society record its length at around 73,000 km, but measurements vary significantly due to the [Coastline Paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastline_paradox).

While southern Skjarnland (including Skjarnland Proper and Danyria) are defined by a [temperate climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate), the northern regions, including central and northern Norrvynia and parts of northern Skjarnland Proper, have a [subarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic) and [Arctic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_the_Arctic). Additionally, parts of the Skjarnish mountain range contain the [alpine tundra climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_tundra). Temperature varies greatly throughout the year; during summer, it can regularly reach around 20-25°C with 19 hours of daylight, while during winters it can reach -15 to -20°C with 5-7 hours of daylight. However, these are averaged figures across the whole country, and the difference between Skjarnland's southernmost and northernmost regions can be extreme. The hottest temperature ever recorded in Skjarnland was 38.0°C in Ultuna, Oppsala in 1933, while the coldest was -50.3°C in Helluland in 1889.

### Language

The official language of Skjarnland is [Skjarnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_language), a northern Alemannic language very closely related to [Norrvynian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language) and [Danyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language), which have been recognised minority languages since the 1884 Minorities Act. Skjarnish is the dominant language, with the 1930 census recording that 92% of the population could speak at a conversational level at the least, with 81% identifying it as their primary language. This is partly owed to the government pushing Skjarnish as the sole official language in administration, schooling and the military since the Skjarnish Unification, especially during the Skjarnification policies enacted in the early 19th century despite cultural and intellectual resistance. Additionally, the similarity in the languages made this policy considerably easier than its application towards the Iskarn and Sjaedrin minorities in the north.

[Alemannian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) is the most widely-spoken foreign language in Skjarnland, with 31% of Skjarns reporting in the 1930 census that they could speak it in at least a conversational capacity, largely due to linguistic similarities and the introduction of Alemannian as a compulsory subject in several natural science subjects in schools and universities, a policy dating back as early as 1824. Immigration from Alemannia also influences this figure to a lesser extent. Fluency in the language rose after the Great War due to Skjarnland’s close cooperation with the Holy Evrian Empire. Other foreign languages are seldom spoken, the next most spoken one being Cortese (<1%), owing to immigration.

### Religion

Before the 12th century, Skjarnland predominantly followed the pagan Asarian faith, before the country’s [[conversion-to-nazarism|conversion to Nazarism]] beginning in the 10th century. Skjarnland is overwhelmingly [Veritist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), with 91% of the population identifying themselves as such according to the 1930 census, a result of conversion in the 16th century following the Mührteran Reformation. After the conclusion of the Twenty-Five Years' War in 1641, tolerance towards religious minorities, including Ecclessarchians, other Nazarist denominations, and Hasmenoean communities in southern Skjarnland was practiced, although non-Nazarists often received second-class citizen treatment until the religious liberalisation of the 19th century.

The [[church-of-skjarnland]] is the primary religious institution in Skjarnland, tracing back its roots to the Nazarisation of Danyria in the 10th century. In 1922 the Social Democrat-led government successfully passed laws to democratise the Church into a 'folk church' (folkkryka) and partially separate it from the state without outright dismantling it. While the Conservative led government of the late 1920s and early 1930s managed to legally preserve the Church's official capacity, following the [[1934-political-crisis]] and the dissolution of the monarchy, the Church was formally separated from the state and is today an independent organisation.
