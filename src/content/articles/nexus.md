**Nexus** is the capital city of the [[elysian-state-of-unity|Elysian State of Unity]]. Located at the end of the Aquila Valley and along the Patawomeck river, Nexus stands as the Unity State's 4th-largest city in terms of population and GDP, with an urban population of 3,52 million as of 1935 and a GDP PPP of 
$414.95 billion, standing as an important hub of commerce and travel for the Elysian Eastern seaboard. It acts as the southern demographic and geographic center of the Northeast Megalopolis.

A city with a long history dating back to around 3400 BCE, Nexus has acted as an important regional settlement since the arrival of human settlers in the area, continuing on well into the Middle Ages, when its importance waned following the decline of the Triumvirate period. The city would only gain a renewed status of importance following the end of the [[elysian-revolutionary-war]], when it was ultimately chosen to act as the capital city for the nascent [[united-states-of-elysia]], a status it would hold from then on. The city is divided into 14 different boroughs, all converging at the Hall of Congress. The city is one of Elysia's few Special Administrative Zones, overseen by a regional Governor appointed by Congress, just as every other region in the Unity State. The city acts as an important hub of Elysian and International politics, home to several foreign embassies and consulates, the current *de facto* headquarters of the 3rd Internationale, as well as the city's Capitol district, home to the Hall of Congress, Protectorate Palace, and many State departments and ministries.

A city wrought with strife and history, the city boasts a varying set of architectural styles, from the few remaining Rumaic-era buildings still standing, to more grandiose Astriyan-Rumaic style government offices and buildings, to the newer post-civil war neighborhoods that dot many corners of the city. Much like many other Elysian cities, greenery is abundant in many parts of the city, with rooftop greenhouses being a common sight in most residential areas. Nexus stands as an important transport hub for the Saharan seaboard, with the Nexus Central Railway station processing over a million passengers daily.

## Etymology

Nexus directly translates to " connection " from Rumaic. The city's name came from its importance as a transit and trade hub for the Territoria Elysiaca's Southern regions. However, while its meaning has been established, its origin is much more nebulous. The earliest known mention of a settlement at the end of the Aquila Valley by the name of Nexus traces itself back to a Gubernatorial decree from 45 CE, regarding official trade licences enabling the sale and distribution of dyes to and from the city. Despite this, archeological evidence indicates Rumaic settlement in the area starting from at least 40 BCE.

## History

### Prehistory

Settlement in Nexus dates back to the earliest signs of human settlement in the area, with the earliest archeological records indicating the first permanent settlement to have been around 3400 BCE. This settlement, founded by the Thram tribe of native Elysians, is known by oral tradition only as **Kainai**, or "*Many Chiefs*" in Brantish. While predating widespread agricultural tradition in Northwestern Elysia by around 1500 years, Kainai, even as a hunter-gatherer society, is estimated to have been home to around ten thousand permanent residents by its pre-agricultural peak, with expansions branching out to support around sixty thousand during its absolute peak. The city's location along the Patawomack River made it a prime candidate for agriculture, with the Valley's geography providing it with ample protection from any outside threats as well. Archeological evidence indicates Kainai to have been an important trade and legislative hub continentally, but also internationally, with Thalassan silver coins and metallurgy being excavated from many locations in the Aquila Valley. This suggests a degree of wealth in comparison to most other contemporary settlements in its vicinity.

### Rumaic settlement and rule (40 BCE - 1582 CE)

Being a population hub close to the Saharan coast, Rumaic colonizers were quick to stake their claim over Kainai and the rest of the Aquila Valley. A series of quick conflicts were fought between the Rumaic settlers and native Thram, ultimately ending with a peace treaty permitting the local Thram populations to practice their traditions and culture as members of the Empire. Following some decades of restructuring and renovation of thousands-of-years-old areas of the city, Kainai would regain its place as an important trade and logistics hub in the region by 50 CE, including a change of name to better reflect its status: Nexus.

The city would undergo its first boom period, rising to a population of around 120 thousand by 250 CE. The city would act as an important center of rule in the Territoria Elysiaca's southern regions, acting as a base of operations for an entire legion within its walls, providing security to all workers and traders operating in the nearby area. This prosperity would continue until 476, when the fall of the Rumaic Empire diminished Nexus' status as an important trade hub. With trade to Evria diminished drastically, merchants had no choice but to begin selling their wares at a loss. While most luxury wares disappeared from Nexus' markets, regular resources required to maintain the Territoria were still in great demand. This forced a drastic shift in the economic landscape of the Valley, transforming Nexus into a hub of extraction industry. Bricks, stone, and timber were all prevalent trade goods within the Territoria, gradually shifting to metalworks and luxuries as the situation following the fall of the Empire stabilized.

Despite this, Nexus would see a gradual decline from its status as a trade hub, with the Triumvirate wars devastating the city greatly, leading to its population dropping to as low as ten thousand by the 7th century. Following the ultimate splintering of the former polity of Elysia, Nexus would fall under the tenuous control of local wealthy landowners, often too busy bickering among themselves to focus on improving the city's conditions. As a consequence of this, most of the city's infrastructure and less inhabited areas were left unattended, with some Elvesterian settlers reporting some buildings having been left in a state of devastation from invasions by rival polities for over 300 years.
