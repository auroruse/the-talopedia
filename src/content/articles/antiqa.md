**Antiqa** (an-tee-kuh), officially the **Godly Republic of Antiqa,** is a country in southern Elysia. It is bordered by Aurtania to the north, Iriria to the west, the Sea of Auritania to the east, and to the south and west the Western Panthalassan Ocean. Antiqa is a geographically diverse country, with habitats ranging from arid plains of the coastal region in the south and east, to the peaks of the Andes extending from the north to the south of the country, to the rainforests and jungles that cover the north. Antiqa has a population of over 34 million, and its capital and largest city is Nawasqa. With a land area of 624,896 km<sup>2</sup>, Antiqa is the 38th largest country in the world.

Antiqan territory was home to several cultures during the ancient and medieval periods, and traces its heritage back to the 9th millennium BCE Lacha Lawpi civilisation, which marked the beginning of civilisation in Antiqa and flourished from the 5th to 2nd centuries BCE. There are many notable succeeding cultures from before the common era, including the Lamaqa culture, the Quswe and the Chaqalaq. The most notable succeeding cultures since then have been the First and Second Kingdoms of the Sun, the Chinkayurqu Confederation and the Kingdom of the Allpa Rakiq. Higher education began in the era of the Second Kingdom of the Sun with the King Waqori University in 1614 in Nawasqa.

In the early 17th century political and religious instability gripped the Second Kingdom of the Sun, culminating in the 1616 coup and resulting civil war known as the Wrestle of Rights in which the kingdom split in the two, the north remaining in royal hands whilst the south was handed over to the newly established Republic of Antiqa. Due to the campaigns of the famous Republican generals Tupaq Yupanqui and Amaru Cusi, the republic almost won the civil war until Askurian intervention in favour of the Second Kingdom forced the war into a stalemate. The peace was signed in November of 1617, a year and seven months after the initial coup, leading to the largest non-monarchical state ever in Antiqan territory at the time. In the following years, both the Republic and Second Kingdom grew more stable, though as the republic began to flourish economically, the Second Kingdom was still bound to the Askurian trade deal, though with less production and fewer workers the Kingdom’s economy struggled.

In 1637, the Kingdom, pressured by Askurias, took a more hostile approach to relations with the republic and relations worsened after the journey of proselytisers from the Republic into the Kingdom to spread their form of the faith. Tensions deteriorated gradually over the period of 1637 to 1659, culminating in the 1659 Antiqan Border Incident that led to war and fighting along the border. However, after a week of tense negotiations, the week-long war was ultimately ended and the ensuing peace deal agreed to the cessation of future hostilities, after which relations slowly improved. This repairing of relations was a contributing factor to the unification in 1702, when the two countries came together, forming the Confederation of Antiqa to present a unified front against potential Askurian aggression. As part of a deal in 1702, many coastal ports were handed over to Askurias, who did not approve of the confederation. Over the period in which the confederation was existent, republican influence grew alongside anti-monarchist sentiment, leading to the abdication of Machiputi IIX in 1799 and the annexation of the Second Kingdom by the Republic. In 1821, seizing upon the opportunity granted by the Auritanian War of Independence, Antiqa recaptured the ports granted to Askurias in 1702. The remainder of the 19th century marked economic expansion and rapid industrialisation as well as the explosion in popularity of football, which became the most popular sport in the country.

Today, Antiqa is a highly religious unitary semi-presidential republic where the political landscape is often corrupt with an imbalance of power between the largest and oldest parties and the newer and liberal ones. The country can be described as an Anocracy. It is a developing country with a poor life expectancy and literacy rates due to lack of modern equipment and sanitary techniques, though improving these metrics is important for the Canul government. It wrestles with increasing religious instability between the more traditional north and the more modern south, which has been causing problems for the government since 1921 when protests in favour of a more theocratic government began. Though these protests were eventually quelled by 1923, the instability caused by them still poses a threat to the country.

Since the late 1800s, the illegal drug trade in Antiqa has been increasing rapidly in size ever since it was banned in 1889, with roughly 400 tons of coca leaf derived products being illegally produced and exported annually. Until the Canul government, the drug gangs had extensive influence in politics and provided a source of revenue for the government, though their influence has been decreasing since 1920. The size and power of the illegal drug trade in Antiqa poses a large problem for Canul’s government, who declared in their 1932 election campaign that the elimination of the drug trade was a priority.
