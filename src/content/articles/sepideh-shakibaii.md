**Sepideh Shakibaii** (Mehrāni: سپیده شکیبایی; born 4 April 1910) is a [[varahmehr|Varahmehri]] footballer who plays as a left midfielder for [[dynamo-mizuhara]] in the Nichirin League and as an attacking midfielder for the Varahmehr national team. Originally a forward, he is widely regarded as the most accomplished player produced by Varahmehri football since the sport was introduced to the country in 1929.

Shakibaii spent his domestic career with [[sepah-kuhestan|Sepāh Kuhestan]], whom he joined at the club's foundation, and was the leading scorer in the 1932-33 [[liga-ye-melli|Liga-ye Mellī]] with nineteen goals. He shared the Golden Boot at the 1932 Foundation Cup and recorded his first perfect match rating against [[tonkin]] during the same competition. He has never won a major domestic honor.

## Early Life

Shakibaii was born in [[dorudgar]], an inland oasis city in the Ghehooh district of [[shushestan|Shushestan]], to a family about whom little is publicly known. He has a younger brother, [[bijan|Bijan Shakibaii]], born in 1912, who is also a professional footballer.

In 1923, aged thirteen, Shakibaii left the family home with his brother, then eleven. The two travelled to [[kuhestan]] without adult company, stowing away aboard a freight train carrying dates from the Dorudgar groves to the city markets. Neither brother has spoken publicly about the circumstances of their departure, and no member of the family has been named in the Varahmehri press.

Both found work in the [[ahanestan|Ahanestan]] smelting industry, where labour was available to the young. Shakibaii was employed at the No. 3 foundry in Kuhestan for approximately six years. At some point in 1927 or 1928 the brothers became estranged, and Bijan left Kuhestan for [[rahgozar|Rāhgozar]], in [[azadestan|Azadestan-e Kuchak]]. They have not been publicly reconciled.

Shakibaii had no contact with organised football before November 1929, when the Majles-e Mellī [[end-of-isolationism|voted to end fifty-one years of isolationism]]. Sepāh Kuhestan was founded the following month in the canteen of the No.3 foundry, by a smelters' mutual-aid association of which he was a member. He was nineteen years old.

Dorudgar's own club, Sarsabz Dorudgar, was founded in February 1930, six years after Shakibaii had left the city. He has never played for them.

## Personal Life

Shakibaii is unmarried and has no children. In interviews he has attributed this to the demands of his career, and he is not known to maintain a residence in Varahmehr outside the playing season.

His estrangement from his brother has attracted persistent press attention, particularly during the four seasons in which the two played in the same division. Correspondents covering fixtures between Sepāh Kuhestan and Āzādī Rāhgozar reported repeated approaches by Shakibaii before and after matches, none of which were acknowledged. Neither brother has commented on the matter publicly.

Shakibaii's appearance has been the subject of frequent comment in the Varahmehri press, and his likeness has been used in federation promotional material since 1933 - an unusual distinction in a country whose sporting culture is less than a decade old.

## Club Career

### Sepāh Kuhestan (1929-1933)

Shakibaii was among the original members of Sepāh Kuhestan, formed in December 1929 by the smelters' mutual-aid association at the No.3 foundry. He played as a forward throughout his time at the club.

Individual statistics were not systematically recorded during the inaugural 1929-30 Liga-ye Mellī season, and no account of his first competitive goal survives. He does not appear in the published leaderboards for goals, assists, or match rating for the 1930-31 season. His earliest documented goal came in the 1930-31 Jām-e Mellī final, which Sepāh lost 5-2 to Pas Nourabad.

His first sustained domestic return came n 1931-32, when he finished second in the league scoring charts and third by average match rating. Sepāh finished the season without a trophy; the club was eliminated 4-1 by Daryā Shushabad in the cup semi-final, a match in which Shakibaii was rated 6.9.

The 1932-33 season was the most productive of his career. He won the Golden Boot with nineteen goals - of the sixty-two Sepāh scored - took the league's highest average rating at 7.8, and led the division in chances created with seventy-seven. Sepāh finished second, three points behind Pas Nourabad, and were eliminated from the cup in their opening match.

His final appearances for the club came at the 1933 [[club-world-cup]], with his transfer not yet completed. He scored two of Sepāh's four goals across six group matches. The club did not advance.

Shakibaii spent four seasons at Sepāh Kuhestan and did not win a domestic honor.

### Toyama Thunderbolts (1933-1934)

Shakibaii joined Toyama Thunderbolts of the Nichirin League following the 1932-33 season, in an exchange that brought six players to Sepāh. The move put him in the same team with Pedram Nazeri, who had transferred to the same club a year earlier.

He scored eighteen goals in thirty-four appearances in the 1934 Nichirin League One season, placing ninth in the division's scoring charts, with an average rating of 7.0 and forty-four defensive contributions. Toyama were relegated. The club made a single appearance in the Shogun Cup, losing its opening tie.

### Dynamo Mizuhara (1934-)

Following Toyama's relegation, Shakibaii and Nazeri both transferred to Dynamo Mizuhara. Squad places had become available at the club after an explosion in its dressing room earlier that year, in which four players were killed.

He is registered at Dynamo as a left midfielder.

## International Career

Shakibaii has been a member of the Varahmehr national side since its formation. Statistical records are complete only from the 1932 World Cup onward; appearances and goals before that date are not systematically documented.

### Early appearances (1931-1932)

His earliest documented international return came in qualification for the 1931 World Cup, where he scored three goals in five matches, placing third in the conference scoring charts and sixth by average rating at 7.4. Varahmehr advanced to the tournament and were eliminated in the group stage without scoring.

In friendlies later that year he scored twice against Shivon in a 3-0 win, taking a match rating of 9.1.

Qualification for the 1932 World Cup was achieved narrowly and Shakibaii did not feature in any statistical leaderboard. At the tournament itself Varahmehr were again eliminated in the group stage, finishing third; his single goal came in a 4-2 defeat to Karjania.

### 1932 PFA Championship

Shakibaii recorded no leaderboard placing in the group stage beyond a share of second for assists. His form in the knockout rounds marked a change in his standing.

Against Albinya he scored the opening goal of the first leg and, in the second, an equalising goal that took the aggregate from 3-2 down to 3-3 and forced extra time; Varahmehr progressed on penalties. He provided two assists in the second leg against Kinshu, rated 8.0, and scored in the final. Varahmehr finished second.

### 1932 Foundation Cup

The competition - later renamed the Nations League - produced the run on which his international reputation principally rests.

Varahmehr lost their opening match 2-1 to Côte de Saumon, Shakibaii scoring, and entered the lower bracket one defeat from elimination. Across the subsequent eight matches he scored the equaliser against Morozia that forced a shootout, rated 9.2; scored against Divia, rated 8.6; opened the scoring against Ponurvia; and recorded a brace and an assist against Tonkin from eleven chances created, taking a match rating of 10.0.

He was less prominent in several of the tournament's most celebrated results, rated 6.4 in the 1-0 win over Karjania, 7.0 against Alemannia and 6.8 in the victory over the Elysian State of Unity, a match in which Touraj Hayaii was rated 10.0. Varahmehr were eliminated by Seignid and finished fourth. Shakibaii shared the Golden Boot with Nichirin's Itoshi on six goals.

### 1933

Varahmehr qualified seventh for the 1933 World Cup from a combined PFA-VAFC conference, an experimental format not repeated. Shakibaii scored four of the side's thirteen qualifying goals without placing in any leaderboard. At the tournament Varahmehr again failed to progress from the group; he did not score but finished second for chances created.

At the 1933 PFA Championship he took the tournament's highest average rating at 7.7, scored four goals and created twenty-six chances. Varahmehr were eliminated in the quarter-finals.

In the 1933 Nations League he scored three goals across two legs against Morozia, including a brace in the second, and scored in the first leg against Rudania. He scored in the second leg of the victory over the Elysian State of Unity and again in defeat to Reino, which ended Varahmehr's involvement.

### 1934 World Cup

Shakibaii was the highest-rated player of the group stage, averaging 8.4 across three matches, with ratings of 8.8, 8.4, and 7.9 and two goals. Varahmehr won the group ahead of Karjania and Vicily.

He sustained an injury in the round-of-16 win over Salishia, rated 6.5, and did not feature again. Varahmehr were eliminated 5-1 by the Elysian State of Unity in the quarter-final. The E.S.U went on to win the tournament.

### 1934 PFA Championship

He recorded six goals, four assists, twenty-eight chances created and forty-one defensive contributions across eleven matches, at an average rating of 7.8 - among the highest in the competition. Varahmehr were eliminated by Selmira on penalties in the lower bracket.

### 1934 Nations League

Shakibaii was named the tournament's most valuable player, having played nine matches to a maximum of five by any higher-rated player. He finished first for chances created with twenty-three, added three goals and four assists, and recorded fifty-four defensive contributions.

He scored in the 2-2 draw with Elvester that Varahmehr won on penalties, rated 8.6; scored twice and assisted once in a 5-1 win over East Kaukasos, rated 9.2; and assisted in the 3-1 victory over the Elysian State of Unity, rated 8.8. He was rated 5.9 and booked in the 4-0 win over Selmira. Varahmehr were eliminated on penalties by Nichirin, who won the competition.

### 1935 qualification

In qualification for the 1935 World Cup, Shakibaii scored no goals across ten matches but recorded two assists, twenty-two chances created and fifty-two defensive contributions, at an average rating of 7.4. Varahmehr qualified sixth from the Eastern Conference.

## Style of Play

Shakibaii began his career as a forward and was deployed as such throughout his time at Sepāh Kuhestan and in the national side's early formations. Following the adoption of a 4-2-3-1 by Varahmehr he was moved into the attacking midfield band, where he has since operated principally from the left.

The transition is reflected in his recorded output. Between 1931 and 1933 his returns were primarily in goals; from 1934 his statistical profile is dominated by chance creation and defensive contribution, the latter unusually high for an attacking player. He recorded no goals in the 1935 qualifying campaign while creating twenty-two chances and registering fifty-two defensive contributions.

His performances are characterised by marked variance. He holds the highest single-match rating recorded in Varahmehri football shared with Hayaii, and has been rated above 9.0 on four occasions, but has also been rated below 7.0 in several of the national side's most significant results.

## Honors

### Varahmehr

- PFA Championship runner-up: 1932
- Foundation Cup fourth place: 1932

### Individual

- Foundation Cup Golden Boot: 1932 *(shared with Itoshi)*
- Nations League Most Valuable Player: 1934
- Liga-ye Mellī top scorer: 1932-33
- Liga-ye Mellī Most Valuable Player: 1932-33
- PFA Championship Most Valuable Player: 1933

Shakibaii has not won a league title, domestic cup or international tournament.

## Legacy

His career coincides precisely with the existence of organised football in Varahmehr. He was nineteen and employed in the Kuhestan smelting industry when the sport was introduced following the end of isolationism in November 1929, and had no prior contact with it. Within three years he had shared a Golden Boot at a global competition; within five he was named the most valuable player of a Nations League.

Assessments on his career commonly note the disparity between his individual record and his lack of team honors. He spent four seasons at Sepāh Kuhestan without winning a trophy, finishing second in the league in 1932-33; the club has not won a major competition in its history. He has reached one international final, the 1932 PFA Championship, which Varahmehr lost.

His statistical profile is also noted for its inconsistency. He has been rated above 9.0 on four occasions and below 7.0 in several of the national side's most significant victories, including the 1932 wins over Karjania and the Elysian State of Unity. He has never captained Varahmehr.

Shakibaii's move to the Nichirin League in 1933 was the second transfer of a Varahmehri international abroad, following Pedram Nazeri, and left the domestic game without its highest-rated player. As of 1935 he has not returned.
