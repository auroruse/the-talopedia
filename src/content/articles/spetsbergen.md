Spetsbergen is an unincorporated territory of [[skjarnland]] in [[evria]] located in the [[arctic-sea]] containing six main islands. It borders by land the [[karjania|Karjanian]] territory of [[cuohpajalla|Cuohpajálla]] only, on the archipelago's largest island, Östspetsbergen (East Spetsbergen), and shares maritime borders with [[rudania]]. The island is also home to the only permanently-inhabited settlement of the Skjarnish side, Långdalshamn, housing the vast majority of the territory's population.

## Etymology

Spetsbergen is derived from the Skjarnish words *spets* (meaning 'peak' or 'summit' and *bergen* (meaning 'mountains'). The name was given to the archipelago when the Norrvynian explorer, Kare Trulsson, observed its mountainous features. While politically it is the name of the Skjarnish-owned Territory only, the name is also a geographic term in Skjarnland used to describe the entire archipelago, including Cuohpajálla.

## History

### Viking Discovery

The islands are believed to have been first discovered by Evrians in the 9th century, during the [Skjarnish Viking Age](https://auroruse.github.io/the-talopedia/skjarnland/#viking-age). The Nylland chronicles, a compilation of stories about Viking trade voyages to the North during the 8th and 9th centuries, describes a merchant convoy from Norrvynia encountering a severe storm while attempting a trade journey to modern-day Western Rudania, and becoming briefly stranded on a barren, white land where they encountered 'white bears' (strongly believed to be [polar bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_bear)) who attacked them; modern historians believe that the chronicle describes one of the islands of the archipelago, possibly Östspetsbergen or Uusromaasuolot, based on their likely route and the fact that polar bears do not inhabit other nearby regions. According to the chronicle, the merchants managed to slay a bear after a struggle which killed two of their men, consumed its meats to survive, and returned its carcass home as a trophy and a gift to the Eldaslandian jarl after building a makeshift ship from the remains of their shipwrecked longboat to escape.

Records of Skjarnish trade with the Iskarn and Sjaedrin peoples of the North become sparse after the 10th century, which historians attribute to the falling profitability of northern trade and simultaneous rising southern trade with the rest of Evria and the Sahara Sea civilisations. As a result, no Evrian would again visit the islands, which were left uncharted, for another six centuries.

### Re-discovery

In the late 15th century, following war around the [[acadian-sea]] region which disrupted traditional trade routes to the Sahara, the Skjarnian Crown developed an interest in finding alternative trade routes to the Sahara and elsewhere via the Arctic Sea, through a hypothetical route known as the Northern Passage. In 1473, the Norrvynian captain Kare Trulsson was contracted by the Crown to explore the Arctic and discover a new route to the Sahara. On 12 April 1474, following the receding of ice, Trulsson's crew set out from [[nimdallr]] on board the *Jacobine Baardson*, a kravel (Skjarnish carrack) alongside two others, carrying a total of 152 men.

On 26 April, after two weeks of sailing north, Trulsson's men sighted land not found on any charts. After several hours of sailing along the coast to find a suitable landing area, he made landfall with a party of 12, immediately noting the cold temperatures despite the summer and the mountainous landscape. In his logs, he describes the landscape as being 'destitute and barren', and after two more days of mapping the coasts of the island, in which Trulsson also observed visible coal deposits, large pods of whales and polar bears, the decision to withdraw and continue sailing north to find the Northern Route was made. About a month later, Trulsson gave the land the name 'Spetsbergen' (peak mountains).

On 21 May 1474, Trulsson crossed the Kagyrgyn Strait and sailed south alongside the Elysian coast, making landfall on an island 50 km from the Elysian mainland on 2 June, which he named Margaretasö (Margareta's Island) in honour of the Queen of Skjarnia Margareta Karlsdotter. Today the island is the northernmost island of the [[nordstjarneo|Nordstjärneö Territory]]. After spending one week on the island and conducting trade with the native peoples, Trulsson began the journey home to Nimdallr due to dwindling supplies and a scurvy outbreak on one of his ships, arriving by 19 July with 106 men left.

### Early Whaling and Settlement

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/VzJmF7J.png" alt="1616 map of Spetsbergen by Askurian cartographer Javier Alacanta."><figcaption>1616 map of Spetsbergen by Askurian cartographer Javier Alacanta.</figcaption></figure>

Trulsson's expedition succeeded in finding a route to the [[western-panthalassan-ocean]], but did not find any new route to the Sahara Sea or other civilisation. Despite this, news of abundant whales and walruses around the newly-discovered Spetsbergen generated interest in the region. In 1513, Brantish explorer William Anselm led an expedition to the islands with seven ships, successfully hunting a dozen walruses and a [bowhead whale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowhead_whale). From then on, Spetsbergen gradually became a lucrative whaling and walrus-hunting area, with hunters from [[elvester]], Skjarnland, Karjania, the [[holy-evrian-empire]], [[askurias]], [[rudania]] and [[portal-arverne|Arverne]] arriving in modest but increasing numbers, some establishing temporary camps on land. However, overwintering among Evrians was not popular due to the extremely cold temperatures experienced during the winter and isolation due to sea ice; this was thus initially practiced primarily by seasoned Saamit explorers from the Karjan Confederation, who established the first settlement on the island of Uusromaasuolut in 1529.

Throughout the 16th century, Skjarnish whalers and hunters (primarily Norrvynians), who themselves hired Sjaedrin (the Skjarnish term for Saamit) explorers, began overwintering more frequently in Southern Östspetsbergen, establishing the first Skjarnish settlement in southern Östspetsbergen. Meanwhile, the Karjanians settled mainly on the northwestern islands. Despite a large multinational presence persisting around the archipelago, Skjarnland and Karjania emerged as the primary powers competing for dominance over the archipelago, with undeclared hostilities becoming frequent by the end of the century. This encompassed firing warning shots at hunters in 'claimed hunting grounds' to armed ships driving whalers away and in some cases directly firing upon them. In 1613, the Skjarnish Crown formally claimed Östspetsbergen and Vestspetsbergen as Crown Territories. Three years later, in 1616, Karjania claimed the five northwestern islands. Despite high tensions, no formal war was declared over the islands, with hostilities remaining unofficial. This status quo remained intact for 300 years until the [[great-war|Great War]].

### Coal Mining and Great War

Coal on the islands had been known since Trulsson's discovery in 1474 and until the 19th century used exclusively locally. However, following the beginning of the Industrial Revolution and the rise in coal prices towards the end of the century, coal mining on the islands became a lucrative business and began in earnest. The first coal mine was opened by Skjarnland near Långdalshamn (which became the primary settlement in the archipelago) in 1871, with Karjania's first mine opening in 1879. In 1903, the Skjarnish government merged the territories of Östspetsbergen and Vestspetsbergen as a single, non-self-governing administrative entity ruled by a Crown-appointed governor, who ruled on Kjellberg's behalf. By 1910, the island was home to several major mines producing 40,000-50,000 tonnes of coal annually.

In August 1914, Skjarnland joined the Great War by launching a large-scale offensive against Karjania, succeeding in capturing large swathes of territories in Karjania's west but ultimately stalling due to Rudanian reinforcements and logistical exhaustion. In January 1915, the Karjanian General Staff, with assistance from their Rudanian allies, began planning an operation to seize the entire archipelago and deprive Skjarnland of an important coal source and Arctic outpost. The Elvesterian and Rudanian navies gradually grinded down their Skjarnish and Holy Evrian adversaries in the Arctic Sea throughout 1915, weakening Skjarnland's ability to send and receive supplies from Spetsbergen. Karjania therefore finalised planning for the operation and amassed a force of roughly 1,000 men of the 1st Marine Division, led by Major-General [[miro-toivanen]] by September 1915 to attempt the operation.

Before September 1915, the archipelago remained in its status quo due to its remoteness and the inability of each country's garrisons to launch any attack on the other, owing to geography (their territories were separated by sea) and poor logistics. On 3 September 1915, Karjania launched Operation Karhu (Bear), landing an initial wave of 200 men of the 1st Marine Regiment in Isviken, the second largest settlement in the archipelago, located on the main island of Östspetsbergen, taking it after a brief firefight with the 30-men-strong Skjarnish garrison which promptly surrendered. Karjanian planners initially considered a direct naval assault on Långdalshamn but concluded it would pose too great of a risk due to Skjarnish naval forces remaining active around its waters. Therefore, they opted to land the entire force near Isviken between 3-4 September during this period they seized the local mines and the town's telegraph lines, while sending reconnaissance elements towards Långdalshamn.

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/OWhTF6a.png" alt="Skjarnish soldier in his defensive position east of Långdalshamn, winter 1916."><figcaption>Skjarnish soldier in his defensive position east of Långdalshamn, winter 1916.</figcaption></figure>

News of the landing reached mainland Skjarnland and Långdalshamn before the cutting of the telegraph lines. The Skjarnish General Staff directed the garrison in Långdalshamn led by Colonel [[astrid-holmstrom|Astrid Holmström]], 300-strong and reinforced by armed miners and personnel from the naval garrison, to prepare defences and introduce rationing, anticipating a siege. Karjanian forces crossed Eastern Östspetsbergen, the main force arriving outside Långdalshamn on 6 September. After conducting a series of brief probing attacks, Toivanen concluded that a direct frontal assault would eventually succeed but at unacceptable cost, due to the Skjarnish garrison's entrenched positions and fire support from the coastal batteries and naval vessels anchored in the harbour. He thus opted to lay siege to the settlement.

Between September 1915 and May 1916, Karjanian forces conducted limited probing attacks and artillery bombardment on Långdalshamn while securing coal mines around the settlement. Skjarnish forces attempted resupply by sea but on several occasions were driven away by Rudanian and Elvesterian squadrons helping to maintain the blockade after short skirmishes. However, with the help of naval escorts from Skjarnland and the Holy Evrian Empire, some resupply convoys, who also carried reinforcements managed to get through.

On 30 May 1916, Skjarnish forces, now numbering 900, launched a surprise counteroffensive on the eastern trenchline, managing to advance roughly 4 km before heavy resistance and poor weather stalled momentum. The Skjarnish advance continued slowly along the coastline towards Isviken over several weeks, characterised by limited attacks supported by naval gunfire from the local garrison. Despite casualties remaining comparatively light by continental standards, the extreme weather and supply shortages on both sides made territorial progress difficult. By August, Karjanian forces withdrew from several forward positions but re-established their lines west of Långdalshamn, successfully defending against repeated Skjarnish attacks. Furthermore, the mountainous inland terrain made any sort of flanking maneuver impossible. limiting warfare to the coast.

Recognising that taking the entire archipelago was not a practical objective anymore, Major-General Toivanen opted to prioritise the defence of his current holdings on Östspetsbergen, constructing extensive trench systems and artillery emplacements along the line. Activity significantly dropped off during the winter of 1916-17, where temperatures plummeted below -30°C and maritime resupply was made difficult due to sea ice. The majority of casualties during not only this period, but in the entire campaign were from frostbite, disease and exposure rather than combat.

In March 1917, the Skjarnish command authorised one final attempt to retake Isviken, beginning on the 17th. No further reinforcements would be sent as they would be needed on the main front in Evria. Skjarnish forces initially succeeded in dislodging the Karjanians from several defensive positions, but the offensive stalled long before reaching the settlement. The lines stabilised once more by 3 April, and no more attempt by either side to bring a decisive end to the campaign was made.

The 1918 armistice effectively ceded the eastern half of Östspetsbergen to Karjania, including Isviken (renamed Jiekŋaluokta) and its coal mine, the largest in the archipelago, the largest territorial gain by Karjania in the war.

### Present Day

Spetsbergen remains an unincorporated territory of Skjarnland, despite being given the same administrative pretitle as the Vikomte Islands, which are internally self-governing. The term Territory (Territoriet) in Skjarnland is a broad constitutional category that encompasses all overseas dependencies that are neither colonies nor integral counties. Recent years has seen a rise in Arctic tourism, although coal mining and whaling remain the primary activities around Spetsbergen today.

Spetsbergen has a land area of 23,913 km² and a population density of 0.13 / km², making it the 59th largest nation or territory on Avium and the least dense. The Territory consists of six major islands and dozens of smaller islets; the largest one, Östspetsbergen, contains the only permanently-inhabited settlement, Långdalshamn, and nearly all coal mines. Research and meteorological stations exist on other islands including Vestspetsbergen and William Anselm Land (or Vilhelm-Anselm-Land in Skjarnish), the second and third-largest islands respectively, but these are only partially staffed throughout the year.

Continuous erosion as a result of repeated ice ages have given the archipelago its distinctive mountainous landscape, along with abundant glaciers and snowfields, which cover more than half of the Territory's area. The southern and western coasts of both Östspetsbergen and Vestspetsbergen are home to numerous fjords, also formed from erosion caused by ice ages. Elsewhere, however, extensive coastal plains exist.

Spetsbergen has an Arctic climate. Winters can become extremely cold, with temperatures frequently dipping as low as -40°C. The lowest temperature ever recorded on the Territory was -49.7°C in northern Östspetsbergen in July 1916. During summers, temperatures are mild, especially in lowland regions including Långdalshamn, averaging 3-7°C. The highest recorded temperature was 13.1°C in February 1924.

## Economy

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/UcMncaK.jpeg" alt="Långdalshamn, 1931"><figcaption>Långdalshamn, 1931</figcaption></figure>

Spetsbergen has a GDP PPP of $245,000,000 (72nd in the world when ranked among nations and territories) and a GDP per capita of $81,667 (5th in the world), according to the 1930 census. The census also records it as having a HDI of 0.859, a life expectancy of 61.2 years, and a literacy rate of 98.9%, making it the world leader in all three categories.

These statistics stem from the fact that the small population of 3,000 is almost entirely concentrated in the capital of Långdalshamn, whose economy is dominated by highly productive coal mining, whaling and fishing operations alongside shipping services. Its population is dominated by miners, engineers, sailors and administrators alongside their families, giving the Territory its high literacy rate, while healthcare is heavily subsidised by the government, allowing for a relatively high life expectancy and HDI.

## Demographics

Spetsbergen has a majority Skjarnian population, the ethnic group making up 68.3% of the population. The other Alemannic Skjarns, the Norrvynians and Danyrians, form most of the remaining minority, at 15.9% and 5.3% respectively. Other groups, including Sjaedrins and Iskarns, alongside foreign ethnicities form a smaller but still significant segment at 10.5%. The foreign population in Spetsbergen, including Alemannians and Elvesterians, are composed primarily of mining and industrial expertise, forming one of the most significant foreign populations in a Skjarnish Territory.
