End of Isolationism (Mehrāni: پایان انزوا, *Pāyān-e Enzavā;* lit. *End of Seclusion*) refers to the vote of the Majles-e Mellī on 23 November 1929, by which the [[varahmehr|Federal Republic of Varahmehr]] resolved to resume diplomatic and commercial relations with foreign states after fifty-one years without them. The resolution passed 48-9. It ended a policy that had governed the federation since shortly after the [[ruz-e-peyman|signing of the Peymān-e Mellī on 3 March 1878]], and it is conventionally treated as the opening of the republic's second era. Within a month of the vote Varahmehr had received its first foreign embassy proposal, entered [[nichirin-oil-deal|negotiations over oil development]] with a state on the other side of the world, and authorized the construction of an air service it did not yet possess. Within five years it had [[operation-monsoon|fought in a coalition war]], [[the-southern-question|acquired a desert territory]] larger than any of its provinces, and [[council-of-free-nabataean-states|signed a charter]] binding it to states it had not known existed in 1928.

## Background

Isolation was never required by the Federal Compact. No article prohibited foreign relations, and the delegations of 1878 made no decision to withdraw from the world. The policy arose from the administrative condition of the new federation. The signing settled the principle of union and almost nothing else: the location of the capital, the distribution of the treasury, the construction of a judiciary, a legislature and a civil service, and the reconciliation of six separate bodies of law were all left to be worked out afterward, and they consumed the federation's entire capacity for a generation. A government that could not yet collect its own revenue had nothing to offer a foreign ministry and no one to send.

What began as incapacity hardened into habit and then into doctrine. By the 1890s the absence of foreign relations was being defended on its merits - as the condition of the federation's survival, and as a guarantee against the fate visible across the northern border, where the Ymirians had established the colony of [[divia]] whose existence had frightened the Six Lands into union in the first place. When the [[great-war|Great War]] came, Varahmehr took no side, was asked by none, and emerged with its institutions intact and its isolation vindicated in the view of most of the Assembly.

The cost was paid elsewhere. By 1929 the federation recorded a literacy rate of 6.4%, a life expectancy of 32.1 years, and no operational aircraft of any kind. Its naval capacity consisted of Shushestani merchant vessels with minimal armed escort. Its industries at [[kuhestan]] and [[ahanshahr|Āhanshahr]] had grown for fifty years without a competitor and had improved accordingly. Oil had been confirmed in the southern interior in quantities the federation had neither the capital nor the machinery to exploit. The republic was, by its own internal measures, wealthy and, by any external measure, a country that had not been examined in half a century.

## The Ambush of 1929

On 17 February 1929 the federal frigate *Sahar* (سحر, lit. *Dawn*), on a routine patrol of the approaches to the Daria Bay, was fired upon in international waters by vessels she was unable to identify. Nine of her crew were killed. The *Sahar* broke contact and reached Bandar Mehr under her own power two days later.

No attacker was ever identified. The Great Staff's inquiry established that the federation possessed no means of determining who had fired, no vessel capable of pursuing, and no diplomatic channel through which to protest to anyone. The report, which was not published at the time, concluded that the incident could be neither answered nor explained, and that this was the predictable consequence of a naval force built entirely for coastal defence and a foreign service that did not exist.

The political effect was out of proportion to the military one. Nine dead sailors and a damaged frigate were a small matter beside the recognition that the republic had been struck and had no address to which to send a complaint. The Morning Kestrel returned to the incident repeatedly through the spring. By summer the question before the Assembly had shifted from whether isolation was wise to whether it was still available.

## The Debate

Formal debate occupied the Assembly for most of the autumn session. The four factions divided in ways that did not follow their ordinary alignments.

The [[peyman-daran|Peymān-dārān]], led by Chancellor Nozar Shariati, argued for resumption on constitutional rather than commercial grounds: that a federation unable to defend its citizens abroad or answer for them was failing an obligation the Compact imposed, and that the protection of Varahmehri persons and property did not stop at the shoreline. The faction's Shushestani wing pressed the commercial case in parallel, and the resolution was introduced by Shapur Davoudi, a delegate from Mosaghat, whose constituency contained most of the federation's shipping.

The [[mardom-e-kar|Mardom-e Kār]] divided. Its [[ahanestan|Ahanestan]] delegates were unwilling to expose the foundries to foreign competition without protection for wages and conditions; its Farzānestani members supported opening almost without reservation, on the argument that fifty-one years of intellectual seclusion had cost the academies more than any tariff could. The faction ultimately voted with the government after securing an undertaking on labor provisions in any future commercial treaty.

The [[risheh-daran|Risheh-dārān]] secured an agricultural protection clause in the resolution's second section before the vote and said little in the chamber, which was widely understood at the time as the price of their support.

The opposition came from the [[azadu-khahan|Āzādi-khāhān]], which called publicly for the vote to be cancelled. The position was a reversal of the faction's ordinary instincts and was noted as such at the time. Its argument was that the federation's institutions were too weak to survive contact - that a republic which opened its ports would find its constitutional protections traded away piece by piece in treaties negotiated by ministers and ratified before anyone could read them, and that Article Nine in particular would not survive a foreign power's displeasure. The faction lost the vote and has been vindicated on several particulars since.

## The Vote

The resolution was put to the Majles-e Nāmāyandagān on 23 November 1929 and carried 48-9. The nine votes against comprised the six Āzādi-khāhān delegates and three members of the Risheh-dārān who broke with their faction's arrangement. No delegate abstained.

The resolution instructed the Chancellor's office to receive and answer foreign communications, authorized the negotiation of commercial agreements subject to ratification by the Assembly, and made no provision whatsoever for the diplomatic service required to do either. That omission shaped the following two years, in which the federation's foreign policy was conducted by a cabinet improvising with a communications bureau.

## Immediate Aftermath

The Morning Kestrel's account of 24 November recorded that "the people are going out into the streets to celebrate", a reaction the paper noted was not obviously proportionate to a procedural resolution about correspondence. The celebrations were largest in [[shushabad]] and [[rahgozar|Rāhgozar]], the two cities with the most to gain, and were muted in the agricultural interior.

Events then movd faster than the Assembly had anticipated. The [[shivon|Order of Shivon]]'s initial telegram arrived within days of the vote and was answered on 1 December. The Peymān-e Shukufāyi-ye Varahmehr-Shivon was ratified on 6 December by 36-21 - a margin far narrower than the resolution that had made it possible, and the first evidence that the federation had agreed to open without agreeing on what it was opening to. An inquiry to [[nichirin]] regarding oil development opened on 7 December.

On 13 December the Assembly sat in emergency session over the civil war in [[sidanya]], to which Varahmehr had committed humanitarian aid that could only be delivered by sea because the federation had no aircraft. The [[hava-ye-melli|Hava-ye Mellī]] was upgraded from a research division to an operational development program by 33-24, with an eighteen-month mandate under Director [[behruz-sepehr]]. A classified review of defence doctrined followed on 20 December, recommending a revision of the federation's posture from territorial defense to what it termed extended reach.

## Consequences

The five years since have realized both the case for the resolution and the case against it.

The Hava-ye Mellī achieved its first powered flight on 14 February 1931 at [[golestan]], lasting seventeen minutes on an engine built at Āhanshahr. The Nichirin concession brought capital and machinery into the southern fields under production-sharing terms with mandatory skills transfer and reversion clauses, and oil revenue has since become the federation's fastest-growing source of income. The survey of the southern corridor that followed brought the federation into contact with the desert communities of the Banu Dakhl and the Ahrar al-Raml, produced an advisory opinion of the Dīvān-e Adl requiring consultation and consent before demarcation through inhabited land, and ended in the accession of Al-Sahrā as a ham-peymān territory, adding more land to the republic than the resolution's supporters had imagined existed.

In November 1930 a volunteer corps, the Sepāh-e Dāvtalab, was raised and deployed to Shivon as part of a coalition that overthrew the Order's regime. The corps fought at Aram and the coalition prevailed. It did so without a declaration of war by the Majles-e Mellī, the volunteer framework having been constructed precisely to avoid one - a device that was lawful and that has been cited since as the clearest vindication of the Āzādi-khāhān's warning.

The federation signed the Charter of the Free Nabataean States on 5 October 1934, accepting obligations to states it had not known in 1928 and a seat of organization at [[eretz]] rather than on its own territory.

Domestically, the resolution reshaped the political system that produced it. The factional structure of 1929 has been replaced by formal parties, the Majles-e Nāmāyandagān has been enlarged to 391 seats, and the 1933 election returned Ehteram Qaedi's ENS as the largest party, ending Shariati's nine years in office.

## Assessment

The vote is now generally read as the moment the federation acquired a foreign policy, and the debate about it has changed accordingly. Almost no one argues for a return to isolation. The argument that has replaced it concerns whether the constitutional structure built for an inward-facing republic can carry the obligations an outward-facing one accumulates - a question raised by the Morning Kestrel within a month of the vote, and not yet answered.

The anniversary is not a public holiday and no proposal to make one has reached the Assembly. On 23 November the chamber sits as usual.
