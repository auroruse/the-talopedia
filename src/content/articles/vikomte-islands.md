The Vikomte Islands (Skjarnish: *Vikomte-Öarna*) is an archipelago located in the Western Panthalassan Ocean on the Southern Pelagonian shelf. The major islands are roughly 1,360 km away from the nearest point in mainland [Pelagonia](https://auroruse.github.io/the-talopedia/pelagonia/), on the coast of the Nichirian colony of [[kinshu|Kinshū]]. It is a self-governing Skjarnish Territory, managing its own internal affairs while [Skjarnland](https://auroruse.github.io/the-talopedia/skjarnland/) administers its foreign policy and defence. Its capital and largest city is Torvbukta, home to roughly 40% of the archipelago's population.

## Etymology

The name Vikomte is a Skjarnish word meaning 'viscount'. It was initially the name given by the explorer Arvid Björnsson to the strait separating the three major islands upon his first arrival to the archipelago in 1761, the Vikomtesund, in honour of the 9th Viscount of Albybyn, Kurt von Heisenberg, who sponsored the expedition and was also a member of the Skjarnish cabinet. During Björnsson's second expedition to the islands in 1769, he formally claimed it for [[king-gustaf-vii|King Gustaf VII]] as the Viscount's Islands (the Vikomte Islands).

The word vikomte itself is a loan-word from the Old Arvernois visconte, a noble ranking between a count and a baron. Visconte in turn was derived from the Rumaic vicecomes; 'vice' is a prefix that still retains its meaning, 'the deputy of', and 'comes', meaning a companion to the emperor, which later evolved into the noble title of count.

## History

### Early Exploration

The Vikomte Islands were uninhabited upon its discovery by the Evrians, although it is possible that they could have been visited by one of the Southern Pelagonian peoples, in particular the Strayans, in premodern times. Evidence of this is primarily attributed to bone piles and stone tools found on the island that predate the earliest known Evrian contact, dating as far back as the 13th century.

The first confirmed Evrian sighting of the islands is attributed to the Arlbernian explorer Lüdecke  Rosenberg in 1703 during a voyage to what would become [[kogelland]], who charted the islands and named them the Akenauer Islands (Akenauer-Inseln), noting the archipelago's game and physical features, including the Vikomte Fox and the total lack of forests.

Evrians would not visit the island for another 58 years, due to its remote location and lack of any incentive. However, in 1759, [[arvid-bjornsson|Arvid Björnsson]], a Skjarnish Navy captain, was appointed by the Skjarnish Royal Scientific Society (which became the Royal Institute of Science in 1774) to lead Skjarnland's efforts to observe the Surtaran transit of 1761 in order to accurately measure the distance between Avium and Ignis Prime by noting the precise time [[surtara]] transited Ignis Prime from widely different geographical locations. Björnsson's crew would go to the southernmost known land, the then-named Akenauer Islands, with the astronomer Valdemar Kragh from the University of Hävnia to accomplish this.

Björnsson was already due to conduct a voyage to the South, initially an expedition to conduct trade in the Strayan Sea and write detailed accounts of the societies of [[arunya]] and [[recolotta]], which were relatively unknown to Evrians. However, following this new order, dispatched to him by the Skjarnish Admiralty, that became a secondary objective. In addition to observing the Surtaran transit, the Skjarnish government viewed the Vikomte Islands as a strategic location in Southern Pelagonia and wanted to assess its viability for settlement. Modern historians believe this was the driving factor behind the expedition's backing.

Björnsson departed [[kjellberg]] on 13 October 1760 on board the Strävan, a research vessel converted from a collier, and 4 other ships carrying additional supplies and providing escort, amounting to 300 men in total. After several weeks of navigating the thickening Arctic Ice, on 2 November he crossed the Kagyrgyn Strait, entering the Hyperborean Ocean. Björnsson steered south and made a rest stop in Sankt Franz-Josenthal, Ponurvia, on 30 November, before returning to sea on the 4th of December.

<figure><img src="https://i.imgur.com/bv0rsP3.png" alt="Arvid Björnsson's research vessel Strävan (right) alongside the escorting frigate Karl Gustaf (left), drawn during an earlier mission."><figcaption>Arvid Björnsson's research vessel Strävan (right) alongside the escorting frigate Karl Gustaf (left), drawn during an earlier mission.</figcaption></figure>

He made his final stop in [[marienhafen]], capital of the Holy Evrian colony of Kogelland, on 21 January 1761 before sailing directly to the archipelago on the 24th. Intense storms on the 28th drove him off course, delaying his landing by one week. On 8 February 1761, Björnsson made landfall on the western coast of the westernmost island, the archipelago's second largest, which he named Kurtsö, in honour of the viscount that sponsored his expedition.

Björnsson founded a temporary settlement on an inlet he named Håkanssonshavn, after [[bjorn-hakansson|the 16th century explorer]], and named the settlement Gustafshavn. In the 4 months leading up to the Surtaran transit in early June,  Björnsson's crew mapped the archipelago, beginning with the island they were on, Kurtsö, noting its resources and suitable harbours for future settlements. He marked one he thought would be the best on the island for any future settlement, naming it Torvbukta (Peat Bay) due to the local terrain's resemblance to peat. The crew also briefly landed on the smaller major island north of   Kurtsö, naming it Ojämnö (Jagged Island) due to its rough features, and went to the eastern and largest major island, which he named Gustafsland in honour of the Skjarnish King. Björnsson, with the help of his deputy Niels Jørgensen, named dozens of smaller islands as his ship circled the archipelago, and named the strait separating the three major islands the Vikomtesund (the Viscount's Sound), again honouring the sponsor of his expedition.

On the 6 June 1761, Surtara made its transit across Ignis Prime, which was observed by astronomers worldwide, including Kragh. Despite this large international effort, poor weather and equipment hampered much of the results' usefulness. Kragh's observations were hindered by overcast weather, which made his observations difficult, and the 'black drop effect' that distorted the planet's appearance through his telescope. After spending four months on the archipelago, Björnsson and his crew withdrew on 10 June, abandoning the outpost of Gustafshavn, resupplying in Sandspitze, Kogelland before entering the Strayan Sea to pursue their secondary objective. He returned to Skjarnland on 10 October, only 3 days from spending a year abroad.

### Settlement and Growth

In 1765, the Skjarnish Royal Scientific Society would approve another expedition to the Vikomte Islands to again attempt to observe the Surtaran transit of 1769. However, this was to be a secondary objective; the primary mission now was to establish a permanent settlement on Torvbukta, whom Björnsson, again selected to lead the expedition, identified as the best site on the island.

Björnsson departed Kjellberg on 3 June 1767 on board the Strävan with 11 ships and roughly 600 men, including astronomer Valdemar Kragh once more, making landfall on Kurtsö on 18 October and anchoring his ships in Torvbukta after a long but relatively uneventful journey. He immediately began construction of the new settlement, which he gave the same name as the harbour, constructing homes using wood brought over from Skjarnland. Construction was partially complete by December, which brought about a harsh winter that would be the first in a series of severe difficulties the colony would face in its early years. By 18 January 1768, 12 people died from disease and exposure. Kragh, who was supposed to make the observations for the Surtaran transit of the following year, fell gravely ill, forcing Björnsson to make an emergency run to Sandspitze to receive medical treatment and be in less severe weather. Kragh survived but became permanently weakened.

On 4 June 1769, Kragh observed the Surtaran transit, alongside the international astronomical community, with more successful results than the 1761 attempt, despite suffering from slightly overcast weather and the persistence of the black drop effect. Kragh would later die on 28 August 1771 of organ complications exacerbated by his illness of the previous year. Initially buried in Torvbukta, at the request of his family, his body would be returned to Skjarnland in 1775 and re-buried near his home in Hävnia.

Despite the difficulties faced by the settlers, the importing of sheep from Skjarnland, who adapted well to the abundant grasslands of the Vikomte Islands, and a growing whaling and fishing economy allowed the colony to grow to several hundred settlers by the beginning of the 19th century, although growth was slow and uneven and substantially dependent on annual supply convoys from Skjarnland. The majority of settlers were either shepherds, fishermen, labourers, or retired naval personnel granted land by the crown, with occasional visits from ships conducting emergency stops or whalers from nearby Kogelland and [[neuhollmar]]. In 1780, the Crown authorised the construction of permanent warehouses and a small naval battery overlooking the entrance of Torvbukta Harbour.

Following the conclusion of the [[napolyonic-wars]], the Skjarnish government feared foreign interference in the affairs of the archipelago, particularly from the Arvernois colony of [[south-pelagonia]]. In 1816 King Gustav VIII formally asserted the Vikomte Islands as a Crown Colony through the Royal Charter of Torvbukta, and introduced a Crown-appointed governor residing in Torvbukta to administer the islands on behalf of the King, the first governor being Tomas Billberg, who was among the original group of settlers in 1767.

Throughout the 19th century the Vikomte Island remained one of Skjarnland's smallest and most remote colonial possessions, remaining strategically important. Wool exports overtook whaling as the island's primary source of income in the mid-19th century, while regular trade with Kogelland and Neuhollmar, alongside improvements in ship technology, reduced the islands' isolation. Smaller communities, and in many instances individual homes on large plots of land, were founded throughout the archipelago while Torvbukta remained the largest settlement and the economic and political centre, receiving improvements including a lighthouse and telegraph lines to Kogelland, South Pelagonia, and Neuhollmar by 1890.

### Great War

Skjarnland joined the [[great-war|Great War]] on the side of the Central Powers in August 1914. During this period, the Vikomte Islands became a strategic naval hub in Southern Pelagonia, used by Skjarnish vessels and Holy Evrian U-Boats operating in the region to refuel and repair. The archipelago became a target of Entente raiding in January 1915 when an ten-ship-strong Arvernois squadron, led by Captain Gaston Frechette on board his battlecruiser, the *Gueydon*, attempted to destroy the supply hub at Torvbukta, launching their mission from Port Gabéra in the Arvernois colony of South Pelagonia. The squadron was spotted by a patrolling Holy Evrian cruiser just outside Cape Natal on 4 January 1915 moving directly south, which proceeded to shadow it, taking advantage of rough seas and foggy weather to avoid being caught and pursued. After a day of movement, the cruiser confirmed that the Arvernois squadron was moving south directly for the Vikomte Islands and radioed the main Holy Evrian Western Panthalassan fleet in Marienhafen to warn them, who in turn cabled Torvbukta by telegraph.

The Marienhafen naval command dispatched a small destroyer squadron of four destroyers patrolling waters south of Sandspitze, led by the V25-class *Montbach*, to reinforce the Skjarnish naval garrison in Torvbukta on 5 January, which consisted of one armoured cruiser and two destroyers, as they were the only Holy Evrian naval forces that could do so in time.

Frechette's squadron arrived near the coast off Torvbukta on the morning of 10 January 1915, with visibility less than 500 metres out due to thick fog. Expecting limited resistance, he ordered his ships to slowly approach the harbour and prepare for a bombardment of the town. However, the warning sent from Marienhafen allowed the Torvbukta garrison to evacuate most of its civilians and organise a defence. At 9:14 AM, Frechette's lead destroyer, the *Enseigne Giraud*, encountered the Skjarnish cruiser *Olof Sigurdsson*, commanded by Captain Einar Johansen, and its accompanying destroyers near the harbour's entrance, which opened a volley of fire on the Arvernois ship. While she suffered two direct hits, both believed to be from the *Sigurdsson's* main guns, no critical areas were struck and the *Giraud*'s quick evasive actions allowed it to avoid more hits during the exchange. Frechette responded by pulling further back from the harbour entrance and then returning fire at 9:26 AM. Due to continued poor visibility throughout the morning, neither side achieved any critical hits on the other's ships. The Arvernois squadron remained outside the range of Torvbukta's coastal batteries.

At around 11 AM, the fog began clearing up and the Arvernois squadron, with its superior numbers and firepower, inflicted more damage on the Skjarnish ships, killing several and injuring Johansen himself. However, at 11:37 AM, one of Frechette's destroyers, the *Royce du Pont*, spotted the Holy Evrian squadron sent from Sandspitze to reinforce the Skjarnish garrison. While Frechette believed he could have still fought, he feared the current squadron was part of a larger enemy fleet sent to destroy them. He then made the decision to disengage and withdraw south, concluding the Battle of Torvbukta.

The battle was tactically inconclusive; while moderate damage was dealt to vessels on both sides, no ships were sunk, and 11 Skjarnish and 3 Arvernois sailors perished. However, the Central Powers regarded it as a strategic victory as Arverne had failed to destroy or damage the supply hub in Torvbukta. In Skjarnland, news of this exchange became a public sensation and morale boost. Skjarnish press spun the battle into a heroic 'last stand' against an overwhelmingly superior Arvernois force, with the Strålholm Times calling it a 'Great Sea Victory in the Far South' on 13 January 1915. The Battle of Torvbukta would be the largest engagement around the Vikomte Islands for the remainder of the war, with no other notable engagements or raids occurring again within the vicinity of the archipelago. In 1919, several of the participants of the battle would receive military honours, with Captain Johansen receiving the Royal Order of the Sword, Skjarnland's third-highest military honour.

### Territorialisation and Present Day

Before the Battle of Torvbukta, the Vikomte Islands were relatively obscure in Skjarnland, but became more known after. After the Great War's end in 1918, military activity around the archipelago dropped sharply, and regular life and economic activities, including sheep farming, fishing, and maritime services. However, the war, in particular the battle, strengthened the identity of Vikomte Islanders, many of whom viewed it as a defining moment in their history.

In Kjellberg on 13 April 1926, the Riksdag passed the Vikomte Islands Territorialisation Act after roughly a decade of mounting public pressure, which elevated the Vikomte Island's status from a Crown Colony to a Territory. Under the Act, the islands are granted the authority of self-governance in internal affairs, including the creation of a Territorial Assembly (Territorialförsamling) in Torvbukta with 25 elected members that has the power to pass internal laws with the signature of the crown-appointed governor, who remains the principal administrator of the islands. Following the 1934 political crisis in mainland Skjarnland and the impending dissolution of the Skjarnish monarchy on 1 January 1935, the Governor of the Vikomte Islands will be appointed by the currently uncreated position of the President of Skjarnland.

The Vikomte Islands have a land area of 2,481 km² and a coastline of about 1,300 km, according to the Skjarnish Geographical Survey. The archipelago is comprised of 779 islands, including the three major islands: Gustafsland, the easternmost one and the largest; Kurtsö, the second-largest and the most populous; and Ojämnö, the smallest of the major islands. All islands are defined by hilly or mountainous terrain, with the exception of the plains of Strävansland, the southeastern region of Gustafsland.

The three major islands are separated by a strait, the Vikomtesund. Kurtsö is the location of the capital, Torvbukta and its accompanying military garrison of 200 personnel, and several smaller towns, including Kragh and Bondenskulle. Gustafsland is home to the largest sheep grazing areas on the archipelago, particularly in Strävansland, as well as Mount Esbjörn, the highest point on the islands. It is also home to Esbjörn Air Base, defended by a small garrison of 50 personnel and home to one fighter squadron, also serving as the islands' primary airfield. Ojämnö is the smallest and least populated of the major islands, owing to its rough terrain features and relative lack of natural harbours.

The islands' climate is primarily cold, windy and subpolar maritime. Rainfall is common throughout half the year, averaging 600 mm in Torvbukta, with sporadic snowfall common elsewhere on the islands. Daily weather is typically varied throughout the archipelago, and temperatures generally stay within -10 to 20°C. The immediate seas surrounding the area are frequently calm, but severe storms have been recorded in the past, including most recently in 1929.

## Economy

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:320px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/3sJnxlN.jpeg" alt="Torvbukta, 1930"><figcaption>Torvbukta, 1930</figcaption></figure>

When ranked among nations and overseas territories, the Vikomte Islands are 75th out of 76 in the world for GDP PPP, at $113,091,000 according to the 1930 census. However, it ranks 41st in GDP per capita at $22,618. The islands have a low HDI at 0.360. Its currency is the Vikomter Guldar, which is pegged to the Skjarnish Guldar.

The islands' economy is dependent on sheep farming, fishing, and maritime services. Its largest exports are wool, hides, venison and fish, which are often shipped to the nearby colonies of Southern Pelagonia (Kogelland and Kinshū being the largest such markets) or back to Evria. The islands are also reliant on selling fishing licenses as a source of revenue to anyone willing to fish within its waters. However, dependence on this, alongside an increase in illegal fishing activity, has led to efforts to establish alternative sources of income, including oil prospecting in the region.

## Education

The government of the Vikomte Islands historically did not emphasize education, unlike in mainland Skjarnland, due to the primary occupation of residents being in the primary sector (thus making literacy non-critical). This has led to a literacy rate of 22.8% as of 1930, compared to mainland Skjarnland's figure of 95%. Schooling exists primarily in larger settlements such as Torvbukta and follows the standard Skjarnish education system set out by the Ministry of Education in Kjellberg. However, schooling outside such locations is virtually non-existant.

Recent efforts have aimed to change this. Following the establishment of the Vikomte Islands as a Territory in 1926, the Territorial Assembly, recognising the islands' low literacy as a barrier to further development, has passed several educational reform acts, including the establishment of several primary and secondary schools in larger settlements outside Torvbukta, the sponsoring of literacy programs, and benefits for teachers willing to relocate to rural areas. Implementation has been made more difficult by the scattered nature of the population and supply issues.

## Demographics

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:320px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/wR8XIme.jpeg" alt="A Vikomte Islander (or Vikomter), pictured in his garden in Torvbukta, 1933"><figcaption>A Vikomte Islander (or Vikomter), pictured in his garden in Torvbukta, 1933</figcaption></figure>

The Vikomter population, numbering 5,000, is near-homogeneous, descended mostly from the settlers of the 18th and 19th centuries, most of whom were Skjarnian. Skjarnians are the dominant ethnicity, at 88.2%, while the other Alemannic Skjarns (Northern Alemannic peoples), the Danyrians and Norrvynians, form a minority. The 1930 census indicated that 82% of residents were born on the islands, with the rest all born on Skjarnland.
