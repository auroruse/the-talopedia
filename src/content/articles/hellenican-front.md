The Hellenican Front was a theater of the Great War, fought from 1914 to 1918 between the Winscor Accord powers of Vicily and East Astriya on one side and Selmira, a member of the Eisenpakt, on the other. It takes its name from Hellenica, the region in which it began. The front grew out of an undeclared conflict that started in December 1913, when Vicily intervened in an Astriyan uprising against the collapsing Murad Caliphate. Historians differ on whether the front should be dated from that intervention or from the formal declarations of war in August 1914.

## Background

### The Murad Caliphate

In 1913 the Murad Caliphate still ruled from Aspadana, on the western shore of the Chrysoporos, as it had for centuries. Its territory comprised Astartolia, the Nabataean coast up to Berytar, and eastern Hellenica, the Astriyan-inhabited country between the river Argyron and the Straits. The Caliph held both religious and temporal authority, but by the early twentieth century the state was governed in practice by the palace and the Grand Vizier.

The Caliphate was heavily indebted to foreign creditors. A large share of its revenue was pledged to servicing loans, and by 1913 the army had gone unpaid for months at a time.

### Vicily and West Astriya

Hellenica was divided between two states along the Argyron. Vicily had taken the western part, West Astriya, from the Murads a generation earlier. Successive Vicilian governments regarded the union of the remaining Astriyan lands with their own as unfinished business. Possession of those lands would also bring Vicily to the Straits.

The Astriyan national movement in the east had a different aim. It was organized by a secret society known as the Torchbearers (*Lampadephoroi*), whose leaders sough an Astriyan state and did not want to exchange Murad rule for Vicilian rule. The difference was set aside in 1913 and returned later in the war.

## Collapse of the Murad Dynasty

On 11 June 1913 the Murad treasury suspended payment on its foreign debt. Army pay, already in arrears, stopped altogether in several provinces.

Caliph Bayezid V died on 2 August 1913 after a reign of twenty-four years. The palace proclaimed his fourteen-year-old son Ahmed IV as Caliph, under the regency of the Grand Vizier,
Nazım Hayri Pasha. Much of the officer corps refused to recognize a regency and declared for the late Caliph's brother, Prince Mahmud Cevdet, a serving field marshal. Mahmud declined to take the throne from his nephew by force, and the succession was never settled.

In September, garrisons across Astartolia mutinied over pay. Their officers formed a council at Iskenderiye under General İsmail Ferid Pasha and stopped taking orders from the capital. With its preferred claimant unwilling to act, the Officers' Council gradually ceased to speak in the dynasty's name at all. From that autumn the Caliphate had two centers of authority: a regency in Aspadana that commanded no army, and an army in Astartolia that answered to no sovereign.

## Astriyan Uprising

The Torchbearers launched the uprising at Odryssa on 26 October 1913, and it spread along the Odrys valley within days. Murad garrisons in East Astriya were unpaid and unsure whose orders to follow, and in several towns they surrendered their barracks without resistance. A Provisional Government of Astriya was proclaimed at Odryssa on 3 November under Theopanes Karydis, a lawyer of the city, and appealed to Vicily for protection.

Where garrisons held, they carried out reprisals against towns that had risen. Through November, refugees crossed the Argyron into West Astriya in large numbers. The Vicilian government of Aurelio Brancati concentrated an army at Castelgulfio and declared that it could not remain indifferent to events on its frontier.

## Argyron Bridge Incident

On 9 December 1913 a Murad column pursuing an insurgent band reached the frontier bridge over the Argyron. Shots were exchanged with the Vicilian frontier guards on the far bank, who had been admitting refugees. Each side maintained that the other fired first.

Vicily demanded the withdrawal of all Murad forces from the frontier district. The regency in Aspadana did not control the troops concerned and could give no answer. On 12 December a Vicilian army under General Ruggero Castellani crossed the Argyron, in the words of the government's communiqué, "to restore order." No declaration of war was issued. Dedeagach, on the Hellenican coast, was occupied on 20 December.

Vicily called the operation the Astriyan Intervention and maintained for the next eight months that it was not at war. The Officers' Council called it an invasion.

## Proclamation of Selmira

The loss of the frontier without resistance destroyed what remained of the regency's standing. On 17 January 1914 the garrison of Aspadana went over to the Officers' Council, which declared the dynasty deposed. Ahmed IV and the Grand Vizier left the capital by sea the same night aboard a neutral vessel.

On 3 February 1914 the Council proclaimed a new state at Iskenderiye. In place of the dynasty's name it took the name Selmira, the old name for the country of the river Selm in the Astartolian interior. General
İsmail Ferid Pasha headed its provisional government. The new state claimed every territory the Caliphate had held, East Astriya included, and kept its seat of government at Iskenderiye, away from the front.

## The Undeclared War

Between February and May 1914, Astriyan and Vicilian forces overran the north of East Astriya in what became known as the Sideron campaign. Melcarta fell on 2 March, followed by Demirtas and Pristinia. The Selmiran garrisons of the north fell back on the Tethys ports of Odessos and Pyrgos. They were evacuated by sea to Aspadana in late May, and the two cities were abandoned.

The south proved harder. The fortress of Adrianopolis commanded the only road and railway to the capital, and its garrison had declared for the Council early and remained intact. The allied siege began on 21 April 1914.

By June, Selmira had reorganized the former Murad army under a single command, and the first conscript classes raised by the new state reached the Odrys. The front ran from Adrianopolis to the sea.

## Entry into the Great War

The Great War began on 28 July 1914. Vicily entered with the Entente and was soon engaged against the Holy Evrian Empire and Askurias on its southwestern and Barbary fronts. Selmira, already fighting Vicily and in need of credit and arms, joined the Central Powers.

In the first week of August the two states exchanged formal declarations of war. The Astriyan Intervention, eight months old and never declared, became the Hellenican Front.
