The Hellenican Front was a theater of the Great War, fought from 1914 to 1918 between the Winscor Accord powers of Vicily and East Astriya on one side and Selmira, a member of the Eisenpakt, on the other. It takes its name from Hellenica, the region in which it began. The front grew out of an undeclared conflict that started in December 1913, when Vicily intervened in an Astriyan uprising against the collapsing Murad Caliphate. Historians differ on whether the front should be dated from that intervention or from the formal declarations of war in August 1914.

## Background

### The Murad Caliphate in Hellenica

#### Murad rule before 1886

The Murad Caliphate, ruled from
Balıkefra (Aspadana) on the western shore of the Chrysoporos, held Astartolia, the Ebernari coast and the whole of Hellenica until the late nineteenth century. Its Hellenican provinces were inhabited mostly by Astriyans, administered by appointed governors and garrisoned from the fortresses of 
İzsin, Tekirdın and Karasir (modern day Castelgulfio, Odryssa and Adrianopolis).

#### Vicilian-Murad War (1886-1888)

The Vicilian-Murad War (Vicilian: *Guerra d'Oriente*, "Eastern War"; Astriyan:
Πόλεμος του Διαμελισμού,
*Pólemos tou Diamelismoú*, "War of the Partition"; Selmiran:
*Seksen Altı Harbi*, "War of '86") began with an Astriyan rebellion at Amasfez (currently Attenza) on 6 April 1886. Murad irregular troops put the rebellion down within three weeks, and the killing of civilians that accompanied its suppression was reported at length in the Vicilian press. Vicily declared war on 2 May 1886.

The Vicilian fleet destroyed the Murad squadron in the Hellenican Sea off Eradea on 19 June, and an army was landed in southern Hellenica the following month. Amasfez fell on 3 August.
İzsin, the principal Murad fortress in western Hellenica, was besieged from 11 November 1886 and capitulated on 27 March 1887. A Murad counteroffensive was defeated on the Argyron in August 1887. A Vicilian attempt to carry the war beyond the river toward
Tekirmir (Dedeagach) failed in October, and an armistice was signed on 9 December 1887.

#### Treaty of Tekirmir

The Treaty of Tekirmir, signed on 14 March 1888, ceded to Vicily all Murad territory west of the Argyron and imposed an indemnity payable in gold over twenty-five years. Under Article 9, Vicily undertook to grant the ceded provinces a statute of self-government. The Argyron, which had never been a political boundary, became the frontier, and the lands on either side of it came to be called West Astriya and East Astriya. Caliph Orhan IV abdicated in 1889 in favor of his nephew, who reigned as Bayezid V.

#### Debt and the reign of Bayezid V

Bayezid V (reigned 1889-1913) inherited the indemnity and added to it the loans raised to rebuild the army and to construct the Aspis lines west of Balıkefra between 1889 and 1896. From 1894 the customs, salt and tobacco revenues were pledged to a Public Debt Council of foreign bondholders. By 1912 the service of the debt consumed about half of the Caliphate's revenue.

### The Astriyan National Movement

The statute promised at Tekirmir was never enacted. West Astriya was governed as the Eastern Provinces by a governor-general at Castelgulfio, which was developed as a naval base and railhead. Vicilian was the language of administration and of the courts. Astriyan petitions for the statute in 1893 and 1901 received no answer.

The Torchbearers (Astriyan: Λαμπαδηφόροι,
*Lampadephoroi*) were founded at Odryssa in 1894 by teachers, clerks and merchants' sons. The society was secret, organized in cells on both banks of the Argyron, and banned by both governments. Its program was a single Astriyan state on both banks, as opposed to the transfer of East Astriya from Murad to Vicilian rule.

## Collapse of the Murad Caliphate

### Default of 1913

On 11 June 1913 the Murad treasury failed to pay the coupon due to its bondholders, in the year in which the final installment of the indemnity also fell due. The Public Debt Council took direct control of the pledged revenues. The army's pay, already seven months behind, stopped altogether, and bread riots broke out in Aspadana in July.

### Death of Bayezid V and the Succession

Bayezid V died on 2 August 1913. His son, aged fourteen, was proclaimed the following day as Ahmed IV under the regency of the Grand Vizier,
Nazım Hayri Pasha. A group of senior officers offered their support to the late Caliph's brother, Prince Mahmud Cevdet, who declined it on 20 August and withdrew from the capital.

### Saptember mutinies and the Officers' Council

The mutinies began on 9 September at
Sultanönü, where the garrison refused to parade unpaid, and reached Siyahkaya and Iskenderiye within five days. On 21 September the mutinous commands formed the Officers' Council at Iskenderiye under General
İsmail Ferid Pasha, which demanded the dismissal of the regent and the payment of arrears. By October the Council controlled the interior of Astartolia. The regency held the capital and the Straits and was nominally obeyed by the garrisons of Hellenica, which were paid by neither.

## Astriyan Uprising

### Odryssa revolt

On 26 October 1913 the Torchbearers seized the arsenal, the telegraph office and the railroad station at Odryssa. The garrison of about 3,000 withdrew to the citadel and surrendered on 29 October. The revolt spread along the upper Odrys valley within a week. The garrisons of the northern districts and of Adrianopolis held their positions.

### Provisional Government of Astriya

The Provisional Government of Astriya was proclaimed at Odryssa on 3 November under Theophanes Karydis. Its proclamation claimed an Astriyan state on both banks of the Argyron. It did not request Vicilian assistance, and Vicily did not recognize it.

### Murad countermeasures

A column of about 8,000 men marched up the Odrys from Adrianopolis in early November, burning villages held to have supported the revolt. It halted short of Odryssa on 19 November, when its troops refused to advance without pay. Irregular cavalry extended the reprisals to the districts between the Odrys and the Argyron, and about 70,000 refugees crossed the Argyron into West Astriya during the month.

## Vicilian intervention

### Mobilization

The government of Aurelio Brancati ordered a partial mobilization on 8 November. The Army of the East (Vicilian: *Armata d'Oriente*), about 120,000 strong, was assembled at Castelgulfio under General Ruggero Castellani. The government gave its purpose as the protection of the refugees and of the frontier.

### Argyron Bridge Incident

On the night of 9 December 1913, Murad cavalry pursuing refugees onto the bridge that carried the Castelgulfio-Dedeagach road over the Argyron exchanged fire with the Vicilian frontier post. A Vicilian captain and six of his men were killed, together with an unrecorded number of Murad troops and refugees. Each side stated that the other had fired first, and no inquiry was ever held. On 10 December Vicily demanded the withdrawal of all Murad forces to a line 30 kilometers east of the river within forty-eight hours.
Nazım Hayri Pasha replied on 11 December, accepting a joint inquiry and declining the withdrawal.

### Crossing of the Argyron

Castellani crossed the Argyron at three points on 12 December "to restore order in the frontier districts". No declaration of war was issued. The Murad frontier troops surrendered without serious resistance, and Dedeagach was occupied on 20 December. Vicilian and Astriyan forces met on the Odrys below Odryssa on 4 January 1914. Vicily termed the operation the Astriyan Intervention (Vicilian: *Intervento in Astria*) and denied until August 1914 that a state of war existed.

## Undeclared war (December 1913 - August 1914)

### Fall of the dynasty and the proclamation of Selmira

The loss of the frontier ended the regency. The Aspadana garrison declared for the Officers' Council on 15 January 1914, and
Nazım Hayri Pasha resigned. On 17 January the Council declared the dynasty deposed, and Ahmed IV left Aspadana by sea the same night.

The state of Selmira was proclaimed at Iskenderiye on 3 February 1914, with
İsmail Ferid Pasha at its head. It tooks its name from the country of the river Selm in the Astartolian interior, where the Council had found its first support. Neither government declared war. Vicily did not recognize the Council as a government against which war could be declared, and the Council, which was seeking foreign credit, preferred not to be the party that declared it.

### Sideron campaign and the encirclement of Adrianopolis

Between February ad May 1914 a Vicilian corps under General Ottavio Serravalle, with Astriyan volunteers, reduced the Murad garrisons of the Sideron Mountains and the northern districts. Melcarta fell on 2 March, Demirtas on 11 April and Pristinia on 6 May. The remaining garrisons, about 30,000 men, withdrew to the Tethys Coast and were evacuated by sea from Odessos and Pyrgos in the last week of May.

Castellani's main body moved down the Odrys against Adrianopolis, whose garrison under
Hüseyin Rıfat Pasha had accepted the authority of the Council in February. The encirclement was completed on 21 April 1914.

## Outbreak of the Great War (July-August 1914)

### Astriyan adherence to the Winscor Accord

The Great War began on 28 July 1914. On July 30 the Provisional Government of Astriya, which no state had yet recognized, addressed a note to the powers of the Winscor Accord declaring its adherence to the alliance and placing its forces, then about 60,000 volunteers, at the Accord's disposal. Elvester, Arverne and Rudania replied jointly on 1 August. The reply accepted Astriya as an associated belligerent, a status that stopped short of recognizing the Provisional Government and made no reference to its territorial claims.

### Selmiran alliance with the Eisenpakt

Selmira was likewise unrecognized and had been unable to raise credit since the default of 1913. It had been in negotiation with the Eisenpakt since the spring, and the Accord's reply to Odryssa ended its hesitation. A treaty of alliance was signed at Iskenderiye on 2 August, under which the Eisenpakt powers recognized Selmira, extended a loan and undertook to supply arms. On the same day the Selmiran government suspended all payments to the Public Debt Council, whose bondholders were primarily Arvenois and Elvesterian, and resumed control of he pledged revenues.

### Vicilian entry

The Brancati government had declared its neutrality in the general war on 29 July, intending to complete the campaign in Hellenica outside it. The Selmiran treaty removed that possibility. Vicily was now at war with a member of the Eisenpakt, and it shared a long land border with Arverne, a member of the Accord. The Accord powers offered a guarantee of Vicilian sovereignty over West Astriya. The cabinet approved allegiance by a narrow majority on 4 August, and Vicily declared war on Selmira on 5 August. Selmira declared war on Vicily the following day.

### Castelgulfio Protocol

Elvester, Arverne and Rudania, which did not wish the Straits to pass under the control of a single power, made their guarantee conditional on Vicilian recognition of the Provisional Government. The Castelgulfio Protocol was signed on 9 August 1914 by Vicily and the Provisional Government, with the three powers as witnesses. Vicily recognized the Provisional Government as the government of Astriya "in the territories east of the Argyron". The Provisional Government undertook to exercise no authority west of the river and to permit no agitation there. The claim to both banks made in the proclamation of 3 November 1913 was neither withdrawn nor mentioned. Vicily afterward held that the protocol had renounced the claim; the Astriyan position was that it had been suspended for the duration of the war.

The council of the Torchbearers accepted the protocol by a majority, and its cells in West Astriya rejected it. With recognition, the Astriyan volunteer bands were reorganized as the Astriyan National Army (Astriyan:
Εθνικός Στρατός,
*Ethnikós Stratós*) under General Markos Lazarides.

### Dating of the front

Vicilian official historians date the Hellenican front from the declaration of 5 August 1914. Selmiran and Astriyan accounts date it from the crossing of the Argyron on 12 December 1913.

## 1914: Adrianopolis and the Aspis

### Vicilian Strategy

The Vicilian General Staff gave priority to the Southwestern front. In a memorandum of 7 August, Castellani asked for two further army corps in order to mask Adrianopolis and march on Aspadana at once. The request was refused, and he was instructed to reduce the fortress with the forces already in Hellenica. The Army of the East then consisted of three Vicilian corps before Adrianopolis, a fourth under Serravalle in the northern districts, and the Astriyan National Army

### Siege of Adrianopolis

#### Adrianopolis and the Three Days

Adrianopolis stood where the Odrys turns south toward the Hellenican Sea, and both the road and the railroad from Odryssa to Aspadana passed under its guns. A ring of twenty-six forts and batteries on a perimeter of about 40 kilometers enclosed a garrison of some 52,000 men under
Hüseyin Rıfat Pasha. The strongest works lay in the eastern sector, anchored on Fort
Demirkapı ("Iron Gate") and Fort
Akbayır ("White Slope"). The garrison had been cut off since 21 April and was living on the stores laid in before the investment.

Castellani attempted to take the fortress by storm before his siege train arrived. Two Vicilian corps and an Astriyan division attacked the eastern sector on 24 August. The assualt reached the ditch of Fort
Akbayır on the second day and was abandoned on 26 August with about 9,000 casualties. The Vicilian press called the attack "the Three Days".

#### Battle of Xerolophos

A Selmiran relief force of about 35,000 men under Cemal Tevfik Pasha advanced from the east in early September. It was met on 6 September at Xerolophos (Astriyan:
Ξερόλοφος,
*Xerólophos*, "Dry Hill"; Selmiran: *Kurutepe*), a ridge 25 kilometers east of the city, by an Astriyan corps of 28,000 under Lazarides. A sortie by the garrison from the eastern forts on 8 September failed to reach the ridge. Tevfik Pasha withdrew on 10 September. The battle was the first fought by the National Army as a regular force, and it cost the Astriyans about 3,500 casualties.

#### Bombardment and storming of the eastern forts

The Vicilian siege train, ninety-six heavy guns and mortars brought by rail from Castelgulfio, opened fire on 19 September, directed from observation balloons. About 111,000 shells were fired into the eastern sector in twenty-two days. By early October the garrison was on quarter rations, and typhus had appeared in the city.

Fort
Demirkapı was taken by Vicilian infantry on the night of 11 October, and Fort
Akbayır by the Astriyans the following morning. With the eastern sector open,
Rıfat Pasha ordered his remaining guns and stores destroyed.

#### Capitulation and occupation

Rıfat Pasha capitulated on 14 October, 176 days after the siege began. About 41,000 officers and men became prisoners; some 11,000 of the garrison had been killed or had died of disease and hunger. The prisoners were held in open camps on the islands of the Odrys below the city, where several thousand died of exposure and disease before the camps were cleared in December.

The city was placed under a Vicilian military governor. The Provisional Government, which had expected to install its own commissioner under the Castelgulfio Protocol, protested the appointment, in the first open disagreement between the two allies since the protocol was signed.

### Advance across the Aspadanan plain

#### Battle of the Leukon

On 22 October
İsmail Ferid Pasha issued the Aspis directive (Selmiran:
*Kalkan Talimatı*). It ordered that the plain was to be defended only for as long as was needed to man the Aspis lines, and that the Western Army was not to be risked in its defense.

Castellani marched east along the railroad on 18 October with about 155,000 men. The Selmiran Western Army (Selmiran: *Batı Ordusu*), about 90,000 strong under
Kâzım Şevket Pasha, stood behind the Leukon (Selmiran:
*Akçay*), a tributary of the Odrys that crossed the plain from the east. The Vicilian corps attacked on either side of the railroad bridge from 27 October and gained two small bridgeheads. The Astriyan corps marched 40 kilometers upstream and crossed at the low-water crossing of Kalamia (Astriyan:
Καλάμια, "the Reeds") on 30 October, turning the Selmiran northern flank.
Şevket Pasha withdrew during the night of 31 October. Allied casualties were about 11,000; the Selmirans lost about 9,000 killed and wounded and 4,000 prisoners.

#### Pursuit and the autumn rains

The Western Army retired in good order, destroying the railroad bridges and stations behind it. About 250,000 Selmiran-speaking inhabitants of the plain left with the army and crowded into Aspadana. The autumn rains began on 2 November and held the Vicilian siege train west of the Leukon. The allied vanguard reached the Aspis on 11 November. Serravalle's corps, about 25,000 men, joined from the north along the Tethys coast on 14 November.

### First Battle of the Aspis

#### Aspis lines

The Aspis lines (Selmiran:
*Kalkan Hattı*, "Shield Line") ran for about 100 kilometers from the Tethys coast to the Mesothalassa, along the ridge behind the Aspis some 35 kilometers west of Aspadana. Lagoons at either end reduced the front that could be attacked to about 85 kilometers. The lines had been built between 1889 and 1896 and consisted of three belts of redoubts. Their principal works were the Sword redoubt (Selmiran:
*Kılıç Tabya*) in the center and the Lagoon redoubt (Selmiran:
*Göl Tabya*) in the north. In November 1914 they were held by about 115,000 men, with warships of the Selmiran fleet covering both flanks.

#### Castellani's plan, execution and suspension

Castellani issued his order of attack on 15 November. He had about 165,000 men and no heavy artillery. He judged that the defenders were demoralized, and that the cholera already present in his own army would not permit him to wait for the siege train. The main effort was to fall on the center, with Serravalle's corps and the Astriyans in the north and a Vicilian corps along the Mesothalassa shore.

The attack opened on 17 November with a bombardment by field guns, fired in fog, which had limited effect. Assaults on the Sword Redoubt on 18 and 19 November reached the wire and failed.

At about two o'clock on the morning of 20 November, Astriyan battalions took the Lagoon Redoubt by surprise. No reserves were at hand to support them. A Selmiran counterattack retook the work at dawn, when the fog lifted.

Attacks along the Mesothalassa shore on 21-23 November were broken up by fire from the Selmiran battleship *Selm* and two cruisers, which the allies had no means of answering.

Castellani suspended the offensive on 24 November. Allied casualties were about 17,000 killed and wounded, of whom 11,000 were Vicilian and 6,000 Astriyan. Selmiran casualties were about 8,000.

### Winter of 1914-15

#### Cholera

Cholera had appeared among the refugees and the Selmiran troops in late October and spread to the allied armies as they crossed the plain. By the end of December about 30,000 allied soldiers had fallen sick and about 7,000 had died. Selmiran losses in the lines were of a similar order, with several thousand further deaths among the refugees in Aspadana.

#### Truce of the Burial Parties

A local truce from 28 November to 1 December, arranged between the two commands, allowed both sides to bury their dead. It became known as the Truce of the Burial Parties. Both armies then entrenched, and the front of the Aspis did not move by more than a few kilometers for the next two and a half years.

#### Morosi plan

The government of Aurelio Brancati retained Castellani in command. Castellani asked for the siege train and a second assault in the spring. Admiral Tancredi Morosi argued that the lines could not be taken from the front while Selmiran warships covered their flanks, and proposed to force the Stenon, enter the Mesothalassa and take the lines from behind. The cabinet adopted the Morosi plan (Vicilian: *Piano Morosi*) on 14 January 1915.
