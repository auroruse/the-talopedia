## Early Life and Education

Mayu Kitta was born on February 3, 1864, in [[takarazuka]], the son of a Shogunate retainer who served under [[aratame-jozui|Aratame Jōzui]] at the siege of Chiyoda Castle in the [[bakuraku-war]]. The family's hereditary stipend was converted into government bonds after the war. Kitta graduated from the National Military Academy and was commissioned into the [[shogunate-armed-forces]]' Ground Corps in 1888. He married Akemi Hiiragi, and their daughter, Tomoko, was born the same year.

His first posting was to the Mizuhara garrison, where he surveyed the crossings of the Shizuku River. He served as a supply staff officer in the reorganization of the army under the reforms of 1894, and afterwards organized garrison supply in Kiritani and Shin-Nichirin (now [[horai|Hōrai]] and [[kinshu|Kinshū]]). In 1907, after Akemi fell ill, he left active duty for a lectureship in logistics and doctrine at the National War College in [[shinkeisei]]; she died in 1909.

## Great War Service

The Xing Empire invaded Nichirin in August 1914, shortly after the outbreak of the Great War. Kitta was recalled to active duty in May 1915. The following month, he succeeded [[aratame-jozui|Aratame Jōzui]] as commander of Mizuhara Defense Command, which held the line of the Shizuku River with six understrength divisions. Xing forces attacked Mizuhara on July 19. By the end of the month, they had occupied the city's southern districts, but Nichirian forces continued to hold the northern bank of the Shizuku.

On July 25, Kitta was taken prisoner after Xing artillery destroyed his command post. He was held for three days by Colonel Zhang Wei, who tortured him and left his right hand permanently maimed. A raid led by one of his former cadets, who was killed in the operation, freed him on July 27. Kitta declined reconstructive surgery on the hand. On July 31, he personally executed seven Guandongese prisoners of war on the Mizuhara docks.

Nichirian forces held the northern bank through the winter. In March 1916, Kitta launched Operation Shōtan, a counteroffensive that retook the southern districts and ended the Battle of Mizuhara. It coincided with Marshal-Admiral [[suiren-makinami]]'s victory over the Xing fleet at the [[first-battle-of-the-bay-of-serica]] and with the [[battle-of-daikei|collapse of the Daikei Pocket]] on the Matsuhana River, and the Xing began a general retreat along the entire front. Kitta was awarded the Order of the Bulwark, first class, for his defense of the city.

After his victory in the [[second-battle-of-tanretsu]], Kitta's forces captured a Xing rear-echelon unit. The unit's male prisoners were executed on the orders of officers under his command, and its women were forced into sexual slavery. Neither was ordered by Kitta. [[uzume|Liang Weixi]], a captured medic who relayed the execution orders to the prisoners as a translator, was later recruited into SEELE. In October, his forces cleared the last Xing positions in his sector in the [[second-battle-of-asamitsu]], and his command held the border opposite [[sangde]] through 1917.

During [[operation-hisho|Operation Hishō]], Nichirin's counterattack into the Xing Empire, frontal assaults on the Kalgan Pass in the Yin Mountains of southern Guandong stalled with heavy casualties in early September 1918. Kitta took command of the Yin Mountains Campaign on September 9. He carried the pass in early October by replacing frontal assaults on the ridgelines with lateral advances along the slopes, each covered by staggered fallback positions at the same elevation. His forces then took [[fuhua]] and encircled the Xing Southern Army at [[daiping]]. The Southern Army surrendered on November 11, the day of the armistice.

In December 1918, an inquiry by Makinami into the conduct of the defense of Mizuhara documented the systematic execution of prisoners between 1915 and 1918, the shelling of medical facilities, the unnecessary use of chemical weapons, and reprisals against civilians. No charges were brought.

## Bakufu Career

Kitta held the Mizuhara Defense Command until April 1919, when he succeeded Tokiyuki Takamori as Chancellor of the National War College. Masashi Miyamoto, the heir apparent, completed a Master of Strategic Studies there in 1922. After assuming power, Masashi appointed Kitta Senior Shogunal Advisor on October 2, 1923, one of four initial appointments alongside Makinami, Chief of Staff [[hideki-otonashi]], and Chief Historian [[kanade-tachibana]]. Kitta also received concurrent command of the Army of the Haguregumo, the main component of the [[metropolitan-theater-command]].

During the [[night-of-falling-stars]], [[keiko-miyamoto]]'s attempted coup of March 4, 1924, advance units of the Army of the Haguregumo reached Shinkeisei within hours, causing the coup to collapse. After Masanori Miyamoto's death that November, Masashi ordered Kitta, Makinami and General [[semen-voronovkin]] to encircle the major cities of [[sangai]], where [[yuriko-okamura]] had taken refuge. Kitta's troops surrounded Okamura at the town hall of [[shichidaika]], and she surrendered on December 8 in exchange for reduced charges against her guards. Kitta served as prosecutor at her trial, which opened on December 10. The tribunal acquitted her, but Masashi overrode the verdict and ordered her execution.

Kitta was a patron of the Red Oni Society (赤鬼会), an association of Mizuhara veterans whose members wore red oni masks at national team matches. On July 22, 1925, he gave the dedication address at the [[tadamune-kuronami-national-stadium]], a war memorial built on the ground where the Mizuhara line had held. He opposed the normalization of relations with Guandong at the Tōhara Conference of March 1926 on the grounds that it betrayed the cost of the war. His opposition was public, organized through the veterans' associations, and continued through the following months.

On the night of November 15, 1926, Kitta shot himself in his apartment in Shinkeisei. The Bakufu announced that he had died of heart failure, and he was buried privately on November 20. His political allies dispersed within months.

## Kagemusha Career

In the custody of SEELE, Kitta survived his gunshot wound. SEELE's director, [[hideaki-katsuragi]], had kept him under watch as a suicide risk since August 1926, when he drafted a protocol for terminating Kitta's identity, and a SEELE team reached the apartment within forty minutes of the shot. The bullet had entered his right temple and exited through the parietal bone, destroying part of the temporal lobe but sparing the brainstem. Under the protocol, his death was recorded as heart failure on November 16, and the casket buried on November 20 contained sand and medical waste. Katsuragi later stated that he had regarded Kitta both as a threat to the Tōhara settlement and as the most experienced field tactician in the country. After rehabilitation by SEELE's doctors, Kitta returned to service by July 1927, without the Shogun's knowledge, in the Kagemusha, a special operations unit then attached to SEELE, under the cover rank of High Commandant and the codename Shuten (酒呑).

The codename, taken from the oni king Shuten-dōji, alluded to the "red oni" of Mizuhara, and Kitta's team leaders bore the names of Shuten-dōji's followers at Ōeyama. Katsuragi's recruitment file also glosses it as a reference to the whiskey Kitta had drunk before shooting himself. In the field, he wore a white full-face oni mask and unmarked winter kit.

In January 1928, Kitta took command of the Kagemusha detachment that relieved the regular army garrison at [[imperial-site-h-041-malkuth|Malkuth]], in [[hyperborea]]. After the bombing of the Elysian embassy in Shinkeisei that November, he assumed command of the 200-strong garrison of [[forward-operating-base-keter]]. On December 27, Kitta was exposed when Tachibana identified his fallback doctrine in the survey of Route Alpha, while, separately, Makinami identified his deformed right hand. Masashi, after confronting him in person, placed all SEELE operations under his personal review.

Later that month, Tachibana recorded Kitta's deposition on the Mizuhara executions in the war record of the Imperial Archive, where it was filed as a historical account rather than as evidence. Kitta held no post in 1929 and lived at the Palace of the Solar Halo. On January 1, 1930, when Otonashi was shot dead by a sniper in Shiomachi, Kitta returned fire and wounded the sniper, [[sae-kujo|Sae Kujō]], in the face.

Following Katsuragi's arrest in February 1930, command of the Kagemusha passed to [[gendo-takahashi]], one of SEELE's three directors. That June, Takahashi restored Kitta's rank so that he could lead the Nichirian contingent of a joint Alemannian, Elysian and Nichirian expedition across the Northern Wall. After the expedition reached the interior of Hyperborea in July, Kitta and the biologist Rin Shiragiku established a camp in a geothermal caldera on August 3, which eventually became [[da-at-research-station|Da'at Research Station]]; he commanded its garrison until November 1932. In October 1930, he recovered Katsuragi and the two other survivors of a penal squad sent through the caves beneath the Wall. In June 1932, he and [[george-elwood]] led a joint Elysian and Nichirian assault on the headquarters of the clandestine organization MAJESTY.

In November 1932, Takahashi recalled Kitta to command the guard at SEELE's headquarters in Shiratsuki. On February 7, 1933, having had fellow director [[yui-shinazugawa]] killed, Takahashi ordered Kitta to detain Kujō, [[mako-murakumo]] and Katsuragi's daughter [[kyouko-katsuragi|Kyouko]], who had seen records of the revived conditioning program at Kelipah. Kitta instead let them escape the compound and reported their disappearance nine minutes later, and Takahashi kept him in his post regardless.

## Arrest and Testimony

On February 16, 1933, Kitta led a Kagemusha detail sent to clear the Site 'Kelipah.' When a Kochōrin prefectural police patrol arrived during the clearance, a member of the detail shot and killed its sergeant. Kitta ordered the detail to withdraw but remained at the site, where he gave first aid to a wounded constable and surrendered. He then directed the police to a crate containing a child's remains that the detail had left behind. Identified by his fingerprints as a man officially deceased since 1926, he was held at Karihira for thirteen months without charge.

At Tachibana's request, Minister of Interior Affairs [[munehiro-ogasawara]] amended the civil register to record Kitta as living on March 20, 1934. Called as a defense witness at the trial of Kyouko Katsuragi, who stood accused in connection with the death of a SEELE agent at Kelipah, Kitta testified on March 26 to the clearance of the site. The Minister of Justice, [[kenzo-kamiya|Kenzo Kamiya]], then put Kitta's 1928 deposition to him, and Kitta affirmed under oath his execution of seven prisoners of war at Mizuhara in 1915. The court referred the confession to the Ministry of Justice. As of 1934, Kitta remains in custody at Karihira pending its decision.
