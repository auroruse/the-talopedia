## History

### Origins

Association football was introduced to Nichirin in the early 1880s by [[elvester|Elvesterian]] sailors in [[takarazuka]]. From the port, it spread among shipyard workers employed alongside Elvesterian engineers; among naval cadets, who were taught it as physical training by Elvesterian instructors hired under the Shinseiki Reforms; and among students at [[takarazuka-imperial-university]], whose side became the [[imperial-sports-society]], the first football club in Nichirin. The early game was largely confined to [Metropolitan Nichirin](https://auroruse.github.io/the-talopedia/nichirin/#topography). Of the ten clubs in the inaugural 1888/89 season of Nichirin League One (NLO), eight were based in Takarazuka or Shinkeisei, and most were military teams.

Nichirin's first international match took place in [[naginomiya]] on May 6, 1892, as an exhibition staged by the Bakufu during a visit by a [[xing-empire|Xing]] diplomatic delegation. As fielding a military team against the Xing was considered provocative, [[takarazuka-imperial-university]]'s side was selected to represent the country, and won 5-2. The side, which had been preparing for a tour of Elvester, sailed shortly afterward and lost 8-2 to Elvester in Dunmaris on June 21, which remains the national team's heaviest defeat.

Following the tour, the service clubs refused to recognize a university side as the national team, and the university declined to cede the role. As a result, Nichirin played no further international matches for the remainder of the decade. In late 1899, after the founding of the [[avium-football-association]] (AFA) and the announcement of the inaugural [[afa-world-cup|World Cup]], [[afa-club-world-cup|Club World Cup]], and continental championships, the Bakufu established the Nichirian Football Association (NFA) by decree, meeting the AFA's requirement of a single member association per country. The NFA formed a joint selection committee of service and university representatives and appointed [[percy-nicholson]], a retired Elvesterian former [[toteham-lilywhite]] player unaffiliated with either faction, as the first head coach. Under Nicholson, Nichirin won the inaugural [[vafc-championship|VAFC Championship]] in 1900 and made its World Cup debut the same year.

### The Nicholson Era

The domestic game was organized from the 1890s largely through state sports societies, whose branch clubs across the country extended the Nichirin League One beyond the metropolitan region; the league had expanded from ten clubs to twenty by 1900. The most prominent societies were Dynamo, run by the Ministry of the Interior for Manshuan police, and Spartak, for trade workers in Shinkeisei. The sport's following grew rapidly after Dynamo Mizuhara became champions of the 1901 Club World Cup, becoming the first Nichirian club to win an international title. Football overtook sumo as Nichirin's most popular sport in the 1900s as sumo tournaments became increasingly a social fixture of the professional class.

Nichirin won the first twelve editions of the VAFC Championship, from 1900 to 1911, but did not advance beyond the quarter-finals in its first seven World Cups. Its principal goalscorer over the period was [[s-ichir-hidaka|Sōichirō Hidaka]], a center-forward with Tsukumo Shinkeisei who debuted in 1901. [[seignid-national-football-team|Seignid]] ended the run with successive titles in 1912 and 1913. Captained by Maglinti Catacutan, father of Seignidese defender Narra Catacutan, the side combined a sweeper with man-marking, including a designated marker for Hidaka, and won both finals 1-0 from set pieces. Nichirin had fielded a reserve side in 1912, a World Cup year, but lost the 1913 final with its first team.

In response, Nicholson restructured the team's attacking play to counter deep defenses. At the 1914 World Cup, Nichirin reached its first final and defeated the [[holy-evrian-empire-national-football-team|Holy Evrian Empire]] 1-0, Hidaka scoring the only goal, to become the first Valtherian side to win the tournament. Nicholson retired after the tournament and is known in Nichirin to this day as the "father of the national team".
