## History

### Origins

Association football was introduced to Nichirin in the early 1880s by [[elvester|Elvesterian]] sailors in [[takarazuka]]. From the port, it spread among shipyard workers employed alongside Elvesterian engineers; among naval cadets, who were taught it as physical training by Elvesterian instructors hired under the Shinseiki Reforms; and among students at [[takarazuka-imperial-university]], whose side became the [[imperial-sports-society]], the first football club in Nichirin. The early game was largely confined to [Metropolitan Nichirin](https://auroruse.github.io/the-talopedia/nichirin/#topography). Of the ten clubs in the inaugural 1888/89 season of Nichirin League One (NLO), eight were based in Takarazuka or Shinkeisei, and most were military teams.

Nichirin's first international match took place in [[naginomiya]] on May 6, 1892, as an exhibition staged by the Bakufu during a visit by a [[xing-empire|Xing]] diplomatic delegation. As fielding a military team against the Xing was considered provocative, [[takarazuka-imperial-university]]'s side was selected to represent the country, and won 5-2. The side, which had been preparing for a tour of Elvester, sailed shortly afterward and lost 8-2 to Elvester in Dunmaris on June 21, which remains the national team's heaviest defeat.

Following the tour, the service clubs refused to recognize a university side as the national team, and the university declined to cede the role. As a result, Nichirin played no further international matches for the remainder of the decade. In late 1899, after the founding of the [[avium-football-association]] (AFA) and the announcement of the inaugural [[afa-world-cup|World Cup]], [[afa-club-world-cup|Club World Cup]], and continental championships, the Bakufu established the Nichirian Football Association (NFA) by decree, meeting the AFA's requirement of a single member association per country. The NFA formed a joint selection committee of service and university representatives and appointed [[percy-nicholson]], a retired Elvesterian former [[toteham-lilywhite]] player unaffiliated with either faction, as the first head coach. Under Nicholson, Nichirin won the inaugural [[vafc-championship|VAFC Championship]] in 1900 and made its World Cup debut the same year.

### The Nicholson Era

The domestic game was organized from the 1890s largely through state sports societies, whose branch clubs across the country extended the Nichirin League One beyond the metropolitan region; the league had expanded from ten clubs to twenty by 1900. The most prominent societies were Dynamo, run by the Ministry of the Interior for Manshuan police, and Spartak, for trade workers in Shinkeisei. The sport's following grew rapidly after [[dynamo-mizuhara]] became champions of the 1901 Club World Cup, becoming the first Nichirian club to win an international title. Football overtook sumo as Nichirin's most popular sport in the 1900s as sumo tournaments became increasingly a social fixture of the professional class.

Nichirin won the first twelve editions of the VAFC Championship, from 1900 to 1911, but did not advance beyond the quarter-finals in its first seven World Cups. Its principal goalscorer over the period was [[s-ichir-hidaka|Sōichirō Hidaka]], a center-forward with Tsukumo Shinkeisei who debuted in 1901. [[seignid-national-football-team|Seignid]] ended the run with successive titles in 1912 and 1913. Captained by Maglinti Catacutan, father of Seignidese defender Narra Catacutan, the side combined a sweeper with man-marking, including a designated marker for Hidaka, and won both finals 1-0 from set pieces. Nichirin had fielded a reserve side in 1912, a World Cup year, but lost the 1913 final with its first team.

In response, Nicholson restructured the team's attacking play to counter deep defenses. At the 1914 World Cup, Nichirin reached its first final and defeated the [[holy-evrian-empire-national-football-team|Holy Evrian Empire]] 1-0, Hidaka scoring the only goal, to become the first Valtherian side to win the tournament. Nicholson retired after the tournament and is known in Nichirin to this day as the "father of the national team".

### Post-Great War

Footballers received no exemption from the Shogunate's conscription during [[great-war|the Great War]]. Eighteen of the twenty-two players in the 1914 World Cup squad were called up, and seven were killed, among them Hidaka, a reserve lieutenant, in the defense of Mizuhara on July 22, 1915. The NL1 was suspended from 1914 to 1919, and neither the VAFC Championship nor the 1916 World Cup took place. Manshuan clubs were the worst affected; Dynamo Mizuhara lost most of its squad in the city's defense.

The league resumed in 1920/21, and Nichirian football was quick to recover. To replace the players lost in the war, the Bakufu funded football in schools and youth leagues from 1920, a program the army supported as a means of improving the fitness of conscripts. Over the same period, the NL1 also became increasingly commercial, with growing transfer fees and zaibatsu investment in new facilities. Nichirian clubs won four of the ten Club World Cups from 1921 to 1930, beginning with the Imperial Sports Society in 1921 and [[spartak-kanagawa]] in 1923. Dynamo Mizuhara did not win a domestic title until 1925/26, but won the Club World Cup in 1930.

Kiyoshi Tatara, a survivor from the 1914 squad, was appointed head coach in 1920 while continuing to coach Spartak Kanagawa, winners of the first three post-war league titles. His dual appointment eased disputes between the clubs and the NFA over the release of players for internationals, which had grown with the league's commercialization. At the 1924 World Cup, Nichirin reached its second final, losing 1-0 to the [[elysian-state-of-unity]]

Nichirin won seven of the ten VAFC Championships from 1921 to 1930, losing to Tonkin in 1922, Giathka in 1925 and Seignid in 1929, but did not reach another World Cup final until 1930. By then, the first players from the state youth program were beginning to enter the national team. Among them, notably, were [[sh-itoshi|Shō Itoshi]], [[luca-higashiyama]], [[k-z-fujise|Kōzō Fujise]], Kenichi Mitsui, and Taiga Bashira, a cohort of star players born in 1908 known collectively in the Nichirian press as the *Gorin* (五輪, "five rings"). At the 1930 World Cup, Tatara's side defeated [[karjanian-national-football-team|Karjania]] 2-0 in the final, with goals from Mitsui and Bashira, to win Nichirin's second title.

### 1930s

Nichirin won its third World Cup title in [[alemannia]] in 1932, beating [[hollosend-national-football-team|Hollosend]] 4-3 in the final; Itoshi was named man of the match in the quarter-final, semi-final and final. Later that year, the AFA introduced the [[afa-nations-league|Nations League]], a double-elimination tournament held as a fundraiser for the AFA Foundation. The tournament's highest-placed eligible finisher earns the right to host the following World Cup, and Nichirin's victory in the inaugural edition made it host of the 1933 World Cup. Nichirin also won the VAFC Championship, for a treble, and finished the year first in the AFA rankings.

As defending champions, Nichirin hosted the 1933 World Cup and was eliminated in the group stage without a win, finishing last in a group with Hollosend, Sidanya and Skjarnland. Tatara was dismissed after the tournament and replaced by Askurian coach [[josue-alferinho|Josué Alferinho]], under whom Nichirin retained both the Nations League and the VAFC Championship later that year.

Nichirin qualified for the 1934 World Cup in the E.S.U. without conceding a goal. At the finals, it eliminated the defending champions, Alemannia, in the round of 16, and lost the semi-final to the hosts, the eventual champions, finishing fourth after losing the third-place match to Askurias. Nichirin then won a third consecutive Nations League and the final edition of the VAFC Championship, which the AFA retired when it replaced its four confederations with two conferences for the 1935 World Cup.

[[kinshu|Kinshū]] and [[horai|Hōrai]], then Nichirian colonies, adopted Nichirin's youth-development model in the 1920s. After becoming constituent states under the [[aureate-co-prosperity-sphere|Aureate Co-Prosperity Sphere]], or Kōrin, in 1931, The two won seven of the ten PFA Championships between 1925 and 1934. Hōrai also reached the quarter-finals of the 1934 World Cup and knocked Nichirin down to the lower bracket in that year's Nations League. Mizuho and Ryūgu, also incorporated into the Kōrin in 1931, have been competitive largely on the strength of local talent; Mizuho beat Alemannia at the 1934 World Cup, and Ryūgu eliminated both Vicily and Elvester from the 1933 Nations League.

On September 30, 1934, a bomb in the dressing room at Dynamo Mizuhara's training ground, beside the Tadamune Kuronami National Stadium, killed four players from the 1934 World Cup squad: Mitsui and Bashira of the Gorin, and the defenders Akira Nagumo and Yoshikazu Takanen. The Ministry of the Interior stated that the explosion was not accidental; no arrests have been announced. Regardless, without the four, Nichirin topped the Eastern Conference's first qualifying tournament unbeaten and qualified for the 1935 World Cup in [[skjarnland]].

As of November 29, 1934, after the match against [[cahaya-national-football-team|Cahaya]] in the Eastern Conference Qualifiers for the 1935 World Cup, Nichirin under Alferinho has a record of 54 wins, 5 draws, and 6 losses, with 201 goals scored and 44 conceded with a win rate of 83.1%.

## Team Image

### Nicknames

The national team is known as the Crimson Oni Legion (赤鬼軍団, *Akaoni Gundan*, "red oni army corps"). The name originated in the Great War, when Xing troops nicknamed the Nichirian defenders of Mizuhara in July 1915 "red oni" (赤鬼, *chì guǐ*), likening them to enraged oni after some units, bloodied and out of ammunition, resorted to hand to hand combat.  Returning soldiers adopted the epithet, and veterans wearing red oni masks became a fixture at national team matches in the 1920s; by the middle of the decade, the press had applied the name to the team itself.

### Kit

Nichirin plays in navy shirts with light blue pinstripes and navy shorts and socks at home, and in light blue shirts with navy pinstripes, navy shorts and light blue socks away. The colors are identical to those of the original Takarazuka Imperial University side that represented Nichirin in 1892; the NFA retained them in 1899 under a Bakufu decree forbidding the crimson of the national flag, and the crimson chrysanthemum at its center, on sporting dress; the shirt carries the chrysanthemum in light blue at home and navy away. The decree was intended to spare the state humiliation in the event of a heavy defeat, though Nichirin has not lost a match by more than two goals after 1930. Following the Great War, all kits have been manufactured by Echigoya Bōseki (越後屋紡績), the spinning company of the Echigoya Group, under the NFA's first commercial supply contract. The pinstripes, stitched in sashiko (刺し子), the running stitch used to reinforce indigo workwear, are the company's signature.

### Crest

The current iteration of the national team's crest was adopted when [[tadamune-kuronami-national-stadium]] opened in 1925, consisting of a three-eyed oni beneath a torii on a crimson shield. The oni is a guardian figure in the tradition of onigawara (鬼瓦), the oni roof tiles that ward off evil, and its face is modeled on the guardian mask of a Kaiseido shrine in Mizuhara whose torii survived the battle of 1915. Local tradition gives the mask a third eye to watch the road eastwards, towards [[guandong]]. The crest is reserved for the NFA's banners and pennants and for the national stadium, as the flag decree bars its crimson from the playing kit.

### Home Stadium

Nichirin plays its home matches at the Tadamune Kuronami National Stadium in Mizuhara, which holds 105,000 and is shared with Dynamo Mizuhara and [[shizuku-athletic]]. Until 1925, the team had no permanent ground and rotated between the [[spartak-coliseum]] in Shinkeisei and the principal grounds of Takarazuka and Naginomiya. The stadium opened on July 22, 1925, ten years to the day after Hidaka was killed in the defense of Mizuhara, on the ground where the city's line had held next to the [[kagatsuchi-blast-furnace]], and serves as one of Nichirin's national war memorials. It is named after [[tadamune-kuronami]], who founded the [[kuronami-shogunate]] in 1336.

### Supporters

National team support is organized around ōendan (応援団), cheer squads descended from the university ōendan that followed the Takarazuka Imperial University side in 1892. Each is led by a *danchō* (団長) in a black student uniform who conducts the stands with a war fan, accompanied by taiko drums and oversized banners, and regional chapters travel with the team abroad. The red oni mask worn by the veterans of the 1920s has become standard dress for supporters. At home matches, the crowd chants "Oni wa uchi!" (鬼は内, "oni in!"), an inversion of the Setsubun cry "Oni wa soto!" ("oni out!").

### Rivalries

The Xing Empire, Nichirin's first international opponent, was its principal rival before the Great War. The teams met eleven times between 1892 and 1913, with five wins for Nichirin, two draws and four defeats, the last in 1911. From 1921, the fixture was contested against Guandong, Xing's successor state, with which Nichirin normalized relations at the [[t-hara-conference|Tōhara Conference]] in March 1926. Against Guandong, Nichirin has won twelve of thirteen meetings and drawn the other; the closest was a 2-1 win in the round of 16 of the 1932 World Cup.

Nichirin's principal modern rival is Alemannia, a treaty ally under the Mizuhara Pentapartite; the Nichirian press calls the fixture the Dōmei-sen (同盟戦, "Alliance Match"). Each has won the World Cup in the other's country, Nichirin in Alemannia in 1932 and Alemannia in Nichirin in 1933, and Nichirin eliminated Alemannia as holders at the 1934 World Cup. At the club level, Nichirian sides beat Alemannian club [[arminia-berelstein]] in three different Club World Cup finals: 1929, 1930 and 1933.

The rivalry with neighboring Rudania, a communist state, is primarily political. The teams last met at the 1932 World Cup, where Nichirin won their group-stage match 2-1. In 1933, Nichirin, as host, barred Rudania from the World Cup after it was revealed that Rudania was harboring [[majesty]], a former Elysian naval intelligence unit operating as a private military contractor; the exclusion was imposed by the Bakufu independently of the AFA. The NFA has since declined to host the fixture.

## Records

Takeru Mikado holds the record for appearances, with 216 caps since his debut in 1918; defender Daigo Takanashi and goalkeeper Eisuke Sato are next, with 179 and 178. Sōichirō Hidaka has been the record goalscorer since 1914, with 91 goals in 103 matches. The closest active players are forwards Ganzorig Erdene, with 52 goals, and Shō Itoshi, with 46.
