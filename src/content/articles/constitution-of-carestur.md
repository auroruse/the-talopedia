## Constitution of Carestur

### Constitution of Carestur

The **Constitution of Carestur** (*Constitucion de Carestur* in Caresturian; *Constitution de Carestur* in Septimanian), formally adopted on **1 June 1934**, is the supreme foundational law of the **Republic of Carestur** and the charter of the **Fourth Republic**. Drafted at the **Constitutional Convention of Tolosa** following the collapse of the **Third Caresturian Republic**, it replaced the parliamentary system established by the **Constitutional Laws of 1875** with a centralized, unitary semi-presidential framework.

Designed to end decades of ministerial instability and legislative gridlock, the 1934 charter pairs a directly elected presidency and rationalized parliamentary rules with technocratic oversight over fiscal and constitutional competence, while establishing the constitutional basis for the self-governing **Autonomous Territory of Numidia**.

### Historical Background and Drafting

Under the Constitutional Laws of 1875, supreme authority had rested with the **Third Caresturian Assembly**, reducing the head of state to a largely ceremonial role and leaving cabinets vulnerable to shifting parliamentary coalitions. Following the Great War, sovereign debt crises, postwar inflation, and industrial strike waves repeatedly paralyzed the legislature. By the early 1930s, street clashes between militant labor syndicates and uniformed nationalist leagues;coupled with mounting unrest in Numidia;eroded confidence in the 1875 institutional order.

In response, a cross-partisan coalition of constitutional jurists, reformist economists, and senior civil engineers formed the **Meridian Renewal Movement**. Convening in Tolosa in the spring of 1934, the delegates of the **Constitutional Convention of Tolosa** drafted a single codified charter to replace the fragmented constitutional acts of the Third Republic. The completed text entered into force on **1 June 1934**, formally inaugurating the Fourth Republic.

## Structure and Provisions

The Constitution is organized into a **Preamble** and **Eight Titles**, establishing the principles of national sovereignty, the separation of powers, and the territorial structure of the Republic.

### Preamble and Title I: Sovereignity and the Republic

The Preamble affirms the republican traditions of the 1818 unification while emphasizing civic order, national solidarity, and constitutional continuity. **Title I** defines Carestur as an indivisible, secular, democratic, and social republic with **Tolosa** as its permanent seat of government. It designates **Caresturian** and **Septimanian** as the official languages of the Republic while providing constitutional recognition for regional linguistic heritage and statutory co-officiality for **Arabic** and **Berber** within Autonomous Numidia.

### Title II: The President of the Republic

**Title II** transforms the presidency from a ceremonial office into an active pillar of executive authority:

**Election and Term:** The President of the Republic is elected by direct universal suffrage for a **five-year term**.

**Constitutional Powers:** The President serves as guarantor of national independence, territorial integrity, and observance of the Constitution; presides over the Council of Ministers; commands the armed forces; negotiates and ratifies treaties; and appoints the Prime Minister.

**Dissolution Power:** To prevent protracted parliamentary deadlock, the President holds the constitutional authority to dissolve the National Assembly and call new legislative elections.

### Title III: The Government

**Title III** vests the direction of national policy and administration in the **Government**, headed by the **Prime Minister**. While appointed by the President, the Prime Minister and Cabinet remain collectively responsible to the National Assembly, creating a dual-executive structure in which the Government must maintain parliamentary confidence to legislate while operating under presidential arbitration.

### Title IV: The Parliament

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/GI8dmm0.jpeg" alt="***The semicircular hemicycle of the Assemblada Nacionala in Tolosa***"><figcaption>***The semicircular hemicycle of the Assemblada Nacionala in Tolosa***</figcaption></figure>

**Title IV** establishes a bicameral **Parliament of Carestur** consisting of two chambers:

**National Assembly (***Assemblada Nacionala*): The lower chamber of **577 deputies**, elected directly for five-year terms, holding primary legislative initiative and the power to censure the Government.

**Council of the Republic (***Conselh de la Republica*): The upper chamber of **348 councillors**, elected indirectly to represent metropolitan regions and territorial communities, serving staggered six-year terms divided into two regional series.

### Title V: Relations between Parlianment and Government

**Title V** introduces rules of rationalized parliamentarism intended to prevent the rapid cabinet collapses of the Third Republic. It defines the respective domains of statutory law and executive decree, grants the Government priority in setting the legislative agenda, restricts motions of censure to formal recorded votes, and imposes strict procedural limits on private members' bills that would increase public expenditure or reduce state revenue without offsetting funding.

### Title VI: The *Conselh Superior de la Competéncia*

One of the signature innovations of the 1934 charter, **Title VI** creates the *Conselh Superior de la Competéncia*, an independent constitutional and technical body composed of senior jurists, administrative magistrates, and appointed financial and engineering specialists. Before promulgation, organic laws, budgetary measures, and contested bills may be referred to the Council to review their constitutional validity, jurisdictional competence, and compliance with statutory fiscal-balance rules. Title VI also codifies the institutional autonomy of the **Bank of Carestur** in safeguarding monetary stability.

### Title VII: Territorial Organization and Autonomous Numidia

**Title VII** affirms the unitary administrative structure of Metropolitan Carestur while creating a distinct constitutional category for overseas territories. Under these provisions, Numidia was reorganized from a directly administered colony into a self-governing **Autonomous Territory**, governed in detail by an organic statute;the **Act of Algernon** of 23 January 1936;which established its 180-seat bilingual **Territorial Assembly**, dual-college electoral rules, the office of the **High Commissioner**, and statutory firewalls separating electoral parties from trade unions and paramilitary leagues.

### Title VIII: Constitutional Revision

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://i.imgur.com/hpKXSJr.jpeg" alt="***Palace of the Conselh Superior de la Competéncia***"><figcaption>***Palace of the Conselh Superior de la Competéncia***</figcaption></figure>

**Title VIII** governs amendments to the Constitution. A revision bill must first be passed in identical terms by both the National Assembly and the Council of the Republic, and subsequently ratified either by a three-fifths supermajority of both chambers convened together in joint session in Tolosa or by direct national referendum. The republican form of government and the territorial integrity of the Republic are explicitly excluded from revision.
