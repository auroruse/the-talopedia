**Carestur**, formally the **Republic of Carestur**, is a country located in Western Evria and North Africa. Bounded by the Gulf of Euscara to the west and Mare Internum to the south, it borders Arverne to the north, Vicily to the east, Askurias to the southwest, and El Maghreb through the Autonomous Territory of Numidia. With a mainland population of 26 million people, as well as 50 million population of its autonomous overseas territory of Numidia. Carestur is a unitary semi-presidential republic governed under technocratic oversight by the *Conseil Supérieur de la Compétence*, with mainland Carestur divided into 14 provinces. The capital of Carestur is Tolosa with a population of roughly 720 thousand making it the 3rd biggest city in the nation, while its biggest city, Barcino, has a population exceeding 3.9 million people.

## Ethymology

**Etymology**

The name **Carestur** derives from the **Caresturi**, an ancient confederation of coastal and **Monts Albères** *(or **Cévenol-Alberian**)* tribes that inhabited the littoral of the **Mare Internum** and the mountain passes between the Garonne. The ethnonym itself originates from the Proto-Celtic root **kar-* ("stone" or "crag") combined with the Old Meridian suffix *-estur* ("dwellers" or "people of the coast"), translating roughly to "people of the rocky coast." While the English exonym uses the shortened **-tur** ending, in Caresturian and the regional Romance languages it often takes the classical adjectival form **Caresturien** (*Caresturian*).

The name *Caresturia* was first officially recorded in the administrative archives of the Western Imperial Prefectures during the 4th Century to designate the maritime provinces linking southern Gaul and Septimania. During the Middle Ages, the term survived primarily as a scholarly and cartographic designation for the shared Occitan-Septimanian cultural sphere before being revived by civic nationalists in the late 18th century.

The state itself was unified over a century earlier in **1818** following the merger of the Occitan Directorate, the Septimanian Commonwealth, and the Maritime Prefecture of Provence under the Treaty of 
Pestelliére, before adopting its current constitutional name and framework as the **Republic of Carestur** on **1 June 1934**.

## History

### Prehistory and Antiquity

Early humans inhabited Carestur at least 550,000 years ago, with the earliest archaic hominin remains discovered in the Tautavel-Corbières caves near the Monts Albères. Similarly ancient traces of modern humans have been uncovered in the Cévennes highlands and the Garonne basin, including bone flutes, ochre cave murals, and carved limestone figurines. By the first millennium BC, the region was populated by the Caresturi;a confederation of Celto-Ligurian and Iberian-speaking tribes;alongside Greek and Phoenician maritime trading colonies established along the coast of the *Mare Internum*.

During the 2nd and 1st centuries BC, the Rumaic Empire systematically annexed the territories between the Garonne, the Rhône, and the Ebro, organizing the mainland into the prosperous imperial provinces of Gallia Narbonensis and Tarraconensis, while simultaneously subjugating the Berber kingdoms across the *Mare Internum* in Numidia. Under Rumaic rule, urban centers such as Narbo, Tolosa, Barcino, and Massillia flourished as hubs of maritime trade, Rumaic law, and engineering. Following the crisis of the 3rd century and the collapse of Rumaic frontier defenses in the early 5th century AD, Visigothic and Burgundian federates settled across the mainland, establishing the Kingdom of Tolosa and later the Septimanian March, while coastal Numidia fragmented into autonomous Romano-Berber principalities.

### Middle Ages and Early Modern Era

In 795, following centuries of frontier warfare between northern Frankish kingdoms and southern emirates, the March of Septimania and the Occitan duchies were consolidated into a network of semi-independent feudal realms, most notably the County of Tolosa, the Duchy of Aquitaine, and the County of Barcino. The High Middle Ages saw a golden age of Occitan-Catalan urban culture, troubadour literature, and Mediterranean maritime commerce, alongside the rise of distinct regional religious currents;most notably Gallican Solarism and early reformist movements that resisted centralizing northern ecclesiastical authority.

In the 13th century, northern crusades and dynastic wars devastated the Languedoc and Provence, forcing the southern lords into loose vassalage under neighboring crowns while leaving the region's municipal charters (*consulates*) and regional parliaments (*Estats*) largely intact. Between the 15th and 17th centuries, Caresturian lands were repeatedly convulsed by the Gallican Religious Wars (1562–1598) and the Pyrenean Conflicts, which pitted Solarist and Civic Rationalist urban communes against orthodox royalist forces. The Edict of Narbonne (1604) finally established religious coexistence across the southern provinces, granting broad municipal autonomy to coastal cities and strengthening the merchant oligarchies of Marseille, Bordeaux, and Barcelona.

### The 1818 Unification and Long Nineteenth Century

During the late 18th century, revolutionary upheavals swept across Western Evria, toppling the old feudal estates and giving rise to three sister republics across the southern littoral: the **Occitan Directorate** in the west, the **Septimanian Commonwealth** in the south, and the **Maritime Prefecture of Provence** in the east. Though initially allied during the Continental Wars, the three states soon realized that economic fragmentation left them vulnerable to larger northern and Iberian powers. On **14 September 1818**, representatives of the three republics signed the **Treaty of** 
**Pestelliére**, formally merging into a single sovereign state;initially styled the **United Provinces of Carestur**.

Throughout the 19th century, Carestur industrialized rapidly around the textile mills of Languedoc, the shipyards of Barciona and Bordigal, and the metallurgy hubs of Septimania. In the 1840s and 1850s, seeking both strategic control over the western *Mare Internum* and outlets for industrial capital, Caresturian naval expeditions annexed the coastal ports and Tellian highlands of **Numidia**, establishing it as an overseas colony before gradually granting it territorial representation. However, the late 19th century was marked by sharp political gridlock between agrarian federalists in the interior and industrial republicans in the coastal metropolises, resulting in revolving-door cabinets and repeated constitutional crises.

### The Great War and the 1934 Constitutional Reform

**The Great War and the 1934 Constitutional Reform**
The outbreak of the Great War in 1914 severely tested the fragile parliamentary system. Mobilizing millions of troops from both the Evrian mainland and Numidia, Carestur endured grueling trench warfare along its northern and Alpine frontiers. While the war ended inconclusively in 1918, the human toll, hyperinflation, and post-war industrial strikes paralyzed the Third Caresturian Assembly throughout the 1920s.

As regional instability and authoritarian movements spread across Europe in the early 1930s, a coalition of reformist economists, civil engineers, and constitutional jurists led the **Meridian Renewal Movement** to prevent state collapse. Following the Constitutional Convention of Tolosa in the spring of 1934, the old parliamentary constitution was abolished. On **1 June 1934**, the state was officially reconstituted as the **Republic of Carestur;** a centralized, unitary semi-presidential republic incorporating the *Conseil Supérieur de la Compétence* to insulate national infrastructure, fiscal policy, and long-term planning from partisan deadlock, while reorganizing Numidia into a self-governing Autonomous Territory.

## Military

The **Caresturian Armed Forces** (Caresturian: *Forces Armées Caresturiennes*, **FAC**) are the unified military forces of the Republic of Carestur, consisting of three primary service branches: the **Caresturian Army** (*Armée de Terre Caresturienne*), the **Caresturian Navy** (*Marine Nationale Caresturienne*), and the **Caresturian Air Force** (*Armée de l'Air Caresturienne*). Under the 1934 Constitution, the President of the Republic serves as Commander-in-Chief (*Chef des Armées*), while strategic planning, procurement, and defense doctrine are jointly overseen by the Ministry of Defense and the Defense Directorate of the *Conseil Supérieur de la Compétence*. In the event of a declared State of Emergency or full mobilization, operational command transfers directly to the Chief of the General Staff (*Chef d'État-Major des Armées*).

The Caresturian Armed Forces maintain an active strength of **430,000 personnel** (approximately **1.3% of the total population**), comprising **255,000** in the Army, **98,000** in the Navy, and **77,000** in the Air Force, supported by an additional **310,000** active reservists. The annual defense budget stands at $**39.5 billion** (**3.8% of GDP**), reflecting heavy capital investment in blue-water and littoral naval projection across the *Mare Internum* and the Gulf of Euscara, coastal air defense networks, and mechanized border fortifications. Universal civic-military conscription applies to all citizens at age 19 for a mandatory period of **9 months**, with options to fulfill service in either active military units or technical civil-defense corps.

The modern *Forces Armées Caresturiennes* were reorganized in **July 1934** following the Constitutional Reform of 1934, which dissolved the separate provincial militias and integrated the former colonial *Armée de Numidie* directly into the unified national command structure. While the reorganized force has not fought a full-scale continental war since 1918, its expeditionary and naval units saw extensive action during the 19th-century Numidian Campaigns, the Great War (1914–1918), and the Mare Internum Maritime Policing Operations of the late 1920s. Due to its strategic position bridging Western Evria and South Evria, Carestur maintains an armed neutrality doctrine backed by strong maritime deterrence and bilateral transit agreements with neighboring coastal states.

## Government

The Republic of Carestur is a unitary, semi-presidential technocratic republic established following the adoption of the Constitution of 1934. This constitution created a centralized system of governance built upon popular sovereignty, administrative efficiency, and institutional competence. Political authority is concentrated within the central government in Tolosa, though wide cultural and administrative autonomy is granted to the Autonomous Territory of Numidia. Executive power is shared between the President of the Republic and the Prime Minister, who leads the Council of Ministers (*Conseil des Ministres*). Directly elected by popular vote for a five-year term, the President serves as head of state, commander-in-chief of the armed forces, and guarantor of constitutional stability, exercising primary authority over foreign policy, defense, and strategic state appointments. The Prime Minister, appointed by the President with the confidence of the legislature, directs domestic administration through national ministries responsible for the interior, finance, industry and energy, infrastructure, agriculture, justice, and education and culture.

Legislation is vested in the National Parliament (*Parlement National*), a bicameral legislature consisting of the National Assembly (Assemblée Nationale) and the Council of the Republic (Conseil de la République). The National Assembly serves as the lower house and represents the citizenry directly through proportional representation, holding primary authority over national budgets and votes of confidence. The Council represents the mainland provinces and Autonomous Numidia, reviewing legislation to ensure territorial balance. Distinguishing Carestur's constitutional order is the *Conseil Supérieur de la Compétence* (Supreme Council of Competence), an independent technocratic oversight body composed of appointed economists, engineers, jurists, and public administrators. While it does not initiate ordinary partisan legislation, the Council audits fiscal sustainability, certifies technical feasibility on major infrastructure and economic bills, and holds a suspensive veto over laws deemed fiscally or structurally reckless, which Parliament can only override by a two-thirds supermajority.

The judiciary is fully independent of the executive and legislative branches. The Supreme Court of Cassation (*Cour Suprême de Cassation*) serves as the highest judicial instance for civil and criminal appeals, while the Constitutional Court (*Cour Constitutionnelle*) adjudicates constitutional disputes, electoral validity, and fundamental rights. Courts operate at national, provincial, and municipal levels, with judges appointed through competitive meritocratic examinations and confirmed by an independent judicial council.

Administratively, mainland Carestur is divided into 14 provinces, each overseen by a centrally appointed Prefect (*Préfet*) alongside an elected Provincial Council responsible for local infrastructure, regional transport, and cultural affairs. The central government retains exclusive authority over national defense, diplomacy, currency, customs, taxation, and national infrastructure, while Autonomous Numidia operates under a special constitutional statute granting it an elected Territorial Assembly, local policing powers, and co-official linguistic administration.

## Economy

The Republic of Carestur is a high-income mixed-market technocratic economy. Anchored by its strategic position astride both the *Mare Internum* and the Euscara coast, Carestur possesses a total national GDP estimated at **$1.53 trillion** (approximately **$57,985** across the mainland, or **$38,450 per capita** nationwide when accounting for Autonomous Numidia). The nation maintains a low unemployment rate of around **3.9%** and stable, controlled inflation, supported by strict fiscal auditing from the *Conseil Supérieur de la Compétence*, a broad progressive tax base, and disciplined monetary policy managed by the *Banque de Carestur*. The Caresturian economic model combines competitive private enterprise with strategic state planning and public ownership in critical infrastructure, energy grids, rail networks, and maritime ports, balancing industrial productivity with universal social welfare.

The economy is heavily diversified and modern, with the service sector accounting for approximately **44%** of GDP, driven by maritime logistics, international trade, banking, telecommunications, and tourism centered in major urban hubs such as Massilia, Barcino, Tolosa, Bordigal. The industrial and manufacturing sector contributes roughly **38%** to GDP, anchored in the Rhône-Lyonnais industrial corridor, the Septimania metallurgical and textile belt, and the Euscara aerospace and shipbuilding complexes. Agriculture, forestry, viticulture, and mineral extraction account for the remaining **18%** of GDP, concentrated in the Garonne basin, the Languedoc plains, the Cévennes highlands, and the Tellian agricultural belt and phosphate mines of Autonomous Numidia. This primary sector is supported by extensive state-backed irrigation networks, cooperative mechanization, and high-speed freight corridors.

Key economic pillars include shipbuilding, naval engineering, and petrochemical refining in Massilia, Tolosa, and Bordigal; metallurgy, textiles, automotive manufacturing, and precision machinery in Barciona, Martelon, and Tolosa; banking, insurance, and maritime brokerage in Massilia, Barciona, and Canas; and large-scale viticulture, olive oil, grain, and citrus production across Occitania, Septimania, and Numidia, alongside phosphate and iron ore extraction in the Numidian interior that supplies domestic heavy industry and generates substantial export surpluses.

### Labor Force

The domestic labor force of mainland Carestur totals approximately **14.8 million workers**, representing roughly **57%** of the mainland population, alongside an additional **3.4 million** economically active citizens in Autonomous Numidia. Across the mainland, **17%** of the working population is employed in the primary sector (agriculture, viticulture, fishing, forestry, and mining), **39%** in the secondary sector (manufacturing, shipbuilding, metallurgy, textiles, chemicals, and construction), and **44%** in the tertiary sector (maritime transport, banking, retail, public administration, and technical services). The national unemployment rate stands at **3.9%**, down from **6.4%** during the post-war stagnation of the mid-1920s, driven by aggressive state-led electrification, railway modernization, and port expansion initiatives launched under the 1934 constitutional reforms. Major state-directed enterprises, cooperative agro-industrial syndicates, and private manufacturing conglomerates employ millions across the Rhône valley, the Septimanian coast, and the Garonne basin.

A central challenge for Caresturian labor planners is the steady demographic shift from the rural interior of the Massif Central, the Monts Albères, and the Numidian highlands toward the booming coastal and riverine metropolises of Massilia, Barcino, Canas, Tolosa, and Bordigal. While rapid urbanization has fueled industrial output and port capacity, it has created seasonal labor shortages in inland agriculture and housing strain in major coastal cities. In response, the *Conseil Supérieur de la Compétence* has prioritized rural electrification, mechanized farming subsidies, and decentralized technical institutes to sustain productivity outside the urban core. Cross-border labor migration remains tightly regulated through merit-based skill quotas, with most inter-territorial movement occurring internally between Autonomous Numidia and the mainland's industrial, construction, and maritime shipping sectors.

## Geography

### Topography

**Geography**

**Topography**
The Republic of Carestur covers approximately **227,039 square kilometers** across its Evrian mainland and approximately 
**719,248** **square kilometers** in the Autonomous Territory of Numidia, bringing its total land area to **946,287 square kilometers** (alongside an internal water area of roughly **13,000 km²**). Encompassing a geographically diverse and strategically pivotal territory bridging Western Evria and South Evria, Carestur's topography is characterized by rugged mountain massifs, broad alluvial river basins, coastal lagoons, and semi-arid interior plateaus. The mainland stretches from the Arctic Sea and the Gulf of Euscara in the west to the *Mare Internum* in the south and east, with the Garonne, Rhône, Aude, and Ebro river systems shaping much of its historical settlement and agricultural structure.

The central and western borders of the mainland are anchored by the **Monts Albères** (the eastern Pyrenean range) and the **Cévennes-Auvergne** highlands, which form imposing natural barriers separating the coastal plains from the continental interior. Peaks in the Monts Albères and the eastern Alpine fringes frequently exceed **2,800 meters**, marked by glacial valleys, high mountain passes, and swift rivers that supply extensive hydroelectric power to the industrial grid. Along the *Mare Internum*, the terrain transitions from the fertile, lagoon-lined plains of Languedoc and Roussillon to the rocky cliffs and deep natural harbors of Provence, the detached coastal enclave of Cannes, and the Catalan coast. These coastlines make Carestur a major dual-ocean maritime power, with commercial and naval ports concentrated in Marseille, Toulon, Barcelona, and Bordeaux.

Across the *Mare Internum*, the **Autonomous Territory of Numidia** is defined by three distinct geographical belts running parallel to the coast: the fertile, hilly **Tellian littoral**, where most of Numidia's population, ports, and agriculture are concentrated; the rugged **Tellian and Saharan Atlas** mountain ranges, which rise above **2,300 meters** and hold rich mineral deposits; and the high, semi-arid **Chotts Plateau**, a steppe region dotted with seasonal salt lakes (*chotts*) that transitions toward the northern desert frontier. While the high mountain zones and interior steppes remain sparsely populated, settlements cluster densely along the coastal plains, river valleys, and major port cities.

The western Euscara lowlands, which span from the Gironde estuary along the Gulf of Euscara to the foothills of the western Pyrenees, consist of broad alluvial plains, pine forests, coastal dunes, and reclaimed marshlands. These regions are traversed by the river systems of the Lower Garonne, Dordogne, Adour, and Charente, creating some of Carestur's most productive agricultural and viticultural land alongside dense port settlements. Centuries of coastal drainage, pine afforestation in the Landes, and canal engineering;most notably the *Canal des Deux Mers* linking the Euscara to the *Mare Internum;* have transformed the western littoral into an integrated corridor for inland navigation, trade, and industrial development.

The northern and central-eastern regions, stretching from the Massif Central and Auvergne through the Rhône-Saône valley to the Alpine foothills, are characterized by rolling volcanic plateaus, fertile river valleys, mixed deciduous forests, and industrial basins. Significant coal, iron ore, and bauxite deposits in the Cévennes, Loire basin, and Provence form the foundation for heavy metallurgy, chemical manufacturing, and engineering, while the Rhône and Saône river systems provide vital north–south freight transport and irrigation. The combination of mineral wealth, hydroelectric generation, and dense railway corridors has made the Rhône-Lyonnais and Languedoc belts the mainland's primary heartlands for heavy industry and manufacturing.

Dense forests of pine, oak, and chestnut cover large parts of the Landes, the Cévennes, the Monts Albères, and the Provençal uplands, while intensive agricultural plains characterize the Garonne Basin, the Languedoc littoral, the Roussillon plain, and the Septimian coastal valleys. Major urban centers are concentrated along river confluences, deep-water harbors, and historic Rumaic trade routes, where favorable geography has fostered dense population and maritime commerce. Mountainous and high-plateau zones remain comparatively sparsely populated, serving primarily forestry, hydroelectric generation, pastoralism, and seasonal tourism.

### Climate

**Climate**

Carestur has a predominantly Mediterranean and temperate oceanic climate, with pronounced regional variations shaped by altitude, maritime currents, and its position bridging the Euscara, the *Mare Internum*, and South Evria. Climate zones range from temperate oceanic (*Cfb*) in the western Atlantic lowlands, through hot-summer and warm-summer Mediterranean (*Csa*/*Csb*) along the southern littoral and in northern Numidia, to alpine (*Dfc*) in the Monts Albères and semi-arid steppe (*BSk*/*BSh*) in the Numidian interior.

The western coastal provinces along the Gulf of Euscara, including Gironde-Landes and western Gascogne, experience a maritime-oceanic climate. Winters are mild and wet, summers are warm, and precipitation is frequent throughout the year due to prevailing westerly winds from the Atlantic. These steady maritime breezes moderate seasonal temperature swings, supporting lush forests and fertile river basins.

The southern and eastern mainland provinces bordering the *Mare Internum;* including Languedoc, Roussillon, Provence, the Cannes enclave, and Catalonia;have a classic Mediterranean climate characterized by hot, dry summers and mild, wetter winters. Seasonal weather is strongly influenced by regional wind systems, notably the cold, dry *Mistral* and *Tramontane* winds funneling down the Rhône and Aude valleys, which clear skies and regulate summer humidity. In the central interior plateaus of the Massif Central and Auvergne, the climate transitions to a cooler, semi-continental regime with sharper seasonal contrasts, cold winters with regular snowfall, and warm summers punctuated by afternoon thunderstorms.

The Monts Albères and the eastern Alpine borderlands feature a mountain and alpine climate, marked by long, snowy winters, cool summers, and heavy precipitation. Persistent winter snowpack in the upper valleys feeds the headwaters of Carestur's major rivers, sustaining summer irrigation and hydroelectric power generation. Across the *Mare Internum*, the Autonomous Territory of Numidia exhibits a sharp north–south climatic gradient: the Tellian coast enjoys a mild, rain-fed Mediterranean climate similar to southern Provence, while the interior Chotts Plateau and Saharan Atlas transition rapidly into a semi-arid steppe climate with scorching summers, chilly winter nights, and scarce, irregular rainfall.

Annual precipitation across Carestur varies dramatically by region, ranging from **900 to 1,600 mm** in the Euscarian lowlands, the Monts Albères, and the Cévennes highlands, down to **550–750 mm** along the *Mare Internum* littoral, and dropping below **300 mm** in the semi-arid plateaus of southern Numidia. Average January temperatures on the mainland range from **2 to 5 °C** in the interior plateaus and **7 to 10 °C** along the southern coast, rising to **22–28 °C** in July, while coastal Numidia averages **11–13 °C** in winter and frequently exceeds **32 °C** during the peak of summer.

### Human Geography

According to the latest national census, the Republic of Carestur has an estimated **26 million** inhabitants across its European mainland, alongside approximately 50 **million** residents in the Autonomous Territory of Numidia, bringing the total national population to roughly 76 **million** people. Population density across the republic is notably uneven, reflecting centuries of coastal trade, riverine industrialization, and rugged upland topography. The highest population concentrations are found along the littoral of the *Mare Internum* and the major river corridors;most notably the **Barcelone-Métropole** urban belt, the **Marseille–Toulon** maritime basin, the **Rhône-Lyonnais** industrial corridor around Lyon, the **Toulouse–Garonne** basin, the **Bordeaux–Gironde** estuary on the Atlantic coast, and the densely urbanized coast of **Canas**.

In contrast, the rugged interior uplands and mountain zones;such as the **Cévennes-Auvergne** highlands, the **Monts Albères**, the rural interior plains of **Gascogne**, and the semi-arid **Chotts Plateau** and **Saharan Atlas** ranges of southern Numidia;remain significantly more sparsely populated, with settlement largely concentrated in historic market towns and fertile agricultural valleys.

## Demographics

### Ethnic Groups

Due to the large population of its South Evrian territory, the Republic of Carestur is a pluralistic, bi-continental nation without a single ethnic majority nationwide. **Numidians** constitute the largest single demographic group at **61.8%** of the total national population (and over **93%** within Autonomous Numidia), followed by **Caresturians** at **32.9%** nationwide (forming over 84% of the Evrian mainland population). Within the mainland Caresturian population, citizens further identify with their historic regional heritage, led by **Occitans** (**19.1%** of the total population), **Septimanians** (including Catalans and Roussillonese, at **11.1%**), and **Gallo-Rhodanians** (**2.7%**).

Smaller recognized communities include **Caresturian-Numidians** (*Meridians*, at **2.6%**), who form a historic urban minority in Numidian coastal cities; **Basques** (**0.4%**) along the western Pyrenean frontier; historic **Sephardic, Maghrebi, and Provençal Jewish** communities (**0.8%**) centered in major coastal ports across both shores of the *Mare Internum*; and **Italian, Iberian, and other Mediterranean diaspora** populations (**1.5%**) concentrated in industrial and maritime hubs.

### Languages

Linguistically, **Standard Caresturian** (*Caresturien Standard*, a codified Occitano-Romance language rooted in Languedocien and Provençal) serves as the primary national language of the Evrian mainland and is used nationwide for federal administration, diplomacy, higher education, and national media. Alongside it, **Numidian** (*Tamazight* / *Numidien*, standardized in the Neo-Tifinagh and Latin scripts) holds full co-official status across the republic and serves as the primary language of administration, education, and daily life for the 50 million residents of the Autonomous Territory of Numidia, while **Septimanian** (Catalan) is recognized as an official regional language across the southern coastal provinces. All national laws, constitutional texts, and state broadcasts are published in both Standard Caresturian and Numidian, with Septimanian editions provided throughout the southern provinces.

Depending on their region of origin, most Caresturian citizens are bilingual or trilingual. On the mainland, the majority of urban residents speak Standard Caresturian alongside a regional dialect or Septimanian, while in Autonomous Numidia, widespread bilingualism in Numidian and Standard Caresturian—as well as **Maghrebi Arabic**, which holds recognized regional status in coastal and eastern Numidian municipalities—facilitates commerce and administrative integration across the *Mare Internum*. Recognized local vernaculars on the mainland include **Gascon**, **Provençal**, **Auvergnat**, and **Basque** in the western Monts Albères, which enjoy constitutional protection and are used in municipal administration, local schools, and cultural broadcasting.

### Religion

The Republic of Carestur is a constitutionally secular state governed by the principle of *laïcité républicaine*, guaranteeing full freedom of conscience while reflecting the distinct religious traditions of its Evrian and South Evrian territories. Nationwide, **Sunni Islam** (predominantly of the Maliki school) is the largest religious affiliation at **58.4%** of the total population, concentrated almost entirely within the Autonomous Territory of Numidia alongside urban diaspora communities on the mainland. 

**Gallician Solarism**

 represents the second-largest faith group at **28.7%** nationwide (and over 48% across the European mainland), divided primarily between 
**Civic Rationalism**

Secular, agnostic, and non-religious citizens account for 4**0.6%** of the national population, concentrated heavily in major mainland industrial and academic centers such as Marsillia, Barcino, Brodigal, and Tolosa, as well as among the urban technocratic class. Historic **Judaism** (encompassing both Sephardic, Provençal, and Maghrebi traditions) makes up approximately **0.9%** of the population, with long-established communities in Marseille, Barcelona, Bordeaux, Montpellier, and coastal Numidia, while **Ibadi Islam** (in the M'zab oases of southern Numidia) and other minority faiths account for the remaining **1.4%**.

### Citizenship

Caresturian citizenship is granted primarily according to the principle of *jus sanguinis* (right of blood), meaning that a person's nationality is determined by the citizenship of at least one parent rather than their place of birth. While a child born on Caresturian territory to foreign parents does not automatically acquire citizenship at birth, the Nationality Code of 1934 provides structured *jus soli* exceptions: a child born in either mainland Carestur or the Autonomous Territory of Numidia acquires citizenship automatically upon reaching the age of majority (18) if they have resided continuously in the republic for at least **8 years**, or earlier if at least one parent has held legal permanent residency for more than **10 years** prior to the birth.

Naturalization for foreign nationals is administered through a meritocratic, points-based evaluation overseen jointly by the Ministry of the Interior and the Civic Integration Directorate of the *Conseil Supérieur de la Compétence*. Applicants must demonstrate at least **7 years** of continuous legal residency, financial self-sufficiency, a clean criminal record, and certified proficiency in either **Standard Caresturian** or **Numidian**, alongside passing a civic and constitutional examination. Dual citizenship is permitted under Caresturian law, provided the applicant's country of origin maintains a bilateral recognition treaty with the Republic.
