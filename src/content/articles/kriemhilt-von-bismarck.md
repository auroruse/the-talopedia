**Hildegard Kriemhilt Adelheid von Bismarck** (クリームヒルト・フォン・ビスマルク; born February 14, 1901) is an [[alemannia|Alemannian]] politician serving under Shogun [[masashi-miyamoto]] as High Commissioner of the [[aureate-co-prosperity-sphere|Aureate Co-Prosperity Sphere]] (光輪共栄圏, *Kōrin Kyōeiken*), the self-governance framework she designed to replace direct Nichirian colonial rule, and, since June 1934, as de facto Minister of Foreign Affairs. Appointed Minister of Colonial Affairs at twenty-three, she was the youngest ministerial appointment of Masashi's tenure and remains the only member of the cabinet with no Nichirian parentage.

## Early Life and Education

<figure class="float float-left" style="width:160px"><img src="https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNMahorQ7UAviM8HKS6vKEuMXgl6k3HtKNJopRcr3v-23JAiSI3lMU41BmmiP-Tqs_PZtpluNp9q-b_02pr3iV7ib6na2z4NoPhUIJC-kl9U3JhztUGlH0YPGmefcm8PT1ZVP7vOT_7FaTK-QxxBxTw=w2000" alt="Alexander and Kriemhilt, January 1903."><figcaption>Alexander and Kriemhilt, January 1903.</figcaption></figure>

Hildegard Kriemhilt Adelheid von Bismarck was born on February 14, 1901, in [[akenau]], the capital of the Kingdom of [[ymiria]], then a constituent state of the [[holy-evrian-empire]]. Von Bismarck is the daughter of [[alexander-von-bismarck]], a Ymirian noble with longstanding ties to the Nichirian government, and Wilhelmina von Thurn, a daughter of the House of Thurn. The Thurns held the hereditary office of Imperial Postmaster General until the empire's dissolution in 1805 and afterward held the telegraph concession for Ymiria; Wilhelmina managed the concession's Akenau office.

Von Bismarck was raised at her father's estate outside Akenau and spent much of her childhood in her mother's telegraph office, where she learned the Ymirian telegraph codes and the routing of the imperial network. She has described a visit to the crypt of [[akenau-cathedral]] with her father at the age of seven, during which she questioned him about the load-bearing structure of its vaulting, as the origin of her interest in engineering. Although christened Hildegard, she has been known by her second given name, Kriemhilt, since the age of nine, at her own insistence. During the Great War, she worked evenings in her mother's office, which relayed casualty lists from the Ymirian southern front to Akenau.

Her father had intended her to attend a finishing school, but von Bismarck applied instead to the [[koniglich-ymirische-polytechnische-schule|Königlich Ymirische Polytechnische Schule]] (KYPS), which trained officials for the Ymirian colonial service and had admitted women to its colonial administration program since 1915, after wartime conscription depleted its enrollment. The school rejected her first application in 1916 on account of her age, and she entered it the following year at sixteen. Her studies were interrupted by the outbreak of the [[holy-evrian-civil-war]] in May 1918.

## Ymirian Advisory Mission

During the civil war, Ymiria and [[arlbern]] negotiated separate peace terms with the Entente through the Provisional Agreements of Zurmark. As part of Ymiria's settlement with Nichirin, her father arranged her dispatch to Nichirin under an agreement with Shogun [[masanori-miyamoto]] during the conflict, which dissolved the Holy Evrian Empire into its successor states over the following six months. The agreement established the Ymirian Advisory Mission, under which Ymiria seconded colonial administrators to Nichirin's colonial governments, and von Bismarck, then seventeen, was attached to it as a ward. Her presence was regarded by both governments as a guarantee of Ymiria's commitments.

Von Bismarck sailed for Nichirin in August 1918, in an Entente convoy that the Nichirian Navy under Marshal-Admiral [[suiren-makinami]] escorted through waters patrolled by [[nordmark|Nordmarken]] submarines. She arrived at Takarazuka in October. The crossing left her with chronic seasickness, and she has traveled by rail or air whenever possible since.

In 1919, von Bismarck enrolled at [[takarazuka-imperial-university]] to complete her studies in colonial administration. In 1920, her father asked her to return to Ymiria, but she remained in Nichirin and completed a Bachelor of Science in 1921, after which she joined the mission's staff as an attaché and entered the Ministry of Colonial Affairs through it. She completed a Master of Science in colonial policy at the same university in 1923. During that attachment, she produced an audit that the ministry had not requested, which found that none of the three aid pledges Shogun Masanori Miyamoto had made to Kiritani (now [[horai|Hōrai]]) between 1919 and 1923 had been entered into the colonial budget. The work drew sufficient attention that, when Minister Shosuke Kaga unexpectedly died, Shogun Masashi Miyamoto appointed her to succeed him on October 13, 1924, instead of promoting internally.

## Ministry of Colonial Affairs

As Minister of Colonial Affairs, von Bismarck administered Kiritani and [[kinshu|Shin-Nichirin]] (now Kinshū), whose combined population exceeds 150 million. In February 1930, she attended the First Conference of the Alemannian Nations at Akenau alongside Chief Liaison Officer [[akane-shikinami-hoffmann]] as a observer of the [[mizuhara-pentapartite]]. Her expertise in Alemannian political history informed the cabinet's assessment of the [[akenauer-bund]], the coordination framework founded at that conference between Ymiria, Arlbern, and Nordmark, which she described as a reunification vehicle for the former Holy Evrian successor states. She had proposed the Bund's name at the conference and designed its flag, three interlocking black, gold, and red rings on a dark blue field. On September 27, 1930, she attended the peace negotiations ending the [[second-genosan-war]], held in a railway carriage at Fehervaros, as a Pentapartite advisor as her father presented the Akenauer Bund's terms to the Genosan delegate.

### Operation Monsoon and the Bachstadt Settlement

In November 1930, von Bismarck represented Nichirin at talks in [[bachstadt]], [[divia]], with the Ministers of Foreign Relations Sven Ullrichs of Ymiria and Arsalan Ershadi of Varahmehr on the crisis in [[shivon]], where she set out the Shogunate's position that the regime of [[mendel-shneerson]] had forfeited its right to exist. The three states [[operation-monsoon|intervened]] in Shivon the following month.

On December 19, the day before Shivon's surrender, Admiral [[ayaka-kyougoku]] ordered a strike on the Hasmonean Temple, the holiest site of the Hasmonean faith. Kyougoku was recalled to Nichirin to account for the strike, and von Bismarck, who had left Bachstadt by ferry on November 23, was ordered back from the Bay of Serica on December 20 to lead the Nichirian delegation at the peace conference in Kyougoku's place. From the ferry, allegedly out of spite, she renamed both of Nichirin's overseas territories by telegram: Kiritani became Hōrai (蓬莱), after a legendary land in Hezhouan mythology, and Shin-Nichirin became Kinshū (金州, "Province of Gold"). In a statement printed by the Senpan Tribune, she said that "if I am going to spend my career administering territories from the ocean, the least I can do is give them names that don't sound like fishing villages."

At the conference in Bachstadt on January 12, 1931, von Bismarck delivered the Shogunate's formal apology for the destruction of the temple. The settlement, signed in Nichirin's name on January 14, fixed Shivon's reparations to the three intervening states and committed Nichirin to fund the temple's reconstruction in full over six fiscal years, with the rebuild directed by the Hasmonean community. The cabinet ratified it as signed on February 3.

## Aureate Co-Prosperity Sphere

<figure class="float float-right" style="width:300px"><img src="https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczMT1A6x1Dsje-U3KpLtB5FtfW_Zngs_Nkvonoq8hO2heKBa7brGNPgAShsg9DqpsI0FJroQceEL_dC78alGy40cPJTdgw29exbg-s449OyYPwjddm0gDuLMaef29k1RTMV7OJcxY4o62H5cRr8Qjmsa=w2000" alt="Von Bismarck announcing establishment of the Aureate Co-Prosperity Sphere in Shinkeisei, February 1931."><figcaption>Von Bismarck announcing establishment of the Aureate Co-Prosperity Sphere in Shinkeisei, February 1931.</figcaption></figure>

On February 23, 1931, the Ministry of Colonial Affairs was abolished and replaced by the Aureate Co-Prosperity Sphere (Kōrin), a self-governance framework that von Bismarck designed and currently heads as High Commissioner. Under the Kōrin, Hōrai and Kinshū became constituent states with full domestic self-governance, while Shinkeisei retained foreign policy, military basing rights, and the Shogun as head of state. The same decree formalized two new constituent states negotiated with native populations: [[mizuho]], on the Western Panthalassan coast of [[elysia]], through a protection agreement with Tamanqui communities, and [[ryugu|Ryūgu]], in eastern [Aphirica](https://auroruse.github.io/the-talopedia/pelagonia/#geography), through negotiations with Sennari tribal councils after a ministry-brokered ceasefire ended an eighteen-month inter-clan conflict. The decree was accompanied by an aid package for Hōrai and Ryūgū, whose allocations were published in full before the framework took effect.

Von Bismarck represented Nichirin at the Fifth Pentapartite Conference in Veynach on March 1, 1931. [[elvester|Elvesterian]] foreign minister Lord Oliver Parkside argued that the Kōrin set a precedent that would proliferate demands for self-governance across colonial empires worldwide. Von Bismarck replied that the Henekuro Revolt had demonstrated the fragility of the existing model, and that if the visibility of an alternative could destabilize Elvester's colonies, the instability was already present. Parkside withdrew from the session, followed by the [[arverne|Arvernois]] and [[ponurvia|Ponurvian]] delegations. By contrast, the Akenauer Bund endorsed the Kōrin and announced similar measures for its own holdings. In June, Elvester dissolved four of its overseas governorates, including the [[golden-coast]], which had been in revolt before the Veynach conference.

At the Second Conference of the Alemannian Nations at Akenau on April 5, 1931, convened after the Pentapartite's collapse, von Bismarck proposed the name under which Ymiria, Arlbern, and Nordmark unified as the Alemannian Confederation that day. While the Mizuhara Pentapartite was never formally dissolved, the alliance's provisions currently continue only as a bilateral agreement between Nichirin and Alemannia.

In June 1934, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs referred the Senpan Tribune's request for the government's position on Rudania's new leadership to von Bismarck, who compared the change to a snake shedding its skin and said, "Ask me again when they have done it twice." Following the retirement of Minister of Foreign Affairs Nariyoshi Miyagi later that month, she has overseen the ministry on a *de facto* basis while remaining High Commissioner.

## Personal Life

Von Bismarck remains close to her father, with whom she has corresponded weekly since her departure for Nichirin in 1918. She did not visit Hōrai or Kinshū until air routes reached them, and has traveled to the territories by air since; the High Commission's transport aircraft is named Adelheid, after her paternal grandmother, whose jeweler in Akenau crafted the garnet and silver filigree earrings she wears.

She has worn the same lipstick, Kirschrot, made by a small cosmetics house in [[nurbrecht|Nürbrecht]], since the age of sixteen, and imports it by diplomatic pouch. Each December, she hosts a reception for Shinkeisei's diplomatic corps, supplied with Printen from the Krämerstraße bakery in Akenau that she has patronized since childhood, at which each guest receives a pair of lined gloves from a glove maker on Büchel. In 1928, she funded a structural survey of the ninth-century crypt of Akenau Cathedral.
